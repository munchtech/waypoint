# Corvane in the Realms — DM Canon, Revision 7

*Revision 7 keeps everything in Revision 6 and adds Section 9 (the table, the timeline fixes and the endings). Revision 6 added three things to The Seventh Bell v5: Corvane is part of the Forgotten Realms, Pellmoor is a port that newcomers arrive in, and memory is a working currency alongside gold. Where this sheet and v5 disagree, this sheet wins. The v5 Canon Sheet (Part 3.3) still governs every date and family line it lists.*

*Rules: Dungeons & Dragons 2024 (Player's Handbook 2024, Dungeon Master's Guide 2024, Monster Manual 2024). Stat blocks in the playbook are built on 2024 Monster Manual creatures.*

## 1. Where Corvane is

Corvane is a continent called **Hesk**, far out in the Trackless Sea beyond the last Sword Coast charts. A permanent band of storm circles it at sea, thirty to sixty miles deep. Faerûnian sailors call it **the Stormwall**. Corvanese who know of it call it **Thrane's Hem**.

For four centuries no ship crossed it. Sword Coast sailors kept a legend of "the Ringing Land" west of the world, from old chanties nobody could date. Candlekeep holds one water-stained portolan labelled *the Belled Coast*. Inside Corvane, most people believed there was nothing beyond the Ninefold Shore.

**DM truth: what the Stormwall is.** When Thrane fell silent on the Quiet Night, its storms did not end. They lost their shepherd and were pushed outward to the edge of the bells' resonance, where Ysh's lullaby stops. They have circled there ever since. That is why no storm has crossed Brassfall in four hundred years: every storm Corvane should have had is in the Wall. Nobody in Corvane or Faerûn knows this. Corin Aldeth suspects it. Edda Thorne's Notes imply it in one line ("Thrane's weather will go to the rim and wait").

**Before the Silence.** A few Faerûnian ships reached Corvane before the Quiet Night. Some of the Stilled in Glassreach are those sailors, frozen in 2 SS in Sword Coast clothes four centuries out of fashion (v5 Part 5.1 already says some Stilled were "travelers from elsewhere"). A Faerûnian character may recognise an old Tethyrian ship's badge on one of them.

## 2. The Wrongweather and the Seam (382 SS / 1462 DR)

In 382 SS the Ironhorn raided Brassfall and broke Old Garrow's sealed shafts (v5 timeline). The dead lightning that poured out called to the storms at the rim. For one night a storm tore inland through the Wall, up the river Pell, and onto the Mute Mere: the first weather the Mere had seen in 380 years. The ferry-folk call it **the Wrongweather**.

- **Davin Sallow drowned in the Wrongweather.** This resolves v5's "drowned in the Mere in a storm 30 years ago." Mother Sallow sold her own memory of their wedding day to the Tithe that same winter, in grief, and has been losing him ever since (v5 Part 5.2).
- When the storm passed it left a calm channel through the Wall, in line with the river's mouth: **the Seam**. It is a mile or two wide, it drifts with the seasons, and only pilots who know its moods take ships through.
- At dawn after the Wrongweather, a battered Baldur's Gate cog, the **Gull's Wager**, drifted up the Pell into the Mere. Its crew had been chasing the Ringing Land legend. They were the first Faerûnians in Corvane in four centuries.
- **Ilse Vesk** seized the Quiet Court that year (v5 timeline). The chaos that followed now includes the Wrongweather, a hole in the world, and strangers on the Mere.

**Calendar.** 412 SS = 1492 DR. The Wrongweather was 382 SS (1462 DR). Faerûnian sailors date by Dalereckoning; Corvanese date "since the Silence." Both are in use at Pellmoor.

## 3. Thirty years of contact

**The Landing Charter (384 SS).** Ilse Vesk declared Pellmoor the *only lawful landing* in Corvane. Foreign ships may anchor only on the Mute Mere at Pellmoor. Foreigners may trade freely there, but travelling inland past the Mere road needs a Court **writ of passage**, a grey paper sealed in grey wax, issued from Hallowmere. The Charter quarantines foreigners from the capital, keeps the Court's own Ninefold ports closed, and lets the Court tax every cargo. Maudrin Vesk keeps it in force.

**Why Pellmoor.** The Seam opens at the Pellmouth. The river Pell runs about forty miles inland from the sea into the Mute Mere. The Mere is unnaturally still (no wind, no current: Ossa's silence), which makes it the only safe water after a Stormwall crossing. Ships limp in to mend. The drowned rooftops and steeples of Old Pellmoor lie just under the surface, so every ship needs a ferry-folk pilot. The ferry-folk, who had nothing, became indispensable.

**Traffic.** The sailing season runs from early spring (Ches) to early autumn (Eleint). Thirty years ago it was one ship a year. Now it is forty to sixty ships a season, and the quays are never empty in summer.

**What comes in:** gold and silver coin, steel, paper, books, spices, wine, glass lenses, Lantanese clockwork. Clocks sell at once in a land without bells, but the Court's Landing House removes every chime before a clock leaves the quay. Silent clocks are a Pellmoor joke and a Pellmoor fashion.

**What goes out:** bottled memories (the most valuable export by far; nobles in Amn and Calimshan pay fortunes to witness a Corvanese summer), glass-flowers from Glassreach, memory-pearls from the Mere, reed-dye, bone-bound books, Ironhorn horses. Storm-stones and bronze are banned exports, and smuggled anyway.

**Who is here from Faerûn.**
- **The Vennmark Coster** of Athkatla (Amn). The largest memory exporter. Run in Pellmoor by Master Orsolo Venn.
- **The Corvane Venture**, a Waterdhavian guild partnership. Cautious, respectable, mostly cloth and steel.
- **Baldurian factors**, who still toast the *Gull's Wager* every Wrongweather night.
- **Lantanese gnome tinkers**, who are fascinated by the bells and keep being told to be quieter.
- **Dwarves from Mirabar**, who want Brassfall bronze and cannot legally buy it.
- **The Harpers** keep two quiet agents in Pellmoor. They distrust the Tithe and sympathise with the Bellwrights. Optional ally.
- **The Zhentarim** run memory smuggling out of Strangers' Row. Optional complication.

**Faiths.** Faerûnian divine magic works in Corvane. Lathander's clergy run the **Dawn Mission** on Strangers' Row; the Court forbids dawn-worship in Hallowmere, a city with no dawn. Kelemvor's faithful are horrified that the dead of the Mere cannot rest, and some want to lay the Drowned Choir to rest by force (a complication, see Act I). Chauntea's faithful are drawn to the ash fields of Kell's Crossing. The Ninefold Church calls the foreign faiths "the loud gods."

**How each side sees the other.** Corvanese call Faerûnians **Stormborn** (polite) or **Loudfolk** (rude). Faerûnians call the Corvanese **Bellfolk** or **the Quiet**. Faerûnians who have settled in Pellmoor for years are **Landers**. Corvanese are mostly human, with halfling ferry-families, dwarven mining clans in Brassfall, and very few elves. Dragonborn, tieflings, orcs, goliaths, gnomes and aasimar arrived with the ships; Corvanese stare, out of curiosity more than hostility. Some whisper that aasimar are bell-touched. Hushmen distrust anything new.

**Language.** Corvanese speak **Hesk**. Thirty years of trade have made Common the second tongue of Pellmoor, and many ferry-folk speak both. Inland, Hesk is all you will hear. Corvanese characters may take Hesk as one of their standard languages; Faerûnians who have lived in Pellmoor a year or more may too.

**Magic.** Spells work by the 2024 rules. In Corvane, every spell hums faintly as it is cast: that hum is what Corvanese call Tonework, and Faerûnian wizards notice it within a day. Silence-stones suppress spells under the v5 house rule (Part 2.6). Loud magic (Thunderwave, Shatter) is legal at Pellmoor and treated as "bell-work" by Hushmen inland.

## 4. Pellmoor Landing

> "The Mute Mere is so still that the ships look painted on it. Forty masts stand over the water where a drowned town lies just beneath, rooftops like the backs of sleeping animals. On the quays, a dozen languages argue over the price of a memory. Gnomes shout. Gulls do not; there are no gulls on the Mere. And at the far end of the waterfront, past the last warehouse, the old reed-roofed village of Pellmoor still leans toward the water as if none of this were happening."

About four thousand people live here year-round; nearly twice that crowd the waterfront in sailing season. Pellmoor is not a Court city: the Tithe Clock (v5 Part 2.12) does not tick here. The Court is present through the Landing House and its Hushmen.

**Districts.**
1. **The Old Shore.** The original ferry village. Reed-thatched houses, the ferry-dock where Ila waits, the net-racks, the chapel of Brother Eld, Mother Sallow's reed-hut, the Harrow house, the ledger-house, the Inn of the Shuttered Bell, and the Mere Memory Shore. The ferry-folk keep it theirs. Foreigners are tolerated, not welcomed. Children still do not play near the water.
2. **The Quays.** New deep-water piers driven into Old Pellmoor's drowned roofs. Warehouses, chandlers, a dry-dock. The **Pilots' Hall**, where ferry-folk pilots are hired to take every ship across the drowned town. The **Landing House**: Court customs, writs of passage, the landing due, and a post of six Hushmen.
3. **The Tally.** The market square where both currencies trade. The **Reckoning House** posts the day's rate on a slate by the door: *1 KEEP = 1 GP 2 SP. MOTES AT 1 SP. HEARTKEEPS BY APPRAISAL.* Reckoners in grey aprons carry tuning forks.
4. **Strangers' Row.** The Faerûnian quarter; the ferry-folk call it **the Loud Quarter**. Foreign inns, the Vennmark Coster's counting-house, the Corvane Venture hall, the Dawn Mission, a dwarven smithy, a gnomish clockmaker with silent clocks, a Kelemvorite shrine-tent, and music at all hours.

**Key places, short form.**
- **The Inn of the Shuttered Bell** (Old Shore): Dorra Pell remembers every face, and now every ship that brought it.
- **The Brass Lantern** (Strangers' Row): the Faerûnian inn. Loud, warm, overpriced, and the only place in Corvane that serves Waterdhavian ale.
- **The Landing House** (Quays): Tobias Quill's office.
- **The Reckoning House** (the Tally): Reckoner Sela Maunt.
- **The Pilots' Hall** (Quays): Pilot-Mistress Brannagh Wick.

### New NPCs

**Pilot-Mistress Brannagh Wick** (ferry-folk, about 60; 2024 stand-in: Scout).
- *Role:* runs the Pilots' Hall. Every ship that crosses the drowned town does it with one of her pilots. Tam Harrow was one of them.
- *Look:* short, wind-cracked hands, a coil of sounding-line always over one shoulder.
- *Voice:* gruff, counts tides out loud, calls every ship "she" and every captain "lad."
- *Wants:* the Hall to stay independent of the Court and the trading houses. Ila safe.
- *Secret:* Tam took his boat out the night he died with no cargo and no ship to pilot, which no pilot does. She told the Landing House nothing. She thinks he was going to the Ossa Belfry, and she is right.
- *Use:* a fourth source for the Tam mystery (v5 Part 3.1). Any two sources establish the Court's responsibility. She can also delay any ship from leaving the Mere: no pilot, no departure.

**Reckoner Sela Maunt** (Corvanese, 40s; Commoner, or Noble for talk).
- *Role:* Court-licensed memory assayer at the Reckoning House. The person who explains memory money to newcomers.
- *Look:* grey apron, three tuning forks in a breast pocket, reading lenses bought from a Lantanese tinker.
- *Voice:* precise, kind, slightly tired; taps a fork against the counter when she thinks.
- *Wants:* honest weights and a quiet life.
- *Secret:* she keeps a "Lost Shelf" of heartkeeps sold in grief and quietly lets the sellers buy them back at cost, which the Court forbids. If the party is kind to her, she will help them read a keep's weight or tell a forged keep from a true one.

**Master Orsolo Venn** of the Vennmark Coster (Amnian human, 50s; Noble, with two Toughs as guards).
- *Role:* the largest memory exporter in Pellmoor. Charming, generous, and entirely sincere that he is helping people.
- *Look:* plum velvet in a grey country, rings on every finger, a silent Lantanese pocket-clock he consults constantly.
- *Voice:* warm, fluent Hesk with an Amnian lilt; calls everyone "friend" and means "customer."
- *Wants:* an exclusive export licence from the Court, and curiosities from the Ringing Land for his buyers in Athkatla.
- *Secret:* he already pays Tobias Quill to look away from certain cargoes. He has a buyer in Athkatla offering 5,000 gp for "a true bell-relic of the Ringing Land." The Coster also buys "surplus" Tithed memories from the Court for export, and its ledgers prove it (see Act II).
- *Use:* a buyer for the humming Heart (Act I), and a recurring, human-sized antagonist of the memory trade.

**Sister Aurelie Dawnmantle** (Waterdhavian human, 30s; Priest).
- *Role:* Morninglord priestess who runs the Dawn Mission. Optional ally for clerics, paladins and anyone hurt.
- *Voice:* bright, earnest, says "when the sun comes back" as if it is a date in a diary.
- *Wants:* a writ of passage to Hallowmere, to bring Lathander's dawn to a city that has none.
- *Secret:* the Court has refused her writ four times. She is close to trying without one, which would get her arrested.
- *Use:* if the party tells her about Vaer's bell, she becomes a devoted ally, and a liability, in Act II.

## 5. Memory as money

Corvane traded memories long before the ships came. The Court's Tithe made it a habit and the Reckoners made it a currency. Gold arrived thirty years ago and is winning, slowly.

### How it works in the world
1. **Drawing.** A memory can be drawn out of a person only with their consent, by a **Reckoner** with a tuning fork and a small stoppered vial of Mere-glass. It takes about a minute. It feels like forgetting a word on the tip of your tongue, and then not minding. Drawing without a Court licence is *leeching*, a crime.
2. **Keeps.** A drawn memory, sealed, is a **keep**: a vial of faintly luminous mist that hums one low note when held. Reckoners grade a keep by its **weight**, how much feeling it holds, and stamp the wax. The content stays private unless someone opens it.
3. **Keeps circulate sealed.** People pass stamped keeps hand to hand like banknotes, unopened. A keep's value rests on two facts: someone will always pay to witness it, and its owner can always redeem it.
4. **Opening.** If the original owner opens a keep, the memory returns to them whole (v5 Part 2.6). If anyone else opens it, they *witness* it, vividly, as a guest in someone else's day, without acquiring the owner's identity. Each stranger's witnessing fades it; after three it is a grey, spent vial.
5. **What cannot be drawn.** Skills, spells, languages, proficiencies and anything a character can do. "You can sell the day you learned to swim, but never the swimming." Nobody can sell someone else's memory.
6. **Blanks** are people who sold or Tithed so many heavy memories that they no longer recognise their own families.

### Denominations (stamped weights)

| Weight | What it holds | Worth at Pellmoor | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mote** | A passing memory: last Ossday's supper, a stranger's coat, the second verse of a song | about 1 sp | Grey wax. Small change. |
| **Keep** | A memory with feeling: a good afternoon, a small win, a lesson learned the hard way | about 1 gp (floats daily; the slate reads 1 gp 2 sp when the campaign opens) | Plain wax. The everyday coin. |
| **Heartkeep** | A defining memory: a parent's face, a wedding day, the moment you chose your road | 25 to 100 gp or more, by appraisal | Red wax. Rarely spent, often sold in desperation. The Tithe prefers them. |
| **Knowing** | A memory that carries knowledge: a route, a recipe, a password, a face | priced by usefulness, often more than its feeling | Blue wax. The seller loses the knowledge. Tam's route memory was taken this way. |

### Gold, and why the Court hates it
Corvane had little coin of its own. Bronze is bell-metal and the Court has restricted its casting since bell-making was banned (112 SS), so old Corvanese money was iron "ticks" and Court scrip. Faerûnian gold cannot be Tithed, cannot fade, and cannot be Reckoned. At Pellmoor nearly every stall takes either. Inland, gold is accepted for Faerûnian goods and frowned on for everything else.

**The rule that matters.** Court taxes on Corvanese subjects must be paid in memory. Foreigners pay the **landing due** in gold. Locals can earn gold, but still owe the Court memories. Paying a local's Tithe in gold, called **gilding**, is illegal and common.

**Exchange.** Posted daily at the Reckoning House. Roughly 1 keep = 1 gp at Pellmoor, where Faerûnian demand keeps memory dear. In Hallowmere a keep fetches about 8 sp. The Hallowmere gate's "courtesy memory" (v5 Session 4) is a registration, not a fee, and the clerk will not take gold for it.

**Why Quill is short.** Pellmoor pays its way in gold now. Its memory Tithe has fallen for years, and Tobias Quill's quota (v5 Session 1, H-03) cannot be met honestly.

### Table rules (2024)
- **Paying in memory.** A character may pay anywhere coin is accepted in Corvane, at the posted rate. The player chooses the memory and writes one line about it in the Memory Ledger. The DM never chooses it.
- **Selling memory for gold.** A Reckoner buys at the posted rate less a 10 percent fee.
- **Motes and keeps** have no mechanical effect. The character simply no longer has that recollection.
- **Heartkeeps.** When a character sells one, the player rewrites one thing about the character that the memory held up: a belief, a bond, a fear. No mechanical penalty. It never removes player knowledge or control of the character.
- **Generosity.** The DM may award **Heroic Inspiration** when a character gives a memory freely to someone who needs it. (This matches v5's Kell's Crossing Gift rule.)
- **Witnessing a Knowing.** Once, a character who witnesses a Knowing keep has advantage on one ability check that knowledge would plainly help (a pilot's route memory helps navigate the Mere).
- **Keep the kinds apart.** A **Toll** pays for a bell and can never be bought back. A **Tithe** goes to the Court and can be redeemed if you find your jar. A **trade** is a keep. **Mere Memories** are borrowed impressions. **Eel bites** return after a long rest. **Stairs** losses follow the stairs' own rule. Record each kind separately in the ledger.

## 6. What changes in the campaign

**Act I**
- **Session 1 opening** is rewritten for the port (the new read-aloud is in the playbook). The party may arrive by ship through the Seam, by the Mere road, or already live here. Ila waits on the *old* ferry-dock at the Old Shore, apart from the noise of the Quays.
- **Quill** is the Landing House's Tithe officer, short on quota because Pellmoor pays in gold.
- **H-03, the Tithe Quota:** the three villagers want to pay in gold, which is illegal for locals. Gilding is a new way out.
- **New Session 3 option: sell the Heart to Master Venn.** He pays 600 gp up front (more if the party haggles) and loads it on the Coster ship *Gilded Heron*, sailing in three days. A pilot is required to leave the Mere, so Brannagh Wick can delay the ship by a day; the Seam crossing takes another day. If the Heart leaves Corvane, Ossa cannot be rung until it is recovered. That is a hard, earned consequence. The DM's safety valve, if the table wants it: the *Gilded Heron* wrecks in the Stormwall and the Heart washes back into the Mere weeks later ("the Mere remembers what is taken from it").
- **New complication:** a Kelemvorite zealot, **Brother Hadrik Voss** (Priest), wants to lay the Drowned Choir to rest by force, which would anger the Choir and endanger Pellmoor. Use him if the table likes conflict among allies.

**Act II**
- **The Hallowmere gate** checks writs of passage. Faerûnians without one are turned back or arrested unless the Court invitation (Session 3's letter) is accepted as a writ, which it is.
- **The Tithe-Ledger** has an **EXPORT — VENNMARK** column: the Court sells "surplus" Tithed memories to the Coster, and they are shipped to Faerûn. Blanks' memories may be in Athkatla. Optional reveal; it gives the party a reason to return to Pellmoor and confront Venn.

**Act III**
- Some **Stilled** are Faerûnian sailors from before the Quiet Night (see Section 1). A woken one speaks an old dialect of Common and asks what year it is.

**Act IV**
- **Ringing Thrane loosens the Stormwall.** The storms come home to Brassfall. For a season the Seam widens into a broad road, and three new fleets arrive within a month. The Court's control of the Landing collapses. This is one more reason the Court sends a strike force after Thrane rings.
- **New ally for Session 22**, if earned in Pellmoor: the **Stormborn** (Harper agents, Sister Aurelie's mission, or a Baldurian crew who owe the party).

**Act V and endings**
- If Thrane is rung and the ending stabilises it (Concord, or the Second Dawn), the Stormwall thins to ordinary sea weather over a few years. Corvane joins the world.
- In the Empty Choir, the Court keeps the Seam, and the memory trade grows.

**Part 9 changes**
- **Situation 17** ("leave Corvane or find another world"): ships sail home through the Seam. The voyage to the Sword Coast takes about six weeks. Leaving pauses the plot: advance every faction clock by two for each month away. The party may bring Faerûnian help back; it arrives as an Act IV ally option.
- **Situation 18** ("fight a dragon"): there are no dragons in Corvane. Sailors swear one tried the Stormwall in 1479 DR and fell burning into the sea.
- **The Edge of the Dream (11.7)** moves into the heart of the Stormwall. Pilots who drift off the Seam describe a warm grey light and a shore that hums.

## 8. 2024 rules notes used across the playbook

- **Heroic Inspiration** replaces v5's "Inspiration."
- **Influence** (2024 action): social contingencies use the Influence action. The DC is 15 or the creature's Intelligence score, whichever is higher; the target's attitude (Friendly, Indifferent, Hostile) decides whether a check is even needed.
- **Study** and **Search** actions: clue scenes use Study (Intelligence) and Search (Wisdom) where v5 said "check." Essential clues are never lost to a failed roll (v5 rule, kept).
- **Extreme Cold** (Dungeon Master's Guide 2024) replaces v5's Glassreach "1 cold damage per hour": at the end of each hour in Glassreach, a creature makes a DC 10 Constitution save or gains 1 Exhaustion level. Creatures with Resistance or Immunity to Cold damage succeed automatically (2024 rule). *House rule kept from v5:* a warm cloak or other cold-weather gear also means automatic success, so Yara's cloaks matter.
- **Exhaustion** (2024): each level gives −2 to d20 Tests and −5 feet of Speed; a long rest removes 1 level.
- **Holding breath** (2024): a creature can hold its breath for 1 + its Constitution modifier minutes (minimum 30 seconds). Out of breath, it gains 1 Exhaustion level at the end of each of its turns, and removes those levels when it can breathe again. The Tidewarden's air pocket and the Mere dive in Act I use this.
- **Conditions** follow the 2024 definitions (Frightened, Charmed, Incapacitated, Blinded, Deafened, and so on). A Toll of Sense uses Blinded or Deafened as v5 already says.
- **Encounter difficulty** uses the 2024 XP budget per character (Low, Moderate, High). Waypoint calculates it from your party roster; the counts in v5 Appendix A are not balanced for 2024.

## 9. Revision 7 additions

These deepen Revision 6; none of them changes it.

**Hearts, not Tongues.** From Revision 7 the bells' clappers are called **Hearts** at the table and in every document: Ossa's Heart, the Frost Heart, the Heart-Hound, Kessa's Heart-Hall. It is the same object with an easier word. In-world, older Court records still say "tongue"; the *Tongues* spell is unaffected.

**Session-zero safety words.** **Off-limits** (never in the game) and **Off-screen** (can happen, but not described) replace off-limits and off-screen topics.

**The table.** Four players, starting at level 3 with subclasses chosen. Sessions 1 to 5 run at level 3; after that the v5 milestones apply (level 4 after the city investigation, up to 11 for Act V). Every encounter in the playbook is sized for four characters at those levels with the 2024 XP budget.

**Timeline fixes.**
- Glassreach froze in one night, in 2 SS. Every Stilled person has been frozen about 410 years. Only Imber Vesk, the Glass Sexton, is different: his own winter-moment came in 32 SS, so he has been frozen 380 years.
- Wyn Talla is Maren Talla's descendant, not her sister. The family kept Maren's name and her lullaby for four centuries.

**Clarifications.**
- **Dawn in Hallowmere.** The Dawn Order is Hallowmere's own knighthood; its oaths to Vaer are old tradition and the Court tolerates them. What the Court forbids in Hallowmere is foreign dawn-worship, which is why Sister Aurelie cannot get a writ.
- **The Tithe.** It is the Court's tax, paid in memories; the Court says it protects people in return (v5's "protection and tax relief"). A Tithed memory sits in a jar in the Court's vaults and can be redeemed by its owner. Only characters raised under the Court have childhood Tithe jars; a Faerûnian who arrived this season has only the courtesy memory taken at the Hallowmere gate.
- **The Silence Warrant** from ringing Vaer is enforced by Hushmen in every Court city, and by the Dawn Order too unless Rook won the oath-duel.

**The endings, as Waypoint tracks them.** Every ending needs someone at the Hush Shrine, at the foot of the Stairs of the Hush. None is open when the campaign starts.
- **The Concord:** all six regional bells rung, Ysh unstruck, the party holding the Key, a willing Ringer. Reached when the Concord tuning is complete.
- **The Second Dawn:** the party seals the unstruck Key in the shrine's recess. With no bell rung it is a stalemate, not a dawn, and the DM says so before the table chooses it.
- **The Hush Unbound:** someone strikes Ysh with the Key. If Maudrin strikes, she pays the six Tolls, and the players still choose Remake or Unmake.
- **The Empty Choir:** the Court holds the unstruck Key, and the players choose to stop resisting. A lost fight never chooses it.

**Motives, made firm (DM truth).**
- **Why Maudrin kept the Key and never sealed it.** The Court's silence-stones and the Hushmen's gloves are tuned to the Key's hum. A sealed Key would go quiet and the stones would die with it, and the Court's power with them. So for 38 years she kept it close to the Seventh's tune and away from any recess. Each bell the party rings shifts the world's key, so the Key's hum from the palace stops holding the lullaby steady: that is why she must carry it to Vael Tor in Act IV. She wants the unrung Hearts (a rung Heart is worthless to her), the Key, and the Ringer, whom she would Tithe so nobody can pay the Great Toll or strike. She will seal the stairs, never the Key.
- **Corin Aldeth wants the Concord.** Edda Thorne's Notes describe six rung bells holding the lullaby while the Key is seated, never struck. The price they name is the Ringer's "whole name". His family read that as a title for three generations; Corin now believes it means the Ringer's own name, and says so. He can offer to be the Seventh's voice himself.
- **Haldric and Isolde are seeded early.** Haldric signs private letters "H.V." and Maudrin calls him "my dear"; the Tithe-Ledger holds an Ironhorn entry in Isolde's hand. Both pay off in Act V.

**The Stairs of the Hush.** The first nineteen steps are free. Every full twenty steps costs one memory. The first time a character reaches step 20 they make one DC 13 Wisdom save (once per campaign): on a failure their total is capped at five memories, on a success at two. The dead pay nothing, children are never asked, and an absent player's character never pays.

**A lost fight is never an ending.** If the Court takes the Key or a Heart in a fight, the party can always take it back: a convoy, a vault, a bargain. Only the players' own choice ends their resistance.

**Maudrin at the end.** Holding the Key at the summit or the shrine, Maudrin uses the Keeper of the Seventh stat block (CR 12, built on the 2024 Archmage, with legendary actions). Before then she is the CR 8 Chancellor.

**After the endings.** The Stormwall thins for good only after the Concord or a Second Dawn that rang Thrane. After the Hush Unbound, the Wall follows Oum's choice: Remake keeps a gentler Wall with a wide Seam; Unmake leaves open sea. After the Empty Choir the Wall stays as Thrane's ringing left it, and the Court keeps the Seam.
