*Session zero is the evening before Session 1. Nobody fights and nothing is at stake: you agree what game you're playing, make four characters who belong in Pellmoor, and put everything into Waypoint. Allow 2 to 3 hours. Read this once beforehand; at the table, read the boxed text and work down the steps.*

### Before you start

- **Bring:** the player site (or the four handouts printed: *What's It About?*, *The Seventh Bell*, *The Ringing Land*, *The Map of Corvane*), the 2024 Player's Handbook (or D&D Beyond), index cards and pens, and Waypoint open on **Party & preferences**.
- **Rules:** D&D 2024. Characters start at **level 3** with their subclass chosen. Use the standard array or point buy; skip rolled stats so nobody starts far ahead.
- **Your goals tonight:** everyone knows the tone and the safety words; four level-3 characters exist, each with a reason to be on the old ferry-dock; and you have each character's "one thing I lost" written down. Session 1 uses all of it.

### Step 1: The pitch (10 minutes)

> **SAY:** "Welcome to Corvane. This is a campaign about bells, memory, and the cost of getting back something you lost. Four hundred years ago every bell in this land fell silent in a single night. People learned to live without them, and they pay their taxes in memories. Thirty years ago ships from the Sword Coast found a way through the storm around Corvane, and now a busy port sits on a drowned lake. Something bronze is humming under that lake, and a fourteen-year-old girl can hear it. You're the people who find out why."

Then show the player site's front page or *What's It About?* and let them read for five minutes. Answer questions honestly; say "you'll find out" for anything that's a secret.

### Step 2: Safety and boundaries (15 minutes)

> **SAY:** "Before we make characters, I want to know what kind of game you want and what you don't want in it. I'm going to use two simple tools. An **Off-limits** topic is something we never include. An **Off-screen** topic is something we can include, but we skip past it without describing it. Any of you can call one out at any time, and we'll change course without asking why."

- **Name what's in this campaign, plainly:** drowning; children in danger (a fourteen-year-old is central, and the book keeps her safe on-screen); memories taken by force; families split up; the possible end of the world.
- **Hand out an index card each.** Ask them to write anything Off-limits or Off-screen, anonymously, and fold it. Read them later, privately.
- **Agree three table rules out loud:** anyone can pause the game without explaining; what happens when two characters disagree (talk it out in character, never take control of another player's character); what happens when someone can't come (their character is quietly busy, never harmed, never pays a Toll).
- **Ask:** "Mostly story, mostly fighting, or a mix?" Set the four sliders in **Party & preferences → Your party's compass** to match (Roleplay, Exploration, Combat, Mischief). Foresight ranks plays by them.

**In Waypoint:** type the Off-limits and Off-screen topics into **Party & preferences → Off-limits, off-screen, and things to avoid**. Write the topics, never who asked for them.

### Step 3: Making characters (60 to 90 minutes)

Make characters together, at the table, so they can connect as they go.

**Where they come from.** Each player picks one:
- **Corvanese:** born in Corvane, raised under the Quiet Court. They have paid the Tithe all their life, and some childhood memory sits in a Court jar in Hallowmere. Human or halfling is most common; dwarves come from Brassfall. They speak Hesk.
- **Stormborn:** from the Sword Coast, arrived through the Seam this season or years ago. Any species. Corvanese stare, curious more than hostile. They need a Court writ to travel inland (the story provides one by Session 3). Anyone who has lived in Pellmoor a year can take Hesk as a language.
- **Lander:** a Faerûnian who settled in Pellmoor long ago. Knows the port, the pilots and the price of a memory.

**Spells and Corvane.** Every spell hums faintly as it's cast; Corvanese call that Tonework. Faerûnian magic works normally. Clerics of Lathander and Kelemvor have shrines on Strangers' Row.

**Three questions for every character.** Ask each player these, one at a time, and write the answers on an index card:
1. **"How did you arrive at Pellmoor?"** Off a ship through the Seam this morning, down the Mere road, or from a house in Pellmoor you've lived in for years.
2. **"What is one thing your character has lost?"** A person, a place, a memory, a skill they used to have. This is the most important answer of the night: the Cantor asks about it in Session 2, and when a bell takes its Toll, this is where you look first.
3. **"Who in Pellmoor do you already know?"** Offer: Dorra Pell at the Inn of the Shuttered Bell, Brannagh Wick at the Pilots' Hall, Reckoner Sela Maunt, Mother Sallow, or a ship's captain of their own invention.

**Hooks.** If a player is stuck for a reason to be there, offer one of the book's starting hooks: **H-01** (Ila asks someone she trusts to take the humming Heart away from her), **H-02** (Mother Sallow is paying to find a missing ferry) or **H-03** (Hushman Quill wants help filling his memory quota). Don't explain what's behind them.

**Tie the party together.** Once all four exist, go round the table: "Pick one other character. How do you know them?" Two links each is plenty. A party that already knows each other plays faster in Session 1.

### Step 4: How the table works (15 minutes)

Explain these in plain words, then stop.
- **Dice and turns** happen in Foundry. Waypoint is the DM's notebook; players never need it.
- **Heroic Inspiration:** "When you do something brave, kind or memorable, I'll give you Heroic Inspiration. Spend it to reroll any one die and keep the new roll. You can hold one at a time."
- **Memory is money:** in Pellmoor you can pay with gold or with memories. "You choose the memory and write one line about it. I never choose for you." The *Ringing Land* handout has the rates.
- **The Toll:** "Ringing a bell always costs the person who rings it something personal, and it lasts. I'll always warn you first: what kind of thing the bell takes and how heavy it will be. Never exactly what. You can always say no." Then ask each player privately: "If a bell ever took your sight or your hearing, would you want to play that?" Write the answer in their roster notes (World wiki → 2.7 The Toll explains why).
- **The player site:** "Everything you discover goes on our wiki after each session. It starts mostly locked."

### Step 5: Put it into Waypoint (15 minutes, or right after)

1. **Foresight → Party & ledger → Roster:** press **Add character** for each: character name, player name, level 3, and in Notes the three answers (how they arrived, what they lost, who they know).
2. **Party & preferences → The adventurers:** one line per character: name, species, class and subclass, and one thing that makes them tick.
3. **Party & preferences → Off-limits, off-screen, and things to avoid:** the topics from Step 2.
4. **Memory ledger:** nothing yet. The first entries come in Session 1.

### Step 6: Close (5 minutes)

> **SAY:** "Next time, you'll come to an old dock at the edge of a loud port, where a girl in a coat too big for her is staring at the water. Before we stop: tell me one thing you're looking forward to, and one thing you'd rather we didn't do much of."

Write both answers down. Then read the folded index cards from Step 2, privately, and add anything new to Waypoint.

### After session zero

- Send the general player-wiki entries (Corvane, Pellmoor Landing, Memory Money and the rest) if they're not on the site yet: **Player handouts → Player site → Send all**. The Session 1 entries wait until after Session 1.
- Read Session 1's **Running this session** once.
- If you want the first three sessions fitted to these four characters, give Claude the roster and the answers to the three questions. It can weave each character into Sessions 1 to 3 and run a practice session with you.
