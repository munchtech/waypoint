## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. At the table, read the boxed text aloud and glance at the bullets. The rules from Session 1's guide (Influence, Study, Search, Help, Hide, Grappled, Heroic Inspiration) still apply; tonight adds water, breath and cold.*

### Before you start (Session 2)

**Level:** four characters at level 3. No level-up tonight.

**First, check Ila.** Open Foresight and look at Ila Harrow. If the raid took her (Arrested), start tonight with [the Mere road cart](#if-ila-was-taken-the-mere-road-cart) at dawn, then pick up the rest. If she's free or hidden, start with the Night Song.

**What this session is about.** The dead of the Mute Mere sing every night, and the Old Shore sings back. Tonight the party learns why. Mother Sallow asks them to find the eastern-marsh ferry, missing a week. They find it grounded on a sandbank in the middle of the Mere, its ferryman dead and peaceful, with memory-eels circling. If they're brave, they dive to the drowned bell, where the Cantor of the Drowned Choir asks each of them one question: who are you grieving? This is the session about grief, water and honesty. It's the first time the danger is real.

**Your goals tonight:**
- The Night Song lands as eerie and sad, not just scary.
- The party finds the ferry and fights or avoids the eels.
- If they dive, the Cantor's question gets a real answer from at least one player.
- They pick up a second clue about Tam Harrow's death.
- End on: "Who was on the ferry?"

**Prepare:**
- **Tokens or minis:** the four characters; Mother Sallow; Ila; Brannagh Wick; a skiff (a long rectangle); the grounded ferry; the dead ferryman; six memory-eels; two Drowned Singers and two more eels for the belfry; the Cantor; the hooded Tidewarden. Optional: Brother Hadrik Voss and one extra Drowned Singer.
- **Maps:** sketch each place on paper, a battle mat or in Foundry. The Memory Shore: a curve of name-stones along the water, the Old Shore's lanterns behind. For the sandbank, a rough oval 30 feet across with the ferry on one end and deep water all round. For the belfry, draw a stone tower top in the middle of the page and write "40 ft down" beside it.
- **Breath cards:** on an index card per character, write how many rounds they can hold their breath: (1 + Constitution modifier) × 10 rounds, minimum 5. A round is six seconds; ten rounds is a minute. Tick it off during a dive.
- **The Mere Memory table** (d6, in the book's Part 11.4) for anyone who stays up all night.
- **The hymn** from Session 1: *Call:* "Who walks the water?" *Answer:* "We remember you."
- **Ask the players, privately or aloud, before the dive:** "Is there someone your character grieves?" A one-word answer is fine. It's what the Cantor will ask.

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **Holding your breath.** A character can hold their breath for 1 + Constitution modifier minutes (minimum 30 seconds). When they run out, they gain 1 level of Exhaustion at the end of each of their turns. As soon as they can breathe again, they lose all the Exhaustion they gained this way. At 6 levels of Exhaustion a character dies, so start warning loudly at 2.
- **Exhaustion.** Each level gives −2 to every d20 Test (attacks, checks, saves) and −5 feet of Speed.
- **Frigid Water.** The Mere is cold. A character can swim in it for a number of minutes equal to their Constitution score. After that, each extra minute they make a DC 10 Constitution save or gain 1 Exhaustion level. Short dives won't reach this; long ones will.
- **Swimming.** Without a Swim Speed, every foot of swimming costs an extra foot of movement. Diving 40 feet takes most characters two or three turns.
- **Fighting underwater.** Without a Swim Speed, melee attacks have Disadvantage unless they're made with a dagger, javelin, shortsword, spear or trident. Ranged weapon attacks have Disadvantage, and miss automatically beyond normal range (crossbows, nets and thrown javelins or daggers are the exception to the Disadvantage). Spells work normally.
- **Frightened** (a condition). Disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls while the source of the fear is in sight, and the character can't move closer to it willingly.
- **Grappled underwater.** Same as on land (Speed 0; action to escape), but the eels and Singers can drag a grappled character down with them.

### If Ila was taken: the Mere road cart

**Skip this unless Ila is Arrested.** At dawn, a covered Court cart leaves the Landing House for Hallowmere with Ila inside, a ferry-folk driver, a Hushman Sergeant and three Hushmen. Brannagh Wick sends a runner with its route and hour: the Mere road, second checkpoint, an hour past dawn.

> **SAY:** "The cart is grey canvas on a black frame. It rolls very slowly along the causeway, and its wheels make no sound at all. Through a gap in the canvas you see a ferry coat, much too big for the person wearing it."

- **Ila is never in danger on-screen.** She stays inside the closed cart, behind the driver, unharmed. Describe the canvas before anyone casts an area spell, and keep spells off the cart.
- **[Three plans that work:](play:s02-intercept-ila-cart)** a false checkpoint that turns the cart onto a drowned causeway (Charisma (Deception) DC 15, and a ferry-folk pilot plays along); an ambush at the ford; or Brannagh's pilots refusing the ford crossing so the cart has to wait while the party talks or sneaks.
- **If it comes to a fight:** see [the Mere road cart fight](#the-mere-road-cart-if-ila-was-taken). The Sergeant yields the cart rather than die.
- **How it ends:** Ila climbs out of the cart, furious and shaking, and says, "I counted the fence posts. Two hundred and twelve." Then the Night Song tonight, as below.

### Scene 1: The Night Song

**Where and why.** The Mere Memory Shore at dusk, the evening after Session 1. The whole Old Shore comes out for the song. The party comes because Mother Sallow told them to, because Ila asked them to, or because the singing pulls everyone outside.

> **SAY:** "The water is perfectly still. Fog lies on it like a sheet. From somewhere beneath the surface, a woman begins to sing. It is not loud. It is grief in a voice you almost recognise. Every word is a name. You hear your own name, then the name of someone you lost, then a name you don't know, and then yours again. Along the shore of name-stones, the ferry-folk stand in a long line, lanterns low, and hum back."

**What's really going on.** The singers are the Drowned Choir: the Mere's dead, who can't rest because their bell, Ossa, has been silent for four centuries. They call out to the living to be remembered. The response hymn is a promise that they are. The Choir doesn't hunt people who grieve or sing. It hunts "the forgetful." Mother Sallow is descended from the Choir's leader, the Cantor, and doesn't say so.

**Mother Sallow.** *Voice, mannerism:* as in Session 1 (dry, short, pipe between her teeth). *Wants:* the party to sing, and to say a dead person's name out loud. *First words:* "Lanterns down. They don't like being looked at." *Lines:* "Sing it back. That's all they want. Somebody to answer." · "You don't have to mean it the first time. You will by the third." · "There. Hear that? They've stopped. An hour, maybe."

**Ila.** She stands beside whoever was kind to her in Session 1 and sings very quietly, counting the lanterns between verses. *Line:* "Dad always sang the answer too loud. Everyone laughed."

**What they can learn here:**
- **Singing the response quiets the song for an hour.** Free. Anyone who sings it sincerely succeeds: a Charisma (Performance) roll only decides how good it sounds, never whether it works.
- **The Choir doesn't attack the grieving or the singing.** Free, from Mother Sallow.
- **Something about Tam's last night,** if a character stays on the shore all night (below).

- **[Staying up all night:](play:s02-stay-the-night)** anyone who stays on the shore until dawn makes a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, roll on the Mere Memory table. These are borrowed memories, not losses: the character remembers something that happened to someone else. **Clue option:** if the party is asking about Tam, make one failed save Tam's last night instead: grey gloves reaching over the side of a boat, a blank feeling where a route used to be, and a boat drifting with no cargo. Record it in the Memory ledger as kind "Mere."

**If they…**
- **[…sing:](play:s02-sing-response)** read "the singing stops, all at once, as if someone had laid a hand on it." Mark which characters sang with a dead person in mind. The Cantor will know.
- **…refuse to sing or mock it:** nothing attacks, but the ferry-folk turn their backs, and Mother Sallow says, "Then don't go on the water tonight." If they stole the Heart in Session 1 and haven't given it back, Sallow won't teach the hymn at all until they make amends: hand it back to Ila, and speak a dead person's name on the shore.
- **…try to stop the song with magic:** the spell Silence doesn't stop it. The Drowned hear grief, not sound.
- **…try to wade in toward the voice:** the water is black and cold and goes deep fast. Mother Sallow catches a sleeve: "Not at night. Not without the answer."

**Brother Hadrik Voss (optional complication).** Use him if your table likes arguing with allies. Halfway through the song, a grey-robed Faerûnian priest of Kelemvor walks down the shore swinging a smoking censer, a flask of holy water in his other hand.
- *Voice:* low, certain. Quotes the Doomguide.
- *Mannerism:* never lets the censer go out; relights it constantly.
- *Wants:* to bless the Mere and "lay the dead to rest," by force.
- *First words:* "The Lord of the Dead does not permit the dead to linger. I will give them peace."
- *Lines:* "These souls are owed judgment, not this endless keening." · "You would leave them singing in the cold forever?" · (if argued well) "...Judged. And remembered. Yes. The Doomguide says both."
- **[Talking him down:](play:s02-stop-hadrik)** Influence, Charisma (Persuasion) DC 15. He's Indifferent. A Study check first, Intelligence (Religion) DC 12, about what Kelemvor actually teaches gives Advantage: Kelemvor's dead are owed judgment and remembrance, not erasure. Restraining him also works (the ferry-folk will thank you). Hold Person works too, but he's Hostile when it ends.
- **[If he pours the holy water:](play:s02-hadrik-pours)** the Choir rises angry and one Drowned Singer climbs out of the shallows for him. Singing the response hymn stops it at once. Mother Sallow steps between Hadrik and the Singer and takes 5 damage if nobody covers her. The Old Shore blames "the Loud Quarter."

**How it ends.** The song fades, or stops for an hour, and the ferry-folk go home. Mother Sallow speaks to the party before she goes:

> **SAY:** "'The eastern ferry's been gone a week. Ferryman and all. It isn't like the Mere to keep a boat that long and give nothing back.' She knocks her pipe against a name-stone. 'Find it. I'll pay. And take a pilot. Brannagh won't refuse you if Ila asks.'"

### Scene 2: The Pilots' Hall

**Where and why.** The Pilots' Hall on the Quays, the next morning. Every boat that crosses the drowned town needs a pilot, and Brannagh Wick owns every pilot.

> **SAY:** "The Pilots' Hall is one long room hung with charts of streets nobody has walked in four hundred years. A short, wind-cracked woman with a coil of sounding-line over her shoulder is shouting tide-times at a ship's captain. 'She'll go out on the noon slack, lad, or she won't go at all.' She turns to you. 'And what do you want?'"

**What's really going on.** Brannagh trained Tam Harrow. The night he died, Tam took his boat out with no cargo and no ship to pilot, which no pilot does. She told the Court nothing. She believes he was going to the drowned belfry, and she's right.

**Pilot-Mistress Brannagh Wick** (about sixty, Scout).
- *Voice:* gruff; every ship is "she," every captain "lad."
- *Mannerism:* counts tides on her fingers out loud while she listens.
- *Wants in this scene:* to know the party won't get her boat or her pilots killed, and to keep Ila safe.
- *First words:* "And what do you want?"
- *Sample lines:* "The Mere's got two towns in it, lad. One you can see." · "Tam took her out with an empty hold. No pilot does that. No pilot." · "You bring my skiff back with everyone in it, or don't bring it back."

**What they can learn here:**
- **A skiff and a pilot are theirs for the asking** if Ila vouches for them or they helped the ferry-folk in Session 1 (filled the quota, defended the raid). Otherwise it's Influence, Charisma (Persuasion) DC 15, or 5 gp for a Quays skiff with no pilot.
- **[Tam went out that night with no cargo,](play:s02-ask-brannagh)** and Brannagh thinks he was heading for the belfry. She tells them with the same conditions as the skiff; on a failed check she tells them anyway after the trip, if they bring her boat back. This is a source for how Tam died.
- **The sandbank is in the middle of the Mere,** over the drowned town's old square.

**If they…**
- **[…go without a pilot:](play:s02-row-to-ferry)** the drowned rooftops just under the surface are a maze. A Wisdom (Survival) check, DC 12. On a failure they still get there, but the skiff scrapes a roof, ships water, and wakes the eels early: the eels get a free first round.
- **…want to swim or wade the whole way:** it's half a mile of cold water. Frigid Water applies. Talk them into a boat, or let them, and count minutes.
- **…ask Brannagh about the Court:** "The Court can't cross this water without my people. They know it. I know it. We're very polite to each other."

**How it ends.** They push off.

> **SAY:** "Under the skiff the drowned town slides by: a chimney, a weathervane, a street that slopes down into green dark. Something long and pale turns over beneath a roof and is gone. Ahead, the fog thins, and you see the eastern ferry, sitting crooked on a bar of yellow sand, with someone sitting very still in the stern."

### Scene 3: The sandbank

**Where and why.** A bar of sand about 30 feet across, in the middle of the Mere. The missing ferry is grounded on it.

> **SAY:** "The ferry is a flat barge with a single rail, run aground on the sand. In the stern sits the ferryman, an old man, face peaceful, eyes closed, both hands on a long oar. The oar is dripping, though it is bone dry around it. All around the sandbank, the water moves in slow pale rings."

**What's really going on.** The dead ferryman is the Tidewarden's old body. The Tidewarden is a bell-spirit of the Mere now, who ferries the dead, and he left this body behind when he took up Ossa's oar. Don't say any of that yet. The pale rings are memory-eels, which bite and steal small memories. They guard the drowned belfry nearby and treat the sandbank as theirs.

**What they can learn here:**
- **The ferryman died peacefully, not by violence.** A Search check, Wisdom (Medicine) DC 10, or free if anyone looks closely: no wounds, no water in his lungs. "As if he simply set himself down."
- **The oar drips with no water near it.** Free. A Study check, Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) DC 13: "This oar belongs to something, and that something isn't him anymore." Detect Magic shows a faint aura on the oar, none on the body.
- **The ferry's hold** has a few memory-pearls in a net bag (see Rewards).

**If they…**
- **[…step off the boat onto the sand or into the shallows:](play:s02-fight-eels)** the eels attack. See [the memory-eels](#memory-eels-at-the-sandbank).
- **[…stay in the skiff and try to slip past:](play:s02-sneak-eels)** each character makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 15. If at least half succeed, the eels never notice them and they reach the ferry quietly. If not, the eels attack, but they ignore anyone sitting still in a boat for the first round.
- **…raise the dead ferryman's oar:** the eels flee at once. Anyone who thinks of it deserves Heroic Inspiration.
- **[…want to take the body home:](play:s02-take-ferryman-home)** good instinct. Brannagh's pilot helps load it. The ferry-folk keep a cold-house for the drowned. The Tidewarden will remember this kindness in Session 3.

**How it ends.** The eels flee or are beaten. If the party treated the body with respect, go to Scene 4. If someone asks "What's under the water here?", the pilot points down: "The bell." Go to Scene 5. Otherwise, row home and go to Scene 6.

### Scene 4: The hooded ferryman (optional)

**Where and why.** On the sandbank, after the eels, or at the Old Shore's dock that evening. A hooded figure appears beside the dead ferryman, or asks the party to carry him across the water.

> **SAY:** "There's someone standing in the ferry's stern who wasn't there a moment ago. Hooded, tall, holding the same long oar as the dead man, and the oar is dripping. His voice is like water over stones. 'You found him. Will you carry him home? I would, but I carry others now.'"

**What's really going on.** This is the Tidewarden. He never lies. He speaks in riddles. He wants the dead carried to rest, and the Heart returned to Ossa's bell.

**The Tidewarden** (Specter; he doesn't fight).
- *Voice:* low, slow, like water over stones.
- *Mannerism:* never looks straight at anyone; the oar drips steadily the whole time.
- *Wants in this scene:* the body treated kindly. Then, a promise about the Heart.
- *First words:* "You found him. Will you carry him home?"
- *Sample lines:* "I paid for my passage once. Everyone does." · "The bell is waiting for what was taken. So is everyone under it." · "Ask me one thing. I'll answer true. Then I'll ask one thing back."

**What they can learn here:** one true answer to one question, in a riddle. If they ask about Tam: "He came looking for a road to the bell, and someone took the road out of his head." If they ask who the dead man is: "He was me. I set him down when I took up the oar." In return he asks: "Will the Heart come home?" Any honest answer is fine.

**If they…** pay him in memory (he asks only a mote, a small thing): he will offer passage to the bell in an air pocket, now or in Session 3. Refuse, and he simply nods and is gone.

**How it ends.** He's gone between one drip and the next.

### Scene 5: Down to the bell (optional)

**Where and why.** Just off the sandbank, the top of Old Pellmoor's Ossa Belfry stands in the deep water about forty feet down. The party goes because they want to see the bell, the Tidewarden offered, or the Heart is humming toward it.

> **SAY:** "You go down through water so clear and still it feels like falling through glass. Below you a whole street opens out, doors and windows and a leaning spire, and in the middle of it a bell-tower rises like a finger pointing at the sky. Inside the open belfry hangs a great dark bell with no Heart in it. Around it, pale figures float upright with their eyes open, singing without breath. One of them rises to meet you: a woman in a soaked ferry coat, hair drifting, eyes too bright."

**What's really going on.** The woman is the Cantor: Ondra Sallow, a ferry-woman who drowned 260 years ago and leads the Drowned Choir. Mother Sallow is her descendant. The Cantor won't attack anyone who answers her truthfully. She attacks liars and anyone who defaces the bell. She wants to be remembered by name, and released.

- **[Breath.](play:s02-dive-belfry)** Unless the Tidewarden gave them an air pocket, start counting breath. Getting down takes two or three turns, the conversation a few rounds, getting up two turns. That's tight but doable. Say each character's remaining rounds out loud every so often. If anyone runs out, give them a turn's warning before the first Exhaustion level, and remember they lose it all the moment they surface.

**The Cantor (Ondra Sallow)** (Ghost; she does not fight tonight unless the bell is attacked).
- *Voice:* she sings everything, in lines that sound like old hymns. Speak slowly, with a little tune.
- *Mannerism:* tilts her head as if listening to something very far away before each line.
- *Wants in this scene:* the truth. Who do you grieve? And, if they have it, the Heart.
- *First words:* "You have come down to the bell. Good. Tell me, who are you grieving? Do not lie to me. The dead can tell."
- *Sample lines:* "Say the name. Only the name. The water keeps the rest." · "A man came looking for a path to me. He never arrived." · "The Heart was taken from us. Ask the ferry-folk what they owe the dead."

**What they can learn here:**
- **Tam came to find a safe path to her and never arrived.** Free if anyone mentions Tam or Ila. This is another source on how Tam died; with Brannagh's or a Mere Memory, the Court's part is clear.
- **[The Heart belongs in this bell.](play:s02-ask-about-tongue)** Free.
- **A verse of Ossa's hymn,** if they answer truthfully: Advantage on the next saving throw against a Drowned Singer.
- **The full release verse,** four lines that let a spirit set down its song, if someone shares a sincere memory of a person who has died. The player can keep the details private and describe only the feeling. If your table is shy, she accepts someone grieving on Ila's behalf.

**If they…**
- **[…answer truthfully:](play:s02-truth-to-cantor)** she sings the name back. The water warms. She teaches the hymn verse.
- **[…share a real memory of someone who died:](play:s02-grieve-to-cantor)** she teaches the release verse too. Give that player Heroic Inspiration.
- **[…lie, or refuse her question:](play:s02-lie-to-cantor)** her eyes go dark and the Choir moves. See [the Drowned Singers](#drowned-singers-at-the-belfry). She keeps singing and doesn't join the fight.
- **…swim up to the bell:** she raises a hand and asks their purpose. Returning the Heart earns safe access. Attacking or scraping the bell after that warning makes a Singer intervene.
- **[…brought the Heart and want to ring the bell tonight:](play:s02-ring-ossa-early)** let them; it's a wild and wonderful early choice. Before anyone fits the Heart, **say the Toll and wait for a yes**: "Ossa's Toll is a memory of someone who has died. The Ringer chooses it, and it's gone for good. If you say no, the bell doesn't ring and nothing is taken." Ila may not be the Ringer; she's fourteen and has just lost her father, so if she's here she stands beside an adult who rings it. If they say yes, use the bell-ringing text in Session 3's plan, record the Toll in the Memory ledger, and record "They bring the Heart down and ring Ossa tonight" in Foresight.
- **…use a druid's swimming Wild Shape:** no breath clock for the druid. The Cantor reads an animal shape as a lie unless it says its own name.

**How it ends.** They surface: with the verse, with a fight behind them, or with the bell rung. Row home. Go to Scene 6.

### Scene 6: Back on the shore

**Where and why.** The Old Shore, late afternoon. Mother Sallow is waiting on the dock with her ledger, because she promised to pay, and because something is wrong with the ledger.

> **SAY:** "Mother Sallow is sitting on an upturned crate at the end of the dock, a heavy ledger open across her knees. She doesn't look up. She runs one green-stained finger down the inside of the spine, where a page should be and isn't. 'Forty years I've kept this,' she says. 'Somebody's been in it.'"

**What's really going on.** The missing page lists the children the Court questioned over the last two years. Brother Eld, the village friar, took it. Ila's name is on it. That's Session 3. Tonight, just let them see the torn edge.

**What they can learn here:** a page is missing from the recent register; it was torn out carefully, by someone who knew where to look. Don't name Eld unless the players work it out; if they ask who could get at the ledger, Sallow says, "Me. Ila. And the chapel, when they borrow it for the Tithe."

**How it ends.** Mother Sallow pays them for the ferry and asks the question that ends the session.

### Running the fights (Session 2)

All of these are written for four level-3 characters. Foresight shows the difficulty for your actual party. Water makes everything harder than its numbers: breath is the real clock.

#### Memory-eels at the sandbank

*Six memory-eels. Each: AC 13, 13 HP, swim 30 ft.*
- **Setup:** the eels circle in the deep water 10 to 20 feet off the sand. Anyone wading, swimming or standing at the water's edge is a target. Anyone sitting still in a boat is ignored for the first round.
- **On their turn:** an eel swims in and **Bites** (+4 to hit, 1d8 + 2 piercing). If the bite deals damage, the target makes a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw; on a failure the player chooses one small memory their character loses until their next long rest (a breakfast, a song's second verse, a friend's eye colour). Write it in the Memory ledger as kind "Eel." Next turn, the same eel tries to **Coil**: a DC 12 Strength saving throw, or 3d4 bludgeoning damage and Grappled (escape DC 12), and it drags the character 10 feet under.
- **Describe hits:** "Something like a cold wet rope whips round your calf, and for a moment you can't remember what you had for breakfast." **Misses:** "A pale shape slides past your hand and is gone."
- **Retreat:** they flee when only two are left, or the moment someone raises the dead ferryman's oar.
- **Scale on the fly:** three players: four eels. Five players: eight. If the party is struggling, the eels lose interest in anyone who climbs back into the skiff.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** a character who drops to 0 HP in the water floats up; the eels lose interest in anything that stops struggling. The pilot hauls them into the skiff. After the fight, let them take a short rest on the sand before any dive: don't chain this straight into the Singers.

#### Drowned Singers at the belfry

*Two Drowned Singers (each AC 12, 22 HP, swim 40 ft, never need to breathe) and two memory-eels. Only if they lie to the Cantor or attack the bell.*
- **Setup:** the Singers rise from the belfry, one on each side of the party. The eels stay near the surface and harry anyone left in the boat or treading water.
- **Round 1:** one Singer uses **Wail of Names**: everyone within 30 feet who can hear it makes a DC 11 Wisdom save, taking 2d4 psychic damage and becoming Frightened until the end of their next turn on a failure (half damage, no fear, on a success). It recharges on a 5 or 6 on a d6 at the start of its turn.
- **Each round:** the other Singer uses **Cold Grasp** (+4 to hit, 1d6 + 2 bludgeoning plus 1d6 cold) on the nearest diver. On a hit the diver is Grappled (escape DC 12), and the Singer pulls them 10 feet deeper. That costs them turns of breath; say so.
- **Describe hits:** "Fingers like wet stone close on your wrist, and the cold climbs to your elbow." **Misses:** "Its hand closes on the water where you were."
- **When it ends:** a character who sings the response hymn as an action stops the Singers attacking the whole group for an hour. Make sure the players remember they know it. The Singers also never pursue anyone above the surface: going up always works.
- **Scale on the fly:** three players: one Singer. Five players: a third Singer. Show the Singers rising a round before they arrive. If anyone hits 2 levels of Exhaustion from breath, have the Singers pause and watch: the dead don't want more company.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** a diver who drops to 0 HP is released, and the Choir lifts them gently to the surface. They're Unconscious and need help, but they're breathing. The paralysis of an ordinary ghoul is removed from these Singers on purpose: a Paralyzed diver would drown.

#### The Mere road cart (if Ila was taken)

*A Hushman Sergeant (AC 15, 52 HP) and three Hushmen (AC 12, 32 HP).*
- **Setup:** two Hushmen walk beside the cart, one rides on the tailboard, the Sergeant rides up front beside the ferry-folk driver.
- **Each round:** the Hushmen keep the cart between themselves and the party, use **Arrest** (DC 12 Strength save or Grappled) on anyone who reaches the tailboard, and shout for the next checkpoint. The Sergeant attacks twice and uses **Command** to give a Hushman an extra attack with +1d4. They don't drive the cart on while it's under attack.
- **Retreat:** the Sergeant yields the cart when two Hushmen drop, or the moment the ferry-folk driver unhitches the horse.
- **Scale on the fly:** three players: drop one Hushman. Five players: add one.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** a downed character is bound and left at the ford. Ila stays inside the cart the whole time, and is never hurt.
