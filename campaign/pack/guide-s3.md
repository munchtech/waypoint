## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. At the table, read the boxed text aloud and glance at the bullets. The rules from the Session 1 and 2 guides still apply. Tonight's big new rule is the Toll, and it has a script: use it word for word.*

### Before you start (Session 3)

**Level:** four characters at level 3. No level-up tonight, even after the big choice. Level 4 comes after the Hallowmere investigation (Session 6).

**Check the world state first.** Open Foresight and look at four facts. They decide which scenes run:
- **Ossa's Heart:** with Ila or the party (the full choice in Scene 5), with the Court (the choice is how to get it back), at the bell already (they rang Ossa in Session 2: skip Scenes 5 and 6), or sold to Venn (Scene 5 becomes the Gilded Heron).
- **Ila Harrow:** free, hidden, or arrested. Arrested means a rescue is still open: Brannagh's pilots can still reach the cart or a Court room.
- **Tobias Quill:** if Suspicious or Bribed, he comes back tonight (Scene 4).
- **The Drowned Choir:** if still Hunting, the ferry-stair to the bell is guarded (Scene 6).

**What this session is about.** A page is missing from Mother Sallow's ledger. It's in the desk of Brother Eld, the gentle village friar, and it lists the children he reported to the Court. Ila's name is on it. The party works out how her father really died. Then they make the biggest choice of Act I: what to do with Ossa's Heart. Return it to the drowned bell and maybe ring it, at a real personal cost? Carry it to the capital? Keep it and defend the village? Sell it? Give it to the Court? Every one of those is a good story. At the end, whatever they chose, a polite letter arrives from the Quiet Chancellor, inviting them to Hallowmere.

**Your goals tonight:**
- The players find the page (always: it's never behind a failed roll).
- They decide what to do about Eld, and live with it.
- Someone tells Ila the truth, gently.
- The players, not you, choose the Heart's fate. Don't steer. Every option is written up.
- End on: "What will you carry to Hallowmere?"

**Prepare:**
- **Tokens or minis:** the four characters; Mother Sallow; Brother Eld; Ila; Quill and one Hushman (if he returns); the Tidewarden and the Cantor (if they go to the bell); three Drowned Singers and two memory-eels (if the stair is guarded); Master Venn, three Coster guards and the ship's mate (only if the Heart was sold).
- **Maps:** your IsoForge kit (below) has Eld's chapel and Ossa's belfry. For the ferry-stair, draw a stone stair 10 feet wide going down from the old ferry landing into the water and along a drowned street to the belfry. For the *Gilded Heron*, a ship's deck with a gangplank, a hatch and a cabin.
- **Props:**
  - *The missing page:* write it yourself on a scrap of paper. A heading, "Children questioned, by report of the chapel, 410 to 412," and six to eight names with dates. Put **Ila Harrow, 410** last, in the same tidy hand.
  - *The Court forms:* two or three copies of the same names on "Court" paper, with a grey thumbprint for a seal.
  - *The patrol entry:* one line: "Pilot T. Harrow stopped on the Mere. Route memory taken. Released." Dated six weeks ago.
  - *The Chancellor's letter:* the text is in the plan above. Fold it, and drip grey candle wax or draw a grey seal on it. Hand it over at the end.
  - *The Heart prop* from Session 1, and the *Toll card* (below).
- **The Toll card.** Write this on a card and read it aloud, every time, before any ringing:

> "Ossa's Toll is a memory of someone who has died. The Ringer chooses it. It goes, and it can never be bought back. If you say no, the bell does not ring and nothing is taken. Who will ring it, and will you pay?"

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **The Toll.** Ringing a bell always costs the Ringer something personal: for Ossa, a memory of someone who has died. You say the price before the ringing, and the player says yes or no. No means no ringing, no loss, and no penalty. The player chooses which memory; you never do. It doesn't have to be a loved one. Write it in the Memory ledger as kind "Toll," bell "Ossa." A Toll can never be bought back.
- **Silence** (Brother Eld can cast it once). A 20-foot sphere where no sound exists. Nobody inside can cast spells that need speech. Eld uses it to run away, never to fight.
- **Influence, Study, Search, Help, Hide:** as in Session 1. Brother Eld is Friendly to anyone gentle and Indifferent to anyone hard.
- **Breath, Frigid Water, underwater fighting:** as in Session 2. The Tidewarden's air pocket removes the breath clock.
- **The rule for clues:** the page is found once they get access. The roll only decides how fast, and whether they also find the forms and the patrol entry.

### Scene 1: Who was on the ferry

**Where and why.** Mother Sallow's reed-hut, the morning after Session 2. She asked the party to come; they owe her an answer about the ferry, and she owes them one about the ledger.

> **SAY:** "Mother Sallow has the ledger open on the table between the tea bowls. 'I've kept this ledger for forty years. Every name the Mere has taken, every name the Court has Tithed. There should be a page in the recent register, listing children questioned by the Court over the last two years. It's gone. And I know who would take it.'"

**What's really going on.** The page is in Brother Eld's desk. Eld has been reporting the families of Bellwright sympathisers to the Hushmen for two years, and the names he reports are the children's. As for the ferry: the dead ferryman is the Tidewarden's old body. The ledger lists him as the eastern-marsh ferryman, **Old Abel Tarn**, who rowed it longer than anyone can remember.

**Mother Sallow.** As before: dry, short, honest, pipe between her teeth. *Wants:* the page back, and Ila safe. *First words:* see the box. *Lines:* "Abel Tarn. Rowed that ferry since before I was born. Funny thing. Nobody remembers him getting old." · "The chapel borrows this ledger for the Tithe. Only the chapel." · "Bring it back. Don't break the man. He breaks easy."

**What they can learn here:**
- **Who was on the ferry:** Abel Tarn, the eastern ferryman. Free. If they met the hooded ferryman in Session 2, a Study check, Intelligence (Religion) DC 12, or simply the players' own guess, connects them: "The Tidewarden wore him once." Mother Sallow nods slowly: "Then he's rowing the dead now. Good. Somebody should."
- **Who took the page:** Brother Eld, who runs the chapel. Free; Sallow says his name if they ask. If they kept her three-day bargain, she also hands them Eld's chapel key without a word.

**How it ends.** They go to the chapel.

### Scene 2: Brother Eld's desk

**Where and why.** The Old Shore chapel, a narrow stone building with a bell-frame that has never held a bell. Eld lives in two rooms behind it. The page is in his desk.

> **SAY:** "The chapel is clean and cold, with nine plain stools in a row and a grey cloth over the empty bell-frame. It smells of lamp oil and ink. Through a side door you can see a little room with a writing desk, a narrow bed and a pile of grey Court paper squared off very neatly. Somewhere in the back, a man is praying, softly, the same line over and over."

**What's really going on.** Eld is a good man who did a bad thing because the Church told him it was kind. He knows the page is gone from the ledger because he took it, and he's terrified that someone will read it. He wants absolution more than he wants to be safe.

**Brother Eld Marrowby** (Priest Acolyte; can cast Silence once).
- *Voice:* soft, careful, apologetic. Quotes the Ninefold scripture constantly: "In quiet, peace." "The bell broke the world; the silence mends it."
- *Mannerism:* rubs his ink-stained fingers together as if they're cold; can't meet anyone's eyes for long.
- *Wants in this scene:* to not be found out. Then, once found out: forgiveness.
- *First words* (when they come in): "Peace on you. Are you here for the Tithe? It isn't Ossday."
- *First words when confronted:* "The page is in my desk, Mother. The Court asked me to keep it. I didn't read it, because I didn't want to. But I know what it says. I copied the names onto the Court's forms myself."
- *Sample lines:* "They said it was for the children's protection. They said the Court would watch over them." · "I asked no questions. That was the sin, wasn't it. Not the asking. The not asking." · "Will you absolve me? Can you? Can anyone?"

**Getting access** (any of these works):
- **Mother Sallow's key** (if the bargain held). They walk in.
- **Ask him.** He's Friendly to gentle people: no roll. If they're hard with him, Influence, Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) DC 15.
- **Sneak in** while he prays. Dexterity (Stealth) DC 13. On a failure, he hears them and comes in from the vestry, frightened, which just moves straight to the confrontation.
- **Distract him:** Ila or Sallow can call him outside. Any reasonable plan works.

**Searching the desk:** once they're in, they find the page. No roll needed. A **Study** check, Intelligence (Investigation) DC 14, finds it quickly and also turns up the matching **Court forms** and the **patrol entry** about Tam Harrow. On a failure they still find the page, but it takes long enough that Eld walks in on them; give them the forms and the entry once they talk to him.

**What they can learn here:**
- **The page lists children the chapel reported.** Ila's name is the last one. Essential; always found.
- **The Court forms** show Eld copied the names for the Hushmen. Useful evidence later (a Dawn Order captain will value them in Session 6).
- **The patrol entry:** "Pilot T. Harrow stopped on the Mere. Route memory taken. Released." This is a source on how Tam died.

**If they…**
- **…take the page and confront him privately:** he asks for absolution first. Then he gives them the name of one more child on the list whose family can still be warned, and leaves Pellmoor on the next ferry for the ash fields of Kell's Crossing, to pray among the dead fields.
- **…expose him in front of the village:** the Old Shore turns on him. He flees to Hallowmere that night in shame. The party will see him again at the Quiet Assembly in Session 4, as a Court chaplain who won't meet their eyes. Mother Sallow says nothing kind and nothing cruel.
- **…forgive him:** he cries like a man who has held it in for two years. Then he asks what he can do. The Court has invited him to be a chaplain at a Hallowmere masque; he offers to go and be their inside ear. He still writes to the Court, and tells them what he writes.
- **…threaten or hurt him:** he casts Silence and runs for the water. He's no fighter. If anyone means to kill him, ask once, plainly: "You mean to kill him?" If yes, he dies; the ferry-folk say nothing and the chapel stays shut. The page and forms still point at Quill and Hallowmere.
- **…use Zone of Truth or Detect Thoughts:** both work. Detect Thoughts shows a man frozen in fear repeating a prayer. Zone of Truth gets a full confession, which he would have given anyway.

**How it ends.** They have the page. Someone has to tell Ila. Go to Scene 3.

### Scene 3: Ila's name

**Where and why.** Wherever Ila is: on the old dock, at the Pilots' Hall, in her house. This is a quiet scene. Let it be quiet.

> **SAY:** "Ila takes the page and reads it the way she counts masts, one name at a time, under her breath. She reaches the bottom and stops. She reads it again. Then she says, very flatly: 'That's me. Four hundred and ten. That's when Dad started locking the door.'"

**What's really going on.** Two years ago Eld reported Ila to the Court, and Tam knew. That's why he was afraid; that's why he hid the Heart with her and went out to find a safe path alone. Now the party can put together how he died.

**Ila.** *Voice:* blunt, and much quieter than usual. *Mannerism:* counts, and then stops counting. *Wants:* the truth, all of it, and a say in what happens next. *Lines:* "Don't say it's fine." · "So he wasn't careless. Somebody did it." · "I want to decide. About the Heart. It was his. It's mine to decide too." · (if someone tells her the hard truth gently) "...Thank you. Nobody tells me things."

**Putting together how Tam died.** Any **two** of these establish it. Confirm it as soon as the players have two:
- the **patrol entry** from Eld's desk;
- **Brannagh Wick** (Tam took his boat out with no cargo, headed for the belfry);
- the **Cantor** (he came to find a safe path to her and never arrived);
- a **Mere Memory** of Tam's last night (grey gloves, an emptied mind, a drifting boat).

The answer: a Court patrol stopped Tam's boat on the Mere, took his memory of the route to the bell, and left him on the water. Confused, he fell in and drowned. The Choir kept his body safe; they didn't kill him. Let Ila hear it from whoever she trusts most.

**If they…**
- **…tell her the truth, plainly and kindly:** she trusts them. She argues for returning the Heart to the Mere, "where Dad was taking it," and asks if she can come. Give that character Heroic Inspiration.
- **…say "don't worry about it":** she stops talking to that character for the rest of the act.
- **…want to hide her:** Brannagh's loft at the Pilots' Hall, with a pilot on the stair, is the safest place on the Mere. The three households (if they filled Quill's quota) also offer one. Ila argues, then accepts it if someone explains why.
- **…want to take her with them:** Mother Sallow allows it only if an adult promises to bring her home. Ila is fourteen. She travels with the party but is never put in a fight or described in harm; give her a quiet place at the edge of every scene.

**How it ends.** Ila folds the page and gives it back. "What are we going to do with the Heart?" Go to Scene 4 or Scene 5.

### Scene 4: Knocks at the door (optional)

**Where and why.** Before the party decides, the world pushes. Use one or two of these, depending on what happened in Session 1. Each one is a short scene and a reason to decide soon.

**Quill comes back** (if he was Suspicious or Bribed). He brings a second Hushman, who isn't polite.
> **SAY:** "Two grey coats on the lane. The first one smiles. 'Good afternoon. Just following up.'"
- He asks the coat question again. If Mother Sallow's name was given in Session 1, he asks for her ledger.
- He wants a reason to search. Influence, Charisma (Persuasion) DC 16, buys a week. A bribed Quill takes more gold and complains less. Hold Person or Suggestion works, but the Landing House reads the hum of the spell as a confession.

**Master Venn's note** (if he knows about the Heart). A Coster clerk delivers it to the Shuttered Bell.
> **SAY (the note):** "Friend: my offer stands. Six hundred in gold for the humming relic, and a berth home on the *Gilded Heron*, which sails in three days. Discretion assured. With warmest regard, O. Venn."

**The three households warn them** (if the party filled Quill's quota in Session 1). A ferry-folk woman (pick a name from the list) knocks: "The Hushmen are asking about the Harrow house again. You can hide her with us, or the bundle. Either. Both." Give Heroic Inspiration to a player who thanks her by name.

**How it ends.** The pressure is clear: decide before the Court does. Go to Scene 5.

### Scene 5: What to do with the Heart

**Where and why.** Wherever the party gathers to talk: Sallow's hut, the end of the old dock, the back room of the Shuttered Bell. This is a decision scene, and it belongs to the players.

**How to run a decision scene.** Put the Heart prop in the middle of the table. Ask: "What do you want to do with it?" Then stop talking. Let them argue. If they ask Mother Sallow, Ila, Brannagh or anyone else for advice, give it in that person's voice. Don't tell them which option is right; there isn't one. If they go round in circles for more than ten minutes, have a knock at the door (Scene 4) or ask each player, one at a time, "What does your character want?"

**What each person thinks** (only if asked):
- **Ila:** "Take it home. To the bell. That's where Dad was taking it."
- **Mother Sallow:** "The Mere's owed. But don't ring it for me. Ring it if it's yours to ring."
- **Brannagh:** "Whatever you do, do it before the Court does. I can get you anywhere on this water. Not past the Hushmen on the road."

**The choices, and what each one does:**
1. **Return it to the Mere.** They take it down to Ossa's bell. Go to Scene 6. Whether they also ring it is a separate choice with a Toll. The Court still wants it (Session 4).
2. **Carry it to Hallowmere,** to the Dawn Order or the Bellwrights. Act II begins early. Brannagh can ferry them across the Mere (three days to the capital instead of six) to a landing that skips the first checkpoint. Faerûnian characters need the Chancellor's letter (Scene 7), which reaches them at the first checkpoint by the Court's own rider.
3. **Keep it in Pellmoor and defend the village.** Act II begins as a siege. Quill issues a deadline; villagers prepare shelter and an escape route. Show the deadline and the retreat routes before anything else happens. The siege itself is Session 4's opening.
4. **Give it to the Court.** Maudrin is delighted. Show the players the arrest order for Ila before anyone acts on it, and give them a chance to warn her, hide her or take her along. If they do nothing, Hushmen come at dawn (Off-screen; she's held in a clean room in Hallowmere, never harmed).
5. **Sell it to Master Venn.** He pays 600 gp (Influence, Charisma (Persuasion) DC 15 raises it to 900). It goes aboard the *Gilded Heron*, which sails in three days and crosses the Seam in a fourth. Ila learns within the hour. Tell the table plainly: if it leaves Corvane, Ossa can't be rung until it comes back. Brannagh can delay the ship a day by refusing it a pilot.

**If the Heart is already gone:**
- **With the Court** (handed over in Session 1): the choice is how to get it back. Session 4's Assembly is the obvious place. Skip Scene 6.
- **With Venn:** run [the Gilded Heron](#boarding-the-gilded-heron). Three ways: board her at night, have Brannagh deny her a pilot until Venn deals, or buy it back (1,200 gp or a heartkeep; Influence DC 15 brings it to 900).
- **Already at the bell** (they rang Ossa in Session 2): skip to Scene 7, after a quiet scene with Mother Sallow, who remembers her husband now.

**How it ends.** They choose. Write it down. If they're returning it, go to Scene 6. Otherwise go to Scene 7.

### Scene 6: Ossa's bell (optional)

**Where and why.** The ferry-stair: an old stone stair that runs from the Old Shore's first ferry landing down into the Mere and along a drowned street to the Ossa Belfry. They're here because they chose to return the Heart.

> **SAY:** "The ferry-stair goes down into the water like a stair into a cellar. The first steps are dry. The next are wet. The next are under the surface, green and clear, and you can see them going on and on, down into the drowned street, toward the finger of the bell-tower. At the top of the stair, a hooded figure is waiting with a dripping oar."

**What's really going on.** The Tidewarden waits for anyone who treated his old body kindly, paid him in Session 2, or simply comes with the Heart and the hymn. He can hold a pocket of breathable air around everyone who accepts peaceful passage. If the Choir is still Hunting, Drowned Singers guard the stair against anyone who comes without the hymn or the Tidewarden. At the bottom, the Cantor waits.

**The Tidewarden.** As in Session 2: slow, riddling, honest. *First words:* "Passage is given to those who carry the dead home. You carry something of theirs." *Line:* "Breathe. The water will let you, for as long as you're welcome."

**Getting down** (any of these works):
- **The Tidewarden's air pocket.** No breath clock. The safest way.
- **The response hymn.** Singing it as they go down earns passage from the Choir with no memory price. Any guard Singers stand aside.
- **Diving.** Use the breath rules from Session 2. It's 40 feet; the conversation at the bottom takes a few rounds.
- **Magic or a clever plan:** let it work.

If none of those apply and the Choir is Hunting, see [the ferry-stair guard](#the-ferry-stair-guard).

**Ila.** She can come to the top of the stair. She stays above the water with Mother Sallow or Brannagh. She never goes down with the divers. Check your session-zero Off-limits list before this scene.

**The Cantor.** At the belfry she rises to meet them: "You have brought it home. Will you ring it?" *Lines:* "A bell rung for nothing is a bell that lies." · "Pay what it asks. Then sing me down, if you know the words."

**The ringing.** If they want to ring Ossa, stop and **read the Toll card aloud**. Wait for a clear yes. Then:
- **Ila may not be the Ringer.** She's fourteen and has just lost her father. If she volunteers, an adult rings it with her hand on theirs.
- The player chooses the memory. They can describe it or keep it private. Write it in the Memory ledger as kind "Toll," bell "Ossa."
- If they say no, nothing is taken and the bell doesn't ring. That's a fine outcome. The Heart still goes home; the Drowned are calmer.

> **SAY (when Ossa rings):** "The bell rings from under the water. It is deep and slow and it makes the whole Mere ripple. Every grey face on the shore lifts toward the sound. The Drowned fall silent, and one voice, very old and very clear, speaks a single name. Then the Mere is only water again, and the reeds bend in a breeze that smells of rain."

**After the ringing:**
- **Mother Sallow remembers.** On the shore, she suddenly remembers her husband Davin's face, and the night of the Wrongweather. She sits down on the stones and laughs and cries at once. Let it be a big moment.
- **The release verse.** If anyone learned it from the Cantor (or Mother Sallow reads it from the ledger: an hour's work), singing it now releases the Choir. The singer must sincerely remember someone who has died. The Cantor speaks her own name, "Ondra," and goes quiet. Released spirits may return once, willingly, as allies later.
- **The Court notices.** The Hushmen pull their collectors back from the newly quiet Mere for a while. The Court still wants the Heart.

**How it ends.** The party climbs back up the stair into a breeze nobody on the Mere has felt in four hundred years. Go to Scene 7.

### Scene 7: The letter

**Where and why.** That evening, wherever the party is. A Court rider in grey brings a letter, whatever the party chose. It's addressed to "those who carried the Heart of Ossa."

**What's really going on.** Maudrin Vesk, the Quiet Chancellor, has read Quill's report. She wants the party (and the Heart, if it's still unrung) close where she can see them. The Court prefers to buy before it threatens. The letter's grey seal is a real writ of passage for everyone named in it, Faerûnians included.

**Hand over the letter prop and read the letter in the plan above aloud.** Then let them react.

**If they…**
- **…accept:** the letter is a writ. Brannagh offers the Mere crossing. Ten days to the Assembly.
- **…refuse:** don't force the trip. Act II comes to them instead: a Dawn Order rider, or a Bellwright courier asking for help. The letter still works as a writ if they keep it.
- **…burn it:** a grey seal hisses in the fire and doesn't melt. Mother Sallow says, "That's Court wax. It remembers." The Court will invite them again.

### Running the fights (Session 3)

Most tables won't fight tonight. These are here if they do. Both are written for four level-3 characters.

#### The ferry-stair guard

*Three Drowned Singers (each AC 12, 22 HP) and two memory-eels. Only if the Choir is Hunting and the party comes without the hymn or the Tidewarden.*
- **Setup:** the stair is 10 feet wide underwater, so only two characters can stand side by side. Two Singers block the stair below the party. The third circles above with the eels, coming at anyone at the back.
- **Each round:** one Singer opens with **Wail of Names** (DC 11 Wisdom save, 2d4 psychic and Frightened on a failure). The others use **Cold Grasp** to grapple and pull divers deeper. The eels bite whoever is treading water at the surface.
- **When it ends:** at once, if someone sings the response hymn as an action, the Tidewarden arrives, or the Heart is held up and offered to the bell. Make sure a player remembers the hymn; Mother Sallow can shout it from the shore.
- **Scale on the fly:** three players: two Singers. If Brother Hadrik forced his blessing in Session 2, add a fourth Singer. Struggling party: the Tidewarden appears a round early.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** as in Session 2. A diver at 0 HP is lifted to the surface by the Choir. The Singers never follow anyone out of the water.

#### Boarding the Gilded Heron

*Three Coster guards (Tough), the ship's mate (Bandit Captain, a Zhentarim hand), and Master Venn (Noble). Only if the Heart was sold.*
- **Setup:** night, the ship at anchor off the Quays. One guard at the gangplank, one at the cabin hatch, one walking the deck. The mate sleeps near the cabin where the Heart is locked in a chest. The Baldurian crew stay out of it.
- **Sneaking aboard:** each character makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 15. If they manage it, only one guard and the mate are awake. Invisibility and Misty Step help, but the deck lamps catch a shimmer on the water; the mate has Passive Perception 14.
- **Each round, if it's a fight:** the guards fight in pairs (Pack Tactics: Advantage when an ally is next to the target). The mate goes straight for whoever holds the Heart. Venn stays behind them and talks.
- **When it ends:** Venn negotiates the moment a guard is Bloodied (half HP): "Friends! There's no need. Take it. Take it, and leave my ship, and nobody tells the Court anything." He means it.
- **Scale on the fly:** if the party arrives with Hold Person and Invisibility ready, add two guards. If they're struggling, Brannagh's pilots row up alongside with lanterns and shout, and the guards lose heart.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** the guards would rather throw someone overboard than kill them. A character at 0 HP is put in a rowboat. Venn holds a grudge, and pays Quill to watch the party.

### Pacing (Session 3)

| Time | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:15 | Recap; check the four facts; Scene 1 at Sallow's |
| 0:15 to 1:00 | Scene 2: Eld's chapel and the page |
| 1:00 to 1:30 | Scene 3: Ila's name, and how Tam died |
| 1:30 to 1:50 | Scene 4: a knock at the door (break after) |
| 1:50 to 2:30 | Scene 5: the choice |
| 2:30 to 3:15 | Scene 6: the bell, or the *Gilded Heron* |
| 3:15 to 3:30 | Scene 7: the letter, and the closing question |

- **Running long:** cut Scene 4. If they choose to return the Heart late in the evening, stop at the top of the ferry-stair and start Session 4 with the descent.
- **Running short:** let Brother Eld come to them and ask for help warning the other families on the list. Add a farewell at the Shuttered Bell, where Dorra Pell names every ship that brought each character. Run both Scene 4 knocks.
- **If you need to stop early:** stop right after the choice in Scene 5. Ask "What will you carry to Hallowmere?" Next session opens with the bell or the road, and then the letter.

### If you get stuck (Session 3)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **Ila asks a direct question.** "Are you going to give it to them?" "Did my dad do something wrong?" Nobody can ignore her.
2. **The Court moves.** Grey coats on the lane; a rider on the Mere road; a Hushman counting houses. Then ask, "What do you do?"
3. **Mother Sallow taps her pipe.** She says one proverb that points at the next step: "A ledger's no use shut." "The water gives back what you carry to it."
