## Running this session (new DM guide)

### Before you start (Session 1)

**Level:** four characters at level 3. Nobody levels up tonight. The party reaches level 4 after the Hallowmere investigation (Session 6).

**What this session is about.** The characters arrive at Pellmoor Landing, a loud foreign port built over a drowned town, and find the quiet old ferry village at its edge. A grieving fourteen-year-old girl, Ila Harrow, is holding a bronze bell-Heart that hums. Her father drowned six weeks ago. Mother Sallow, the village's old reed-witch, asks the party to keep the Heart from the Court for three days. Then the Court arrives, politely, in grey gloves. The session is about meeting people, earning trust, and making the first real choice: what do you tell the man from the Court?

**Your goals tonight** (not the players' goals; theirs are whatever they choose):
- Every player gets one moment to show who their character is.
- The players meet Ila, Mother Sallow and Quill, and care about at least one of them.
- The players learn three facts: the Heart belongs to a drowned bell, the Court wants it, and memory is money here.
- End on a question, not a resolution: "Who was singing?"

**Prepare:**
- **Tokens or minis:** the four characters; Ila; Mother Sallow; Tobias Quill; four Hushmen (one walks with Quill, three wait at the lane's end); a Hushman Sergeant for the night raid. Optional: Brannagh Wick, Sela Maunt, Master Venn, Sister Aurelie, a Harper with a lute.
- **Maps:** sketch each place on paper, a battle mat or in Foundry. The old dock: a long pier, net-racks, reed roofs behind. Mother Sallow's hut: one round room, a table, a stove. For the raid, sketch the Harrow house: one room, one door, one shuttered window over the water, net-racks outside.
- **The Heart prop:** something heavy and roughly pear-shaped wrapped in an old cloth: a brass doorknob, a big smooth stone, a candle in a jar. Put your phone inside the cloth on vibrate and call it from the table, or simply hum one low note when Ila holds it. Stop humming when she puts it down. Players remember this.
- **The response hymn:** two lines you can speak or sing. Use this, or make up your own: *Call:* "Who walks the water?" *Answer:* "We remember you."
- **Quill's question,** written on a card so you don't forget it: "Where did the child's coat go?"
- **Names:** have the list at the end of this guide open.
- **Ask before play:** how each character arrived (off a ship through the Seam this morning, down the Mere road, or living here for years), and one thing each character has lost. Write the losses down; Session 2 and the Toll use them.

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **Influence** (an action). A character tries to talk someone into something. If the person is Friendly and the request is reasonable, it just works: no roll. If they're Hostile, it doesn't. If they're Indifferent or the request costs them something, the player rolls Charisma (Persuasion, Deception or Intimidation). The DC is 15 or the person's Intelligence score, whichever is higher. A failure usually can't be repeated the same way until something changes.
- **Study** (an action). Think hard about something: an Intelligence check (Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature or Religion).
- **Search** (an action). Look or sense carefully: a Wisdom check (Insight, Medicine, Perception or Survival).
- **Help** (an action). A character who is proficient in the skill helps a friend, who gets Advantage on that check. In a fight, Help gives an ally Advantage on their next attack against an enemy within 5 feet of the helper.
- **Hide** (an action). Out of sight and behind cover, a character rolls Dexterity (Stealth) against DC 15. On a success they're hidden until they make noise, attack or are found; the Stealth total is the number anyone searching has to beat.
- **Grappled** (a condition). Speed 0, and Disadvantage on attacks against anyone except the grappler. To escape, use an action and roll Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) against the escape DC. The Hushmen's Arrest grapples.
- **Heroic Inspiration.** You give it for great play: a brave choice, a kind one, a moment that made the table laugh. The player can spend it to reroll any one die and must keep the new roll. A character holds only one at a time.
- **The golden rule for clues:** an essential clue is never behind a failed roll. Give the clue. Let the roll decide how fast, how much extra, or what it costs.

### Scene 1: The old dock

**Where and why.** The Old Shore's oldest ferry-dock, mid-morning. However the characters arrived, the dock is where the noise of the port runs out: newcomers walk here to get their bearings, locals walk here because they always have. Ila is standing at the end of it.

> **SAY:** "The fog lifts off the Mute Mere in long grey ribbons. Forty masts stand over water so still the ships look painted on it, and just under the surface you can see rooftops, a whole drowned town, asleep. Behind you the Quays shout in a dozen languages. Ahead, past the last warehouse, the old reed-roofed ferry village leans toward the water as if none of that were happening. On the end of the oldest dock stands a thin girl in a man's ferry coat, much too big for her. She is holding a bundle of oilskin against her chest. She doesn't turn round. She says: 'Are you here for the Heart, or for the bell?'"

**What's really going on.** Six weeks ago Tam Harrow, Ila's father, brought up the bronze Heart of Ossa in his fishing net. He had stolen it from the Court twenty years ago and hidden it; the net found it by accident. The Drowned Choir asked him to return it to its bell under the Mere. He left it with Ila and went out at night to find a safe way down. A Court patrol took his memory of the route and left him on the water. He fell in and drowned. Ila doesn't know any of that. She knows her father is dead, the Heart hums, and nobody believes her.

**Ila Harrow** (fourteen, Commoner).
- *Voice:* blunt, flat, quick. Asks direct questions and waits for answers.
- *Mannerism:* counts things when she's nervous: masts, boards, people. Out loud, under her breath.
- *Wants in this scene:* someone to take her seriously. Someone who heard the hum too.
- *First words:* "Are you here for the Heart, or for the bell?" Then, whatever they say: "Did you hear it too?"
- *Sample lines:* "Thirty-one masts. There were thirty yesterday." · "Don't say it's fine. Everybody says it's fine." · "It hums when I hold it. Watch."

**What they can learn here:**
- **Ila has a bronze bell-Heart and it hums when she holds it.** Free, once someone asks kindly. She shows it to whoever asks respectfully.
- **[Her father, Tam, drowned six weeks ago.](play:s01-ask-ila-father)** Free if anyone asks why she's alone. She says he went out in his boat at night with no cargo and no ship to pilot, "which no pilot does," and they found him in the reeds.
- **[It belongs to a bell.](play:s01-detect-magic-tongue)** A Study check, Intelligence (Religion or History) DC 13: "This is a Heart: the clapper of one of the Seven Bells. One of them hung in the Mere." On a failure, Mother Sallow tells them in Scene 2. Detect Magic shows a faint aura of no school, and the Heart hums back one clean note.
- **[Ila's mother, Nessa, is alive](play:s01-ask-ila-mother)** and lives far north at Kell's Crossing, if anyone asks. Don't make an offer to find her on Ila's behalf.

**If they…**
- **[…are kind](play:s01-kind-to-ila)** (ask her name, ask about the hum, kneel to her height): she unwraps the oilskin. Hum. Read the box below. She trusts this character from now on.
- **[…are rude, brush her off or demand the Heart:](play:s01-rude-to-ila)** Ila turns away and pockets it. Mother Sallow is suddenly there: "She's a child. You can be a child too, or you can be useful." Sallow gives them one way back: find the eastern-marsh ferry that's been missing a week (that's Session 2).
- **[…try to take it by force or stealth:](play:s01-take-tongue)** first check your session-zero Off-limits list; nothing is ever done to Ila on-screen. A sleight-of-hand attempt is Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) DC 17, and a ferry-folk widow mending nets sees it anyway unless the roll beats 20. If they keep it, the whole Old Shore goes cold on them (no boats, no hymn) until they give it back by hand and apologise. Foresight has this as "They try to take or steal the Heart."
- **…ask her to come with them:** "Mother Sallow says I'm not allowed to be brave yet." She walks them to Sallow's hut.
- **…ignore her completely and head for the Quays:** fine. Run Scene 3, and let Ila find them there: she follows the people "who looked like they heard it."

> **SAY (when Ila shows the Heart):** "She folds back the oilskin. Inside is a lump of dark bronze the size of a fist, shaped like a teardrop, worn smooth. As her fingers close on it, you hear it: a hum, very low, more in your teeth than your ears. Out on the far shore the still water shivers in a ring. Ila says: 'It's warm. Not like a hot pan. Like something alive that's been asleep. Nobody believes me except Mother Sallow, and she only believes me because she's scared.'"

**How it ends.** Mother Sallow walks over from the net-racks once Ila smiles (or once Ila turns her back on someone rude). "You'll want tea. Everyone who's heard it wants tea." Go to Scene 2.

### Scene 2: Tea at Mother Sallow's

**Where and why.** Mother Sallow's reed-hut on the Old Shore, a few minutes later. She invites them because Ila trusted them, or because she wants to find out who they are before the Court does.

> **SAY:** "The hut smells of wet reeds and woodsmoke. Bundles of dyed reed hang from the roof, green and rust and black. A tiny woman with green-stained hands pours tea the colour of river water. It is very bitter. She puts an unlit pipe between her teeth, looks at each of you in turn, and says: 'Well. Sit, then. Nobody gets anything standing up.'"

**What's really going on.** Mother Sallow keeps the ferry ledger: forty years of names the Mere has taken and the Court has Tithed. Her husband, Davin, drowned in the Wrongweather thirty years ago. That winter she sold her memory of their wedding day to the Tithe, in grief, and she has been losing him ever since. She can't picture his face. She knows the Drowned Choir are her ancestors and won't say so. She is frightened for Ila, and she needs strangers, because the Court watches the ferry-folk.

**Mother Sallow** (seventies, Druid).
- *Voice:* wry, short sentences, half-invented proverbs. Dry as reed.
- *Mannerism:* chews her unlit pipe; taps it on the table before she says something important.
- *Wants in this scene:* to know if they're honest, and to get the Heart kept away from the Court for three days while she thinks.
- *First words:* "Sit, then. Nobody gets anything standing up."
- *Sample lines:* "The Mere keeps what you give it. That's the trouble with the Mere." · "Charm's for people who want something. What do you want?" · "My husband's name is on a stone on the shore. I can read it. I can't see his face. Make of that what you like."

**What they can learn here** (she answers honestly, but not fully):
- **The Mere holds the dead, and the Heart belongs to Ossa, the Bone Bell,** which hangs drowned in the old town under the water. Free.
- **The Drowned Choir sings at night.** You sing back, and they're quiet for an hour. She teaches the response hymn: free, a few minutes, no roll.
- **The Court collects memories as tax (the Tithe).** Hushman Quill is short on his quota and will come. Free.
- **Her husband Davin's name is on the Mere Memory Shore,** and she can't remember his face. Free if anyone asks about her family. A Search check, Wisdom (Insight) DC 14, notices she's hiding something bigger (her ancestors are the Choir). Don't reveal that; let her say, "Some things you're told when you've earned them."

**DO:** Ask each player, "What do you want from Mother Sallow?" Let each one ask a question. Answer every one, honestly and briefly, in her voice.

> **SAY (her offer):** "Keep it safe for three days. Not from thieves. From the Court. Do that, and I'll tell you what the water knows. And learn this before the dark comes: when they sing, you sing back."

**If they…**
- **[…accept the three days:](play:s01-sallow-bargain)** she nods once and pours more tea. Record the bargain in Foresight → Party & ledger → Bargain log ("Keep the Heart from the Court for three days"). Session 3 rewards it: she opens the ledger without being asked.
- **…try to charm her:** it slides off. Her attitude stays Indifferent. Blunt honesty, especially about something they've lost, makes her Friendly.
- **…offer to take the Heart themselves:** "It's Ila's to give." If Ila trusts them, Ila gives it. That's a real choice; let it happen.
- **…ask for payment:** "Gold? I've got some, and it buys nothing I need. Find the eastern ferry and I'll pay you for that." (That's Session 2's hook.)
- **[…want to see the Memory Shore:](play:s01-memory-shore)** she walks them there. Read: "Stones smoothed by four centuries of water, each scratched with a name. The water whispers them back." Davin Sallow's stone is near the edge. If you like, one stone has a player character's name on it, dated ten years from now. Mother Sallow only says, "The Mere remembers what hasn't happened yet." Use it as foreshadowing; nothing else has to follow.

**How it ends.** The party leaves with the hymn, a promise, and either the Heart or Ila's trust. Ask: "The afternoon's yours. What do you do?" Most tables go to the port (Scene 3). If they want to stay on the Old Shore, skip to Scene 4.

### Scene 3: The port (optional)

**Where and why.** Pellmoor Landing proper: the Quays, the Tally market and Strangers' Row. Characters go because they want to sell, buy, ask questions, find a drink, or sort out papers. Run as much or as little as they want. It's the best place for each character to have a spotlight moment.

> **SAY:** "Back past the last warehouse the noise hits you like warm water. Gnomes shout prices. A Baldurian sailor sings. On the Tally, a slate outside a grey stone house reads: ONE KEEP, ONE GOLD TWO SILVER. MOTES AT ONE SILVER. HEARTKEEPS BY APPRAISAL. A woman in a grey apron stands in the doorway with three tuning forks in her pocket, and the people queuing past her are selling what they remember."

**What's really going on.** Memory is money here. A Reckoner draws a memory out with a tuning fork, with consent, and seals it in a little vial: a **mote** (a passing memory, about a silver piece), a **keep** (a memory with feeling, about a gold piece) or a **heartkeep** (a defining memory, 25 to 100 gold or more). Foreign gold is winning, which is why the Court's memory tax runs short. Master Venn of the Vennmark Coster exports bottled memories to rich buyers across the sea, and he pays Quill to look away from some of his cargoes.

**People they can meet** (each one is a short scene; use one or two, not all):
- **Reckoner Sela Maunt** (Corvanese, forties) at the Reckoning House. *Voice:* precise, kind, tired. *Mannerism:* taps a tuning fork on the counter when she thinks. *Wants:* honest weights and a quiet life. *First words:* "Buying or selling, love? Selling's the queue on the left." *Lines:* "You can sell the day you learned to swim. Never the swimming." · "That's not a memory. That's a bell. Put it away." If shown the Heart, her fork goes silent mid-note and she refuses to touch it.
- **Master Orsolo Venn** (Amnian, fifties) two stalls from Sela, in plum velvet with a silent pocket-clock. *Voice:* warm, fluent, calls everyone "friend." *Mannerism:* checks the clock before every offer. *Wants:* the humming Heart, the moment he hears of it. *First words:* "Friend! You have the look of someone holding something interesting." *Lines:* "Six hundred gold and a berth home on the *Gilded Heron*. Think about it. Three days." · "I only ever help people sell what they don't need." If he hears the Heart, he tips off Quill, who comes on the first evening instead of the second.
- **Pilot-Mistress Brannagh Wick** (ferry-folk, about sixty) at the Pilots' Hall. *Voice:* gruff; calls every ship "she" and every captain "lad." *Mannerism:* counts tides out loud. *Wants:* the Hall free of the Court, and Ila safe. *First words:* "Need a pilot, lad, or just lost?" *Lines:* "Tam Harrow was mine. Best pilot I had." · "Every ship on this water goes where my people take her." She won't say more about Tam yet (that's Session 2), but she'll lend a skiff to anyone Ila trusts.
- **[Sister Aurelie Dawnmantle](play:s01-dawn-mission)** (Waterdhavian, thirties) at the Dawn Mission on Strangers' Row. *Voice:* bright, earnest. *Mannerism:* says "when the sun comes back" like a date in a diary. *Wants:* a writ to go to Hallowmere. *First words:* "When the sun comes back, I'd like to be standing somewhere it can see me." She heals anyone who comes in, free.
- **[A Harper with a lute](play:s01-brass-lantern)** at the Brass Lantern. She asks, lightly, "What do you make of the Tithe? Asking for a friend who's lost his mother's face." Insight DC 14 notices she's working. Kindness about the Old Shore earns a silver brooch and a cellar address.
- **[The Landing House clerk](play:s01-stormborn-writ)** stamps foreigners' papers: "Landing due, five gold. Pellmoor and the Mere road only. Anywhere else needs a writ from Hallowmere. They take about a season. Next." A Faerûnian character can't legally go inland. Don't block the story: the Court's letter at the end of Session 3 works as a writ. Foresight has plays for buying a true writ from Quill or a forged one on Strangers' Row.

**What they can learn here:**
- **How memory money works,** from Sela. Free. Players can sell a memory for coin if they like; the player always chooses the memory and writes one line about it in the Memory Ledger. You never choose it.
- **The Heart is valuable and dangerous,** from Sela's silent fork or Venn's offer.
- **[Tam went out the night he died with no cargo.](play:s02-ask-brannagh)** Brannagh lets that slip if Ila vouches for them; otherwise she says it in Session 2.

**If they…**
- **[…try to sell the Heart now:](play:s01-tongue-to-the-quays)** Sela refuses; Venn offers 600 gp and gives them three days. Don't let the sale happen tonight; he wants a contract, a witness and a pilot. The real choice is in Session 3.
- **…start a fight on the Quays:** foreign captains don't interfere with the Court. Six Hushmen sit in the Landing House. Telegraph them: boots on the boards, a door opening. Give the party a round to walk away.
- **…split up:** let them. Cut between them every few minutes. Bring everyone back together for Scene 4.

**How it ends.** The light goes grey. Lamps come on along the Quays. On the Old Shore, someone is walking from house to house with a ledger. Go to Scene 4.

### Scene 4: Quill comes for the quota

**Where and why.** The Old Shore, on the second evening (the first evening if Venn tipped him off). Quill walks over from the Landing House to collect the village's memory quota and inspect for "unlicensed bell-work." The party is there because the Heart is, or because Ila came to find them when she saw grey gloves.

> **SAY:** "He walks along the Old Shore with a lantern in one grey-gloved hand and a ledger in the other, knocking on doors. A broad Hushman follows him carrying a box of little vials. At the far end of the lane, by a handcart, three more grey coats wait under a lamp. The man with the ledger reaches you, smiles a sharp, careful smile, and says: 'Good evening. I'm Tobias Quill, of the Quiet Court's Office of Tithes. I'll be brief, since I'm sure you're busy.'"

**What's really going on.** Quill's quota is short every season because the Old Shore offers him gold, and gold can't pay a Corvanese subject's Tithe. Someone has told him (or Venn has) that something here is humming. He has been Tithed himself and doesn't remember his sister. He wants his quota and a promotion. He is pushy, not murderous, and he takes bribes.

**Hushman Tobias Quill** (Hushman, CR 1/2).
- *Voice:* polite, bureaucratic, unhurried. Always has a form.
- *Mannerism:* writes in his ledger without looking down while he talks to you.
- *Wants in this scene:* three memories for his quota, and the humming thing, if it exists.
- *First words:* "Good evening. I'm Tobias Quill, of the Quiet Court's Office of Tithes."
- *Sample lines:* "Pellmoor is short on its quota this season. Several villagers have offered me gold, which I can't put on a shelf." · "And I'm told someone here has been listening to something they shouldn't. Would any of you know anything about that?" · "Where did the child's coat go?"

**What they can learn here:**
- **The Court is hunting the Heart,** and Quill knows roughly where it is. Free.
- **Quill can be bought.** A Search check, Wisdom (Insight) DC 12: his eyes keep going to their purses. Free if anyone offers money.
- **The three households he's hounding** (choose names from the list) want to pay in gold, which is illegal for locals. Paying a local's tax in gold is called **gilding**: common, and a crime.

**If they…**
- **[…hand over the Heart:](play:s01-hand-to-quill)** Quill takes it in both gloved hands and the hum stops dead. He writes a receipt, records their faces, and leaves. Ila walks to the end of the old dock and sits there till dark. Don't punish the choice; Sessions 3 and 4 have ways back.
- **[…lie to him:](play:s01-lie-to-quill)** he nods, then asks his one question and waits: "Where did the child's coat go?" (Ila wears her father's coat; the oilskin bundle was in its pocket.) If the lie needs a roll, it's Charisma (Deception) DC 16. If it fails, he says, "Then I'll look myself," and the Hushmen search the Old Shore tonight (Scene 5). He finds the Heart unless it has been moved off the Old Shore: a ship or a Quays warehouse is outside his warrant.
- **[…bribe him:](play:s01-bribe-quill)** no roll. He pockets the gold and asks one favour: "A name." If they give Mother Sallow's, he writes it down. If they refuse, he keeps the coin and says, "Then I'll remember you were generous."
- **[…give memories of their own for the quota:](play:s01-fill-the-quota)** the honest way out. Quill draws each one with a fork in about a minute. The player chooses the memory and its weight. The three households will never forget it. Give **Heroic Inspiration** to each character who gives a memory freely.
- **[…gild the quota with gold:](play:s01-gild-the-quota)** Sela Maunt can stamp stand-in motes (about 40 gp). Quill must look away: no roll if bribed, otherwise Influence DC 15. Foresight has this play.
- **[…attack him:](play:s01-attack-quill)** see the fight below. Quill steps back so neatly it looks rehearsed: "You've made yourselves a Tithe-case. I do hope you understand what that means."
- **[…use magic on him:](play:any-l3-hold-suggest-charm)** every spell in Corvane hums faintly as it's cast. Hold Person or Suggestion works on a failed Wisdom save, but his next report names the caster. Detect Thoughts shows a tired man adding numbers.

**How it ends.** Quill leaves: satisfied, suspicious, bribed, wounded, or with the Heart. Note which in Foresight (it decides tonight's raid). Go to Scene 5.

### Scene 5: Night on the Old Shore

**Where and why.** The same night. Either the Hushmen come back for the Heart, or the Drowned Choir sings for the first time. Run the raid only if Quill left **suspicious** or the party made themselves a **Tithe-case**. Otherwise skip straight to the song.

- **[The raid.](play:s01-defend-the-raid)** The Harrow house, after midnight. A Sergeant and three Hushmen come quietly by boat to search for the Heart and Tam's papers.

> **SAY:** "No knock. Just the door, opening very slowly, and two grey gloves catching the lamplight. Somewhere outside, oars dip in the water without a splash."

**What's really going on.** They want the Heart, not a fight, and they don't want the Old Shore to wake. Ila is the one thing that must never come to harm on-screen. If the players hide her, she's hidden. If she's taken, it happens Off-screen and Foresight's play "The raid takes Ila" handles it: she is held safe in a lamp-lit room at the Landing House, and the party gets a rescue chance on the Mere road in Session 2.

**Hiding Ila:** under the net-racks or at the Pilots' Hall. The character hiding her makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 14; ferry-folk neighbours take the Help action without being asked, so the roll has Advantage. Even on a failure, give the choice a cost rather than a loss: the Sergeant sees the net-racks move and has to choose between Ila and the Heart, and he chooses the Heart.

**The song** (every night, raid or not). Read this as the session's last words:

> **SAY:** "The Mere is perfectly still. Fog lies on it like a sheet. Then, from somewhere under the water, a woman begins to sing. It isn't loud. Every word is a name. Along the Old Shore, doors open and the ferry-folk step out and hum back, softly, as if they'd been waiting all their lives to answer. You hear your own name. Then a name you don't know. Then yours again."

**How it ends.** Stop there. Ask the closing question: **"Who was singing?"**

### Running the fights (Session 1)

Both fights are written for four level-3 characters. Foresight shows the 2024 difficulty for your actual party and suggests a retune. The Hushmen want arrests, not bodies.

**Quill's patrol** (Quill and four Hushmen, all Hushman stat block: AC 12, 32 HP, Bloodied at 16).
- **Setup:** Quill and one Hushman stand in front of the party. Three more wait at the far end of the lane by the handcart, about 60 feet away. They come running at the first shout and arrive at the start of round 2. Show their lantern bobbing as they come.
- **Round 1:** Quill steps back and shouts for witnesses. His guard uses **Arrest** on whoever holds the Heart: a Strength saving throw, DC 12. On a failure that character is Grappled (escape DC 12) and can't cast spells with a Verbal component while held.
- **Round 2 onward:** two Hushmen stand shoulder to shoulder in the narrowest gap between the houses (they get Advantage from **Pack Tactics** when an ally is next to their target). Two go round through the net-racks to flank. They use the **Silence-Glove Strike** (+4 to hit, 1d6 + 2 bludgeoning plus 1d4 necrotic) and keep trying Arrest on the Heart-holder. Quill hangs back and fires his heavy crossbow only if nobody is watching.
- **Describe hits:** "The glove lands with no sound at all, and the cold goes into your arm like river water." **Misses:** "His fist goes past your ear and you hear nothing, not even the air." Silence is the creepy part; lean on it.
- **Retreat:** a Hushman retreats when Bloodied unless a Sergeant is watching. Quill backs off toward the Landing House as soon as his first guard is Bloodied, or as soon as the ferry-folk come out of their doors (two rounds of noise). They don't follow anyone onto the water.
- **Scale on the fly:** if the party is struggling by round 2, the last two Hushmen arrive late or not at all ("They're stuck at the lane, shouting"). If the party is crushing it, a fifth Hushman joins from the Quays. If someone drops Quill with Hold Person in round 1, let it work: that's a great moment. Note the caster's name will be in his report.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** a character at 0 HP is bound and dragged to the handcart, not killed; Quill offers them back for the Heart. A Hushman reduced to 0 HP by a melee attack can be knocked out instead of killed (the attacker chooses). Killing Quill is a big choice: ask once, plainly, "You mean to kill him?" If yes, the Old Shore is closed by Hushmen within the hour and the Court sends a Sergeant with a warrant.

**The night raid** (a Hushman Sergeant and three Hushmen).
- **Setup:** one Hushman waits in the boat under the window. Two come in through the door and start searching. The Sergeant stands in the doorway.
- **Each round:** the two searchers keep searching unless someone blocks them; then they fight with Pack Tactics and Arrest. The Sergeant attacks twice (Silence-Glove Strike or hand crossbow, +4) and can swap one attack for **Command**: one Hushman takes a reaction to make an attack with +1d4. When he's hit in melee he can **Parry** (+2 AC against that hit).
- **Describe the room:** the lamp, the shutters, the net-racks outside, the cold water under the window. A character can shove someone out of the window into the Mere: Hushmen hate the water.
- **Retreat:** they leave when one of them drops, or when the Old Shore wakes (two rounds of noise: shouting, a pot banged, the hymn sung). They never chase anyone onto the water.
- **Scale on the fly:** three players or a struggling party: no Sergeant; the boat-guard comes in instead. Five players: Quill waits outside and runs at the first blow.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** as above. If they find the Heart, they leave with it, and Session 3 still has ways to get it back.

### If you get stuck (Session 1)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **The Heart hums.** Out of nowhere, in someone's pocket or Ila's hands. Ask, "What does your character do?"

3. **Ask a player a question.** "What did you lose, that you'd want back?" "Who does Ila remind you of?" Then let them act on the answer.
