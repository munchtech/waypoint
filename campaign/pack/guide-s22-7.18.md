## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. Two halves. In the first, they find out what all of it has been about. In the second, everyone they've ever been kind to comes up the road. It's the session that pays them back, so let each arrival have its own minute.*

### Before you start (Session 22)

**Level:** four characters at level 9.

**Check the world state first.** It's the longest check of the campaign. Take ten minutes over it.
- **Who's marching?** Maudrin, with the Key (the usual). **If the party holds the Key,** she marches to take it back. **If she's dead,** Tristan marches in her place.
- **Which bells are silent?** Open the Forecast. Is the Concord still possible? Be ready to say so in one sentence.
- **Who's available?** Tick each one that's true: **Rook** won the duel and lives · **Kessa** lives, and the oath's **kept** · the **lamp's** with Yenna · **Corin's** an ally · **Ossa** rang · **the Stilled** trust them · **the Blanks** were helped · **Ilam's** an ally · **Stormborn friends** · **the Cult** has ceased.
- **Isolde:** where is she? **Ansel:** alive?
- **Hearts the Court holds.** Make a list. They can be won back at the Strike.
- **The Librarian:** did they rob the courier?

**What this session is about.** The truth, and company. They learn what every ending costs. Then they find out they won't be alone for it.

**Your goals tonight:**
- Read Edda's page, and have them say in their own words what the Key is.
- Let them decide who else deserves to know.
- Run one ally at a time. A face, a line, a job. One player chooses, each time.
- End on the question: **"Who will you bring to the Tor?"** Then stop.

**Prepare:**
- **Edda's page,** copied out on old-looking paper. Give it to a player to read.
- **An ally card for each company that's coming:** a name, one line, one objective. Blank ones too.
- **The allies clock,** six segments, drawn big.
- **Tokens or minis,** only if they'll ambush: four Wardens; two Sergeants; three Knights; eight Hushmen.
- **A map** of the road, with Hallowmere at the bottom, the Tor at the top, and days marked along it.

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **Study action:** an Intelligence check to remember or work something out. Here it's DC 13, and only for a bonus. **The main truth needs no roll.**
- **Surprise:** a creature that's surprised has Disadvantage on its Initiative roll. That's all it does now. To get it: group Stealth, DC 14.
- **A group check:** everyone rolls, and if at least half succeed, the group does.
- **Scrying:** the target makes a Wisdom save against the caster's spell DC. Succeed, and you see nothing for a day.
- **Influence:** Willing, Hesitant or Unwilling. Only Hesitant rolls.
- **Allies take objectives.** Each company does **one** thing: hold the ridge, cut the retreat, break the line. You never track their Hit Points, and they never kill anyone's foe for them. **+1 on the allies clock each, up to 6.** At **three,** the party picks which flank to hold. At **six,** the Court can't hold the Tor road.

### Opening recap (Session 22)

> **SAY:** "You *(rang / turned away from)* the last bell before the Seventh. A patrol came for you, and *(what happened)*. And then a rider fell off her horse at your feet and said a thing nobody's said in thirty-eight years: Maudrin Vesk has left Hallowmere. You said you'd meet her *(where)*. It's dusk. There's a barn at a crossroads, and somebody's lit a lamp in it."

- **Then hand it over:** "Who's the first person your character thinks of, when they hear she's coming?" One name each. **Write them down. They're your allies, or your stakes.**

### Scene 1: Edda's page

**Where and why.** The barn, one lamp. Everything they've learned, put together. No roll. Don't rush it.

**Where does the page come from?** Whichever fits: **Corin's Notes; the diary** they took in Session 5; or the **Librarian,** who'll read it out for one true memory (not if they robbed the courier). If none of those works, the courier finds them anyway, or Corin sends a copy.

**Give the page to a player and have them read it:**

> **SAY (Edda Thorne):** "He wants to tune the seven into one long spring. I told him the seventh is not a bell. It is the lullaby. The Key is not a tuning tool. It is the heart that sings Oum to sleep, and he has taken it to make the calendar behave. If he strikes the Seventh with it, the dream will wake or it will crack. I told him so. He did not hear me."

**What's really going on.** [This is the truth of the campaign.](play:s22-read-notes) Ask them, one piece at a time, and let them get there:
1. **"What's the Seventh for?"** It keeps Oum asleep.
2. **"So what's the Key?"** It's the Seventh's Heart. The only one that can ring it.
3. **"So what happened on the Quiet Night?"** Imber struck the Key against the bells. The wrong note cracked the lullaby, and the bells went silent because the dream was waking.
4. **"Who's known, all this time?"** Maudrin. Since the day she took it.

**If they…**
- **Ask whether to ring it:** say plainly, out of character, what each ending costs as far as they know it. Nothing here's a trick.
- **Want more:** Study, Intelligence (History or Arcana), DC 13. One line in the margin: Thrane's weather "will go to the rim and wait". Somebody may connect it to the Stormwall.
- **Are angry with Imber:** they've met him, or they might. Edda wasn't angry. She'd underlined *he did not hear me* once.
- **Are angry with Maudrin:** she's kept a lullaby going for thirty-eight years with other people's memories. Both halves of that are true.
- **Have Corin here:** he's read that page a hundred times. He goes very quiet, and then: [he offers to pay the price himself.](play:s22-corin-strike)

> **SAY (Corin):** "If the price is a name, then let it be mine. My grandmother died one breath into this song. Let me finish it."

  Let them argue. He's wrong to want it for his family's sake, and he isn't evil. He won't push. It's still each of theirs to offer, or not.

**Bring it back.** Somebody's humming the lullaby under their breath. It's the player's choice who. They don't notice until it's pointed out.

**How it ends.** Ask: "Who else ought to know this?"

### Scene 2: The people who should know

**Where and why.** Short scenes, one or two of them, wherever they choose. The truth changes people.

**Run the ones they name:**
- **[Kessa.](play:s22-tell-kessa)** He's been living on the idea that the Key would cure him.

> **SAY:** "Kessa laughs, and then stops laughing, and looks at his blackened hands for a long time. 'Well. Then I'd better die somewhere with weather.'"

  He isn't fated to die. It's his choice and the table's. If they've kept their word, he's still with them.
- **[Isolde.](play:s22-tell-isolde)** Her coach is drawn up in a lane a mile off with the blinds down. She's been waiting for somebody to make it easy.

> **SAY:** "Isolde Drave sits very still. 'So the Court has been keeping the lullaby, not the peace. What a waste.'"

  She'll change sides if it costs her nothing. If the Court's got the Frost Heart, [she's got it with her:](play:s22-isolde-returns-frost-tongue)

> **SAY:** "Isolde hands over a velvet bundle, as if it were a gift to a lover. 'You did not hear it from me.'"

- **[Corin, if he's refused or against them:](play:s22-corin-comes-round)** he's sitting by the road.

> **SAY:** "Corin sits on the road with his back against a milestone. 'All this time,' he says, 'I thought it was a bell-name.'"

  Tell him the truth and don't mock his family. Persuasion DC 15 if they fought him; no roll if they give him the Quiet Night page.
- **Haldric,** if he's the sort they write letters to. Keep that truth for the summit; it's worth more there.
- **Nobody:** that's allowed too. Ask why.

**Bring it back.** Each person, when they hear it, says the same thing first. "Who else knows?"

**How it ends.** Hooves. A mule, in fact. It's a thin man with ink on his cuffs.

### Scene 3: The column

**Where and why.** The barn again, and a map. What's coming, and why now.

**First, the news.** Use the one that fits:

- **[Maudrin has the Key:](play:s22-maudrin-moves)**

> **SAY:** "The scout is out of breath and still won't sit down. 'She's left the city. Hushmen, Dawn Order knights, wagons. She's wearing it on a chain around her neck. A grey case.'"

- **[The party has the Key:](play:s22-maudrin-marches-to-retake)**

> **SAY:** "The scout is out of breath. 'She's left the city. No case on the chain, this time. She's wearing her gloves and nothing else.'"

- **[Maudrin's dead:](play:s22-tristan-takes-the-march)**

> **SAY:** "The man in dove-grey is not a woman, and he is not alone: Wardens, a hundred Hushmen, Dawn Order knights."

(If Rook won the duel, only Haldric's own few knights ride with the Court, or none.)

**Then Ansel.** Stooped, ink-stained cuffs, spectacles he wipes when he's nervous. *Lines:* "I counted that shelf every morning for thirty-eight years. I've just dusted it. There's nothing on it." · "I thought if I kept the books straight, it'd be all right. I'm so sorry." · "Ask me about roads. I do know about roads." (If he's dead, Linnet brings his route-book.)

[If they ask about the vault:](play:s22-key-leaves-vault)

> **SAY:** "The palace vault stands open and empty. Ansel Quay is dusting the shelf where the case used to sit."

(He tells it himself, as the last thing he saw there. There's nothing left in Hallowmere to rob but the Ledger.)

**What's really going on.** [Why now?](play:s22-why-maudrin-marches) He asks it himself:

> **SAY (Ansel):** "She has had it thirty-eight years," *(quietly)*. "She never took it out of the vault. Why would she take it out now?"

Insight DC 15 off him or Isolde, or let a player guess: the Key's hum used to hold the lullaby steady from the palace. Every bell they've rung has shifted the world's key. From that far away it doesn't hold any more. **She has to carry it to the Tor to keep things as they are.** She wants the unrung Hearts, the Key, and the Ringer, Tithed, so nobody can ever strike it. She means to seal the stairs.

**If they…**
- **[Scry on her:](play:s22-scry-the-column)** she's got Advantage on the save only if she knows she's being watched.

> **SAY:** "In the bowl of water a column winds up a road, grey horses, grey gloves, and one rider in dove-grey at the centre."

- **Ask what she's like, on the road:** Ansel: "She folds her gloves before she eats. She asked the cook his daughter's name, and wrote it down."
- **Want to steal the Key on the march:** it's on her, in a lead case, in the middle of a hundred people. They'll get their chance at the Strike. Say so.
- **Ask how long:** four days from Hallowmere. She left yesterday.

**Bring it back.** Ansel counts them. He can't help it. He gets to the Ringer and starts again from one.

**How it ends.** Linnet, from the door: "There's somebody on the road. There's *several* somebodies."

### Scene 4: Who comes

**Where and why.** The heart of the night. An hour. [They called, and people came.](play:s22-gather-allies)

> **SAY:** "Word goes out along every road you have travelled, and some of the people you helped start walking toward the Tor."

**How to run it.** Go round the table. Each player picks **one** company to meet, and they're the one who walks out to the road. Read the line. The player says one thing to them. They get a job. **Tick the clock.** Then the next player. Only run the ones that are true. Debts from Act I come up the road too, when they're true: [Venn's carts for the Court](play:s22-venn-ships-for-court), [the released Choir](play:s22-ally-choir) and [Tobias Quill, out of uniform](play:s22-ally-quill).

**The lines, to read one at a time:**

> **SAY (Rook):** "Captain Dessa Rook arrives at dusk with forty knights, mud to the thigh."

> **SAY (Ironhorn):** "The earth shakes before you see them: Kessa on a brown horse, bronze wire glinting, a thousand riders behind."

> **SAY (Yenna):** "Yenna rides in at the head of fifty riders, her father's lamp tied to her saddle, humming."

> **SAY (Bellwrights):** "Corin comes up the road with a cart of half-finished bells and four Heart-Hounds that heel to him."

> **SAY (the hymn):** "The courier hands you a stoppered vial of Mere-water that hums, faintly, a hymn you almost know."

> **SAY (Stilled):** "Down the road come glass figures in a slow line, frost on their shoulders."

> **SAY (Blanks):** "There are hundreds of them on the road, quiet, in grey coats, and every one of them looks at you as if they were trying to remember your name."

> **SAY (Ilam):** "Ilam stands in the road with his gloves off. Behind him, far down the hill, a woman holds his son's hand."

> **SAY (over the sea):** "The rider is not Corvanese. He wears a coat of a cut you have not seen since Pellmoor, and he carries a harp."

> **SAY (Cult):** "The Leader walks out of the dark with twenty robed figures, humming very softly."

**If they…**
- **Ask why they came:** one true sentence, about something the party did. Look it up. It matters more than the number.
- **Have Rook here, and she's got the armory's Hearts:** [she gives them back.](play:s22-rook-returns-the-tongues)

> **SAY:** "Rook unwraps a bundle of oilcloth. 'These were never the Order's to keep.'"

- **Want the children to come:** no. Jonah's kept well away, with somebody kind. So's Pip. So's Ila, unless her guardian says otherwise and the Ringer wants her near; she doesn't fight.
- **Have hardly anyone:** then make the one who did come count. "It's just me," says Linnet. "Is that all right?"
- **Have all six:** the Court can't hold the road. Maudrin has to come in person.

**Bring it back.** At every arrival, Ansel writes a name in his book and gets it right.

**How it ends.** The barn's full. Somebody's started soup.

### Scene 5: The ridge

**Where and why.** The next morning, above the road. One decision: hit the column here, go back for a bell, or go on to the Tor.

> **SAY:** "From the ridge you can see the column coming: grey horses, grey gloves, and one rider in dove-grey at the centre."

**If they…**
- **[Ambush it:](play:s22-ambush)** Ansel knows exactly where the road narrows. Group Stealth, DC 14. Run the fight below. It's the vanguard, not her. Whatever they break here won't be at the Strike.
- **[Go back for a missed bell:](play:s22-detour-for-a-missed-bell)** you can. Three to six days; Maudrin gets to the Tor first; the Court Clock goes up 2. Say it before they choose.

> **SAY:** "The map shows Pellmoor six days south and the Tor two days north. The scout says Maudrin is coming now."

- **Go on to the Tor:** they'll be there first, with whoever came. That's Session 23.
- **Watch her:** Perception, no roll. She's riding very upright. She looks at the ridge, exactly where they are, for as long as it takes to breathe in. Then she looks at the road.
- **Want to talk to her:** she's not stopping. At the Tor, she will.

**Bring it back.** The humming. It's in the column too. One of the Hushmen's got it, and the Sergeant can't work out which.

**How it ends.** Ask: **"Who will you bring to the Tor?"** Each player names one. Then ask what they'd want that person to do if it went wrong.

### Running the fights (Session 22)

#### The column's vanguard

*Four Wardens (AC 16, 82 HP), two Sergeants (AC 15, 52 HP), three Dawn Order Knights (AC 18, 52 HP), eight Hushmen (AC 12, 32 HP). Only if they ambush. If Rook won the duel, the Knights are Haldric's holdouts; leave them out if you like.*

- **Setup:** a sunken road between gorse banks, 15 feet wide, with an old cart bridge at one end. The vanguard's strung out along it in a line. The party chooses where they're standing.
- **With Surprise:** everyone down there rolls Initiative at Disadvantage.
- **What happens:** the Wardens stop and hold the road where they are, so the rest of the column can come up. The Knights look for a way up the banks. The Hushmen go round both ends.
- **How long they've got:** five rounds. Then the column arrives, and they must be gone. **Count it out loud.**
- **A Warden's turn:** two blows (+5, 12, pushed 10 feet), or two crossbow shots (+4, 13). Stone: no spells within 10 feet; AC 12, 10 HP; or Sleight of Hand DC 15.
- **A Knight's turn:** two swords (+5, 10 and 4 Radiant each).
- **A Sergeant's turn:** two blows or shots (+4; about 5, and 3 Necrotic with the glove), or one and a signal: an ally attacks with a d4 extra.
- **A Hushman's turn:** a glove (+5, 5 and 2 Necrotic), or an arrest (Strength DC 12 or Grappled).
- **Who to worry about:** whoever goes down into the road. Getting out takes a climb, or a friend.
- **When it ends:** three Wardens down; or the fifth round; or the party holds the Key (if it's somehow here).
- **If it goes well:** take two Wardens and four Hushmen off the Strike next session. Say so.
- **Scale on the fly:** leave out a Warden and the Knights for a lighter fight.
- **Non-lethal:** they arrest. So might the party.

### If you get stuck (Session 22)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **Ansel takes off his spectacles** and says what he saw her do the night she left.
2. **Somebody arrives whom nobody called.** One of the names from the recap.
3. **The Key hums,** wherever it is, and every bit of bronze in the barn answers.

**If they don't follow the lore:** say it in two lines. "The Seventh's a lullaby. The Key's the only thing that can play it, and it's always been the wrong hands."

**If nobody's available:** it's fine. Drop the Strike to its lighter size.

**Names for knights, riders and Blanks** (cross one off when you use it): Ser Aldis, Merrow, Gorse, Thessaly, Corporal Sabel, the Blank they call Monday.
