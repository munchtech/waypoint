## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. The only living witness to the Quiet Night tells them what happened, in his own words, and gives them the Frost Heart. They may free him, leave him, fight him or kill him. All four work. They reach level 6 when his story is settled.*

### Before you start (Session 13)

**Level:** four characters at level 5. They reach level 6 at the end, once the Sexton is resolved, however that goes.

**Check the world state first.** Five facts change tonight:
- **Is he Friendly?** If they gave him a patient hour, the confession starts with no roll. If he's Hostile, start with an apology (Influence DC 15).
- **Did someone agree with the sermon?** He speaks to that character first, and offers them the Heart.
- **Edda's diary:** if they have it, Scene 1 has its best version.
- **What did they tell him about the Court?** A lie has to be put right before he'll talk about the Key.
- **Isolde:** at Yara's camp, or a day out. She doesn't come into the chapel.

**What this session is about.** Imber Vesk ordered Edda Thorne to tune the bells into one long spring. She refused and wrote it down. He tuned them himself, with the Key, and the bells cracked. He has been sorry ever since. Tonight he says so to somebody. Then he asks for one thing: take me out of here, and let me finish my sermon somewhere it can be heard.

**Your goals tonight:**
- Read the confession slowly, and stop in the middle for them to ask things.
- Tell them the truth about the Key plainly, if they tell him who holds it, and let them plan with it.
- However the scene goes, **the Frost Heart leaves with the party.** Every route ends that way.
- If they fight, he's a man in pain, and the fight stops the moment they stop.
- End on his warning: "Don't ring the Frost Spire without the Heart. The winter will come back all at once." Then ask: "Who will carry it up?"

**Prepare:**
- **Tokens or minis:** the four characters; the Glass Sexton; Imber (an old man, if you have one); two Stilled; Yara; Maren.
- **Maps:** the chapel from last session. Mark the pulpit, the aisle and the pews.
- **Props:**
  - *The warm cloak.* Put a real scarf on the table. Whoever picks it up gives Imber theirs.
  - *The confession,* printed large enough to read without looking down too much.
  - *A jug of cold water,* if your table likes that sort of thing. One sip each when the shell breaks.

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **Freeing him:** Strength DC 15, or 20 Fire or Lightning damage aimed at the glass over the whole scene. Two or three Fire Bolts, one Burning Hands, or Produce Flame held close for a minute. Yara's saw does it in ten minutes with no roll. Thunder (Shatter, Thunderwave) breaks it and hurts him.
- **Once he's out,** he's an old man: Commoner, 4 hit points, unarmed. The cold counts for him too: Constitution DC 10 each hour, unless he has a cloak.
- **Spells:** Detect Thoughts works (a lullaby, and the name Cassia). Zone of Truth works and finds nothing hidden. Dispel Magic does nothing: a winter-moment isn't a spell. Counterspell does nothing to his Sermon or Icicle Volley.
- **The sermon in a fight:** Wisdom DC 15 or Charmed, with Speed 0, until the end of the character's next turn. Calm Emotions suppresses Charmed. Roll the save every time he uses it.
- **Shattering:** when his ice breaks, everyone within 15 feet makes a Dexterity save, DC 15, for 4d6 slashing (half on a success).
- **The Frost Heart** falls when his broken-form ends. Free to grab if you're next to it; otherwise Dexterity (Acrobatics) DC 12 from up to 10 feet.

### Opening recap (Session 13)

> **SAY:** "Last time, you went into the chapel and a man in a pulpit made of glass asked you who rules the Court. *(Say what they told him.)* You led three carters out by the hand. *(If they did.)* He says he has one thing left to say. He's said it to the frost every night for four hundred years. He asked whether you'd listen. *(Say what they answered.)*"

- **If they said no:** "The candles are still burning. He's still there. He hasn't started again."
- **Then hand it over:** "Where are you sitting?" Let them choose a pew, the aisle, or the door.

### Scene 1: The confession

**Where and why.** The front of the chapel. They're listening.

> **SAY:** "He takes a breath. You didn't know he breathed. 'I shall not preach it,' he says. 'I shall only say it.'"

**What's really going on.** [He tells it plainly.](play:s13-listen-confession) He isn't bargaining. If they read Edda's diary to him, he'll cry.

> **SAY:** "'I ordered her to tune them into one long spring. Edda refused, and she wrote it down for me, so that I could never say I did not know. I tuned them anyway. The Key sang against the bells, and the bells cracked on the discord. I have been sorry for four hundred years.'"

**Read it in three pieces, and stop after each:**
1. *The order.* "There were seven bells, one for each part of the year. I wanted one. No winter. No harvest to fail. I thought: how can it be wrong, to want spring?" **Stop. Let them ask.**
2. *The warning.* "She came to my door at night. She said a bell that is asked to sing only one note goes mad. She said it twice, so that I would hear it. Then she wrote it down and gave me the page. I remember thinking how neat her hand was." **Stop.**
3. *The ringing.* "I put the Key to the first bell and it sang, beautifully, and the others leaned to listen. And then they all sang *against* it. Every bell in the world at once, and none of them the same. And then nothing. I thought I had gone deaf. I hadn't. That's what it was, you see. It was quiet." **Stop. Let it be quiet for a moment.**

**If they…**
- **[Read Edda's diary to him:](play:s13-read-diary-to-imber)** the glass fogs. "She told me," he says. "She wrote it down so I could not say I did not know." He asks for the last page again. Then the crown opens its hand, as it were: the Frost Heart comes out into the Ringer's hand without anyone touching it. No roll, and no need for the rest of the confession.
- **Ask what the Key is:** Scene 2. He won't, until they've told him who holds it.
- **Ask about Cassia:** his daughter. She took the Key home to the Vesks' reliquary the night he was disgraced. "She had my mother's laugh. You won't have heard it. I'd give you the Heart for an hour of it."
- **Get restless:** "Take the crown," he says. "I am too tired to wear it." Move to Scene 3.
- **Argue again:** the glass cracks. **"Do you wish me to go on?"** Then see the fights.
- **Tell him they don't forgive him:** he nods. "No. I shouldn't, in your place. I only wanted it heard."

**The gift.** When he's finished he asks one of them, the one he trusts, to lift the crown from his head. The glass lets go. The Frost Heart comes away in their hand: a bronze clapper, cold enough to burn, singing very softly.

**Bring it back.** In the pews, a Stilled woman who's been kneeling for four hundred years has turned her head towards him. Nobody saw it happen.

**How it ends.** They have the Frost Heart, and he asks them for one more thing: "Take me with you. I should like to finish it somewhere there's an answer."

### Scene 2: The Key

**Where and why.** At any point. The one question he most wants answered is who has it now.

> **SAY:** "He is silent so long you think the winter has taken him. Then: 'The Key passed to my daughter, Cassia, when I was disgraced. It stayed in the family's reliquary. Who holds it now? Tell me, and I will tell you what it is.'"

**What's really going on.** [The Key isn't a tool.](play:s13-ask-about-key) It's the clapper of the Seventh Bell. Maudrin uses it and doesn't fully understand what it was for.

**If they…**
- **Say Maudrin Vesk:** he keeps his word. "It is not a tool. It is the Seventh's tongue. It sings the Dreamer to sleep. I struck the other bells with it, to make the calendar behave. Imagine using a cradle-song to hammer nails." Then he tells them where it's kept: a vault beneath the family chapel, now under the white palace; a lock, and a weighted line tied to the case, so that lifting it rings a bell somewhere else. (In Act IV, if they rob the palace, they find the line without a roll, and have advantage on the DC 16 lock.)
- **Ask who the Dreamer is:** "The one under the hill. I only ever saw its breath."
- **Refuse to tell him:** he says nothing more about it. "Then we'll each keep one thing."
- **Lied to him last session:** he asks again. This time the truth is enough.
- **Don't ask:** near the end, he asks them. "Who has the Key now? Someone must."

**Bring it back.** Ask the character who saw themselves in the glass: "A small dark key. Do you say anything?"

**How it ends.** They know, or they've chosen not to. If they know, *don't* soften it or say it's for later. Let them start planning.

### Scene 3: Out of the glass

**Where and why.** The pulpit. Skip it if he's already dead, or they've decided to leave him.

**If they…**
- **[Free him:](play:s13-free-sexton)** Strength DC 15, or 20 Fire or Lightning damage, or Yara's saw. Read:

> **SAY:** "The glass runs like wax. Inside it there is only an old man in wet ceremonial robes, thin as a rake, shaking so hard his teeth click. 'Oh,' he says. 'It is cold.'"

- If he hasn't handed over the Heart yet, he lifts the crown from his own head and gives it to them now.
- **[Use other spells:](play:s13-spells-on-sexton)** he knows what they're doing and enjoys watching it. "Ah. Tonework. I had almost forgotten the sound." Fire and lightning free him gently. Thunder breaks it and hurts him.
- **[Leave him frozen:](play:s13-leave-him-frozen)** he doesn't argue. The Heart still comes out of the crown when he asks it to. "Then tell me when it rings," he says. "I would like to know when the winter ends, even if I am the last to feel it." Remind them, once, that the Court is a day away. If they leave him, Isolde finds him first.
- **[Kill him:](play:s13-kill-imber)** ask once: "You mean to kill him?" He's unarmed and frail. If they do, he says "I know," and closes his eyes. The glass around the chapel goes dull. The Heart falls from the crown. Say plainly that the only living witness of the Quiet Night is gone. If they hesitate, he asks one thing: finish the sermon for me.

**Bring it back.** The candles, all of them, at once, begin to flicker.

**How it ends.** He's free, or he isn't. Either way the Heart is theirs and the chapel is quieter than it has ever been.

### Scene 4: The old man in the cloak

**Where and why.** The walk back, or the camp. Only if he's free.

> **SAY:** "'It is cold,' Imber says again, wonderingly, as if the word were new. 'I had forgotten it was a thing a person could feel.'"

**What's really going on.** [He needs looking after.](play:s13-escort-imber) A cloak, food, and a slow pace. He wants to see the Spire before anything else.

**Imber, out of the glass.** The long sentences have gone. He speaks in short ones now, and stops, surprised, when they end. *Lines:* "Is that soup? Is that what it smells like?" · "You have very kind hands. Were you a nurse?" · "Nobody asked me to stop. All those years. I'd have stopped." · "I'd like to see it ring. I won't ask to be forgiven. I'd just like to see it." · To Maren: "I know you. You laughed. During the long prayer."

**If they…**
- **Give him a cloak:** whoever gives up theirs makes the cold saves. He tries to give it back.
- **Sit with him:** he asks their names, and repeats each one carefully, and gets them all right afterwards.
- **Ask him to finish the sermon:** not yet. "When there's a bell to say it under."
- **Ask about the Court:** he's four hundred years out of date. But he knows the palace as it was: doors, passages, where the chapel stair comes out.
- **Yara** wants to know who's paying for the soup. She also gives him the good blanket.

**Bring it back.** Imber walks past a glass tree, stops, and puts his hand on it. "I'm sorry," he tells it. It isn't clear whether he means the tree.

**How it ends.** The Spire is above the trees. He stops and looks at it. Then: **"Don't ring the Frost Spire without the Heart. The winter will come back all at once."** Ask them: **"Who will carry it up?"**

### Running the fights (Session 13)

#### The Glass Sexton

*One Glass Sexton (Imber Vesk), and one Stilled who wakes in the pews in round 2.* **Only if they attack, or push him past "Do you wish me to go on?"**

- **Setup:** he's in the pulpit at the front and never leaves it in the first form. The party is in the pews. Mark three pews as cover.
- **First form (the ice): 95 hit points, AC 15.** Each round he uses two Icicle Volleys (+7 to hit, range 60 feet, 3d6 + 4 piercing plus 2d6 cold), or swaps one for the Sermon (Wisdom DC 15, or Charmed with Speed 0). He aims the Sermon at whoever's loudest and the ice at whoever hit him last. Fire or Lightning drops his AC by 2. Piercing and slashing do half. Anyone who hits him in melee is showered with shards: Dexterity DC 15 or 2d6.
- **Round 2:** the Stilled in the back row wakes and attacks behind the party. Influence DC 15 calms it.
- **The break:** when the ice runs out, it shatters (Dexterity DC 15, 4d6, half on a success, within 15 feet). Take a breath here and describe the old man inside. Any leftover damage is lost.
- **Second form (the man in broken glass): 45 hit points, AC 13, Speed 30.** Two claws (+6, 2d6 + 3 slashing and 2d4 cold), or Shattering Voice (4d10 thunder, Constitution DC 15, half on a success). He's weeping now.
- **When it ends:** at 0 the shell falls away and he's frail and alive. [The Frost Heart lands on the ice](play:s13-sexton-shell-breaks): free to take if you're next to it. He says: "Have we finished?" Only a deliberate act kills him.
- **The off switch:** at the end of any round in which nobody attacked him, he stops and asks, "Have we finished?" If they say yes, the fight is over, and he can still confess if asked kindly.
- **Leaving:** he doesn't follow.
- **Scale on the fly:** three characters, 70 hit points in the first form and no Stilled. If someone drops early, leave the Stilled asleep. At level 6, a second Stilled wakes in round 2.
- **Non-lethal outcomes:** a character who drops is left where they are. He calls for somebody to help them.

- **If you need to stop early:** stop when he lifts the crown.

### If you get stuck (Session 13)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **He asks one player something small.** "What do you eat for breakfast, now?"
2. **The glass drips.** One drop, on somebody's hand. It's warm.
3. **A candle turns towards whoever hasn't spoken.**

**If they can't decide what to do with him:** go round the table. "One sentence each: what does he deserve?" Then: "And what are you going to do?"

### End of session 13

**The closing question:** "Don't ring the Frost Spire without the Heart. The winter will come back all at once." Who will carry it up? Then: "Let's each say one thing we'll remember."

**Record in Waypoint:**
- **Plays that happened:** the confession, the Key, and how Imber's story went (freed, left, beaten, killed).
- **World-state facts to check:** the Frost Heart is with the party (set this on every route); Imber is frozen, freed or dead; do they know what the Key is, and does he know who holds it.
- **Level 6, now.** Give players a few minutes if they want to level up at the table.

**Rewards:**
- **The Frost Heart.** It goes with them, whatever they did.
- **The confession:** one of the three sources for the truth of the Key, with Edda's diary and Lysse's notes.
- **The Vesk reliquary:** where it is and how it's guarded, for Act IV.
- **Level:** level 6.
- **Heroic Inspiration:** to whoever gave up a cloak, and to whoever asked him about Cassia.
