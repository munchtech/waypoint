**Silly and serious (for you, not the players).** At long-running tables people laugh every couple of minutes, and four laughs in five come from the players. The serious stretches come about five times a night, and almost all of them are conversations, not fights.
- **Let bits land.** Three jokes in four are a single laugh, and most longer bits end on their own. Laugh, answer in a few words, carry on.
- **If a bit runs on, move the world, not the players.** Each scene in Sessions 1 to 3 has a **Bring it back** line: a sound, a change of light, someone arriving. Use it once. Never tell anyone to stop being funny.
- **Protect the quiet scenes.** When the room goes quiet, slow down, say less and wait. Session 3's Scene 3 (Ila's name) is the first one.
- **When a Toll lands or a character falls,** say what happens plainly, in one or two sentences, then stop and let that player speak first. Don't describe it at length, and don't make a joke to fix the mood. Some tables laugh straight away out of nerves; some go quiet for twenty minutes. Both are fine.
- **If someone is really upset,** pause and step out of the game once: the dice did it, nobody at the table is to blame, and you can talk about what happens next. Offer a break. Then let them lead.
- **Let the players bring the light back.** After a heavy scene, don't plant a joke or a funny NPC. The players nearly always find the way back themselves; wait for them.

> **SAY:** "This game has funny nights and sad ones. Joke as much as you like. If a scene goes quiet, I'll let it be quiet, and anyone can ask for a break at any time."
