### Places at a glance (Session 2)

- **The Mere Memory Shore** (Scene 1). *Light:* dusk going to dark. *Footing:* shingle, then black, cold water that goes deep fast. *Sound:* the song; the spell Silence doesn't stop it. *Exits:* the shore path back to the Old Shore. *Cover:* the crowd of ferry-folk.
- **The Mere road** (if Ila was taken). *Light:* an hour past dawn. *Footing:* a raised causeway, a drowned causeway beside it, and a ford. *Sound:* the cart's wheels make none. *Exits:* along the causeway, or into the marsh. *Cover:* reeds, the checkpoint hut and the cart itself (Ila is inside: keep area spells off it).
- **The Pilots' Hall** (Scene 2). *Light:* morning. *Footing:* one long room hung with charts of drowned streets. *Sound:* Brannagh shouting tide-times. *Exits:* the door to the Quays. *Cover:* chart tables.
- **The sandbank** (Scene 3). *Light:* open sky over the Mere. *Footing:* wet sand about 30 feet across, deep water all round. *Sound:* nothing, except the pale rings moving on the water. *Exits:* the skiff. *Cover:* the grounded ferry; anyone sitting still in a boat is ignored for the first round.
- **Ossa's belfry** (Scene 5). *Light:* dim, but the water is glass-clear. *Footing:* none: swimming, 40 feet down, a drowned street below. *Sound:* singing without breath. *Exits:* up; going up always works, and the Singers never follow anyone above the surface. *Cover:* the belfry's arches. Breath is the clock.

### Rumours on the Old Shore (Session 2, d6)

Five are true. The one marked *false* is what frightened people say.

1. The marsh ferry never came back. A pilot swears she saw it sitting on a sandbank in the middle of the Mere, and nobody will row out to it.
2. Whatever is singing at night, don't answer it unless you know the words.
3. A priest of Kelemvor off a Baldurian ship means to bless the Mere and lay the singers to rest.
4. Something near the sandbank bites, and afterwards you can't remember your own breakfast.
5. Brannagh Wick knows why Tam Harrow went out that night, but she won't say it to a Court face.
6. The Drowned Choir takes anyone who weeps on the shore. (*False:* it hunts the forgetful, never the grieving.)
