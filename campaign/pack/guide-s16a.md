## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. A dead foreman asks a small favour, down a mine where the lightning went to die. There's a ghost who swears at them, a dark bell, and one room full of treasure that fights back. If they carry his lamp, the hardest fight of the night never happens.*

### Before you start (Session 16A)

**Level:** four characters at level 7.

**Check the world state first.** Five facts change tonight:
- **The Act IV route.** [They chose Brassfall](play:s16a-brassfall-first): mark the route as A. Vael Tor comes later, shorter, in Sessions 20 and 21. If the party really does split, use [the split card](play:s16a-split-the-party): run the road most of them walk, and give the others one decision each.
- **The lamp:** nobody's seen it yet. Tonight it becomes carried or refused.
- **Who's with them?** Imber, Yara, the Brightwater's crew, or a Stormborn ally.
- **Did anyone cross the Seam?** A character from Faerûn already knows the sound the stones make.
- **How many Hearts do they carry?** The riders will count.

**What this session is about.** An old man died down here worrying about his daughter, and he's still worrying. He can't leave; his lamp can. Everything else, the dead lightning, the bell in the dark, the vault, comes second to whether they'll do a stranger a kindness that's inconvenient.

**Your goals tonight:**
- Make Garrow funny in his first sentence and sad in his last.
- Say plainly that the lamp has a storm-stone in it, and that stones attract the Thane. They should choose with their eyes open.
- Let them see the bell. It's the reason they're here, and they can't ring it yet.
- End on the question: **"Who will you carry the lamp to?"** Then stop.

**Prepare:**
- **Tokens or minis:** the four characters; Old Garrow; three Storm-stone Golems; four riders.
- **Maps:** one tunnel that splits in three (the third mouth is cracked), and one round room with a single wide door and twenty glittering shelves.
- **Props:** a blue light. A torch with a bit of blue plastic over it will do. Hand it to whoever agrees to carry the lamp.
- **A sound:** snap your fingers, or click a pen, every few minutes. That's a stone cracking.

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **Study** is the action for working something out from what you know. The lamp: Intelligence (Arcana), DC 15.
- **Picking up a storm-stone:** a DC 13 Dexterity check. On a failure it goes off: 3d8 Lightning damage to whoever's holding it.
- **The cracked shaft:** a DC 13 Dexterity save for everyone who goes in; 3d6 Bludgeoning damage, or half on a success.
- **Utilize** is the action for using an object or doing a fiddly job. Pulling a golem's stone out is a Utilize action: Strength (Athletics), DC 15, next to it, once it's Bloodied.
- **Bloodied** means at half its Hit Points or fewer.
- **Wood is safe.** A golem only throws sparks at whoever hits it in melee with metal.
- **Influence** with the riders: Persuasion or Animal Handling, DC 15. They start Indifferent.
- **Heroic Inspiration** for a kindness to the dead that costs something.

### Opening recap (Session 16A)

> **SAY:** "You chose Brassfall. Six days around the foothills, with the thaw running in every ditch behind you. *(If anyone's travelling with them, say who, and one thing they complain about.)* On the third day the grass turned yellow. On the fifth, the hair on your arms stood up and stayed up."

- **Then hand it over:** "What metal thing do you own that you'd be sorry to lose?" One answer each. Write them down. They matter tonight, and next session.

### Scene 1: The Brass Road

**Where and why.** The last mile, at mid-morning. It's short: it shows them the place and makes them careful.

> **SAY:** "The mountains are veined with bronze. Old lightning-shafts drop into the earth like the throats of bells. Storm-stones glitter in the walls, pale blue and humming. The sky flickers above you, though there are no clouds. Somewhere below, a stone cracks like a whip."

**What's really going on.** When Thrane stopped ringing, the storms had nowhere to go. The lightning went into the ground and stayed. The whole mountain is charged, and it's been waiting four hundred years.

**Give them three small things to find, one each:**
- **A dropped pick,** standing straight up on its point. It falls over when it's touched.
- **A row of boots** outside the hut, small to large, full of dust. Forty pairs.
- **The flicker:** anyone who counts finds it's a beat. Seven, and a rest. The rest is where the thunder should be.

**If they…**
- **Drop their metal:** good. Ask where they leave it. It'll be there when they get back, and slightly warm.
- **Take the third mouth at once:** Garrow shouts from the dark before they've gone ten feet. Start Scene 2.
- **Ask about the bell:** it's down there. They can feel it in their back teeth.

**Bring it back.** Snap your fingers. A stone cracks, closer.

**How it ends.** A blue light, in the middle mouth, coming up.

### Scene 2: The lamp

**Where and why.** Just inside the middle mouth, where the roof came down.

> **SAY:** "A gray-bearded miner flickers into view in the dark of the tunnel, holding up a lamp that burns blue. He is not quite solid, and his lamplight doesn't touch the walls. He squints at you like a foreman at a late shift."

**Old Garrow.** Gruff, impatient, funny. He calls everyone lads and lasses. *Lines:* "Lads and lasses! You're late, you're loud, and you're standing where the roof came down. Move." · "Sealed it myself. Every shaft. Too well, maybe. Should've filled the lot with rock and walked away." · "My girl's out with the horse-clans. If you see her, tell her her old man's sorry. She'll know what for." · To a Court accent: "Inspectors. Counted my men like sacks of ore. Get out of my mine."

Let him fuss at them for a minute. He checks their boots. He asks who's in charge and doesn't like the answer. Then he looks at the lamp, and asks.

> **SAY (Old Garrow):** "Right, lads and lasses, you look like you can carry a lamp. My girl's in the Ironhorn foothills, near the camp, with a red horse-skull on her tent. Yenna's her name. Take the lamp to her. Tell her I'm sorry about the collapse, and tell her I'm proud of her. And don't drop it. It's the only thing down here that hums on purpose."

**What's really going on.** The lamp holds the storm-stone that ties him here. If it goes to Yenna, he can rest beside her. If they take it, he'll walk them safely through. **Tell the table straight:** there's a stone in the lamp, and the Thane collects stones.

**If they…**
- **[Examine it first:](play:s16a-examine-lamp)** Study, Arcana DC 15: a storm-stone no bigger than a thumb, and its dead lightning is tied to him. It sparks near metal. Detect Magic shows abjuration and necromancy. **Dispel Magic on it frees him at once.** He looks surprised, says "Oh. That's lighter," and he's gone. There's no help in the mine tonight, and they've still got a message to give.
- **[Say yes:](play:s16a-carry-lamp)** hand that player the blue light. Garrow hides his face for a moment and then complains about how they're holding it.
- **[Say no:](play:s16a-refuse-lamp)** he isn't angry. He's tired. He still warns them about the third shaft, because he'd never not.

> **SAY (if they refuse):** "Garrow's lamp dims. 'Suit yourselves. Mind the third shaft, then. It's cracked. I'd know.'"

- **Ask how he died:** the roof. Forty men. He got thirty-nine out and went back for the last pair of boots.
- **Ask what he's sorry for:** "She wanted to come down with me that morning. I said no, and I said it badly." He won't say what the words were.
- **Ask about Thrane:** "Aye, there's a bell. Biggest you'll ever see. We dug round it. You don't dig *through* a thing like that."
- **Ask about Kessa:** "Big lad. Laughs. He came down himself once and stood in front of it. I didn't like how he listened."

**Bring it back.** He taps the roof with the lamp, frowns, and moves everyone two steps to the left. Where they were standing, some dust comes down.

**How it ends.** The lamp's in somebody's hand, or it's back in the dark. Either way, they go down.

### Scene 3: Down the Old Works

**Where and why.** The galleries between the mouth and the Shafts. With Garrow it's a guided tour. Without him it's a choice of tunnels.

> **SAY:** "The tunnel opens, and there isn't any floor. It's a shaft as wide as a market square, dropping out of the light, and in the middle of it, on chains as thick as trees, hangs a bell. It's dark bronze, and it's beaded with wet, like a thing that's just come in from the rain. There's nothing inside it."

**What's really going on.** That's Thrane. Its Heart is in Kessa's Heart-Hall, at the camp. Until they've got it, all they can do is look.

**Go round the table:** "It's right there. What does your character do?" Let someone touch it. It's warm, and their fillings hurt.

**If they…**
- **Carry the lamp:** Garrow walks ahead. Step where he steps, don't touch the blue seams. No rolls. He points out the good work: "Timbered that myself. Four hundred feet, not a nail in it."
- **Haven't got the lamp:** there are three galleries. The first is long and safe, an hour of crawling. The second is flooded to the knee, and hums. The third is short and visibly cracked: DC 13 Dexterity save, 3d6 Bludgeoning or half. They can see the cracks before they go in. Nobody's tricked.
- **[Notice which way the stones lean:](play:s16a-stormwall-clue)** every stone hums a bit louder when it's turned west, toward the sea. Study, Arcana or Nature DC 15, or automatic for anyone who crossed the Seam: it's the sound of the Stormwall. Garrow: "Always leaned west, since before my time." **Let them guess what'll happen out there when Thrane rings.** Don't confirm it.
- **Try to ring it without a Heart:** it sounds like a fist on a door. Every stone in the wall blinks once.
- **Look for the bodies:** there's one. It's under the fall in the middle gallery, with one hand stretched out to a very small pair of boots. They were Yenna's.

**Bring it back.** Garrow stops, counts them under his breath, and gets the number right. He says it again, to be sure.

**How it ends.** Past the bell there's a wide door standing open. Every metal thing they own starts to sing.

### Scene 4: The storm-stone vault

**Where and why.** The old strong-room. Nobody has to go in, and it's the only treasure in the mountain.

> **SAY:** "A treasure room of storm-stones, pale blue and humming. Every metal thing you carry sings back at them."

**What's really going on.** [The guardians.](play:s16a-storm-vault) There are three golems. One's awake, at the door, and it'll stop anyone who touches a stone. **Each stone that's lifted wakes one more, up to three.** They never leave the vault.

**If they…**
- **[Have the lamp:](play:s16a-golem-stands-aside)** the golems know it. Read this, then let them enjoy it.

> **SAY:** "The great bronze shapes turn their heads toward the blue flame, and then, one by one, they step back from the door."

- **Then they can take what they like,** carefully: a DC 13 Dexterity check for each stone, 3d8 Lightning if it's fumbled. The golems only fight if the lamp's put down or a stone is thrown. Garrow chuckles. "Don't look at me. I only dug it."
- **Don't have the lamp:** the fight's below. Let them see all three golems before anyone reaches for a shelf.
- **Want to leave it alone:** fine. There's nothing in here they need.
- **Ask what a stone's worth:** [800 gp in Pellmoor, to the right dwarf.](play:any-sell-storm-stone) Selling one is a crime, and the Court arrests people for it. Kessa wants them too, and he'll see who's carrying.

**Bring it back.** A stone rolls off a shelf by itself and stops against somebody's boot. The golem's head turns.

**How it ends.** They've got stones, or bruises, or sense. Garrow stops at the line where the daylight starts. He can't come further unless the lamp does.

> **SAY (Old Garrow):** "Go on, then. Shift's over. Mind your heads."

### Scene 5: Four riders

**Where and why.** Outside, at dusk. They could have met the patrol this morning instead, but it's better now, with a blue light in somebody's hand.

> **SAY:** "Four riders on hairy ponies cross the road ahead of you, bronze wire braided in their hair. 'State your business. And your hands, rider. Show me your hands.'"

**What's really going on.** [The patrol](play:s16a-ironhorn-patrol) isn't hostile. They've got one question: do you work for the Court? They look at hands because the Thane's told them to, and hasn't said why.

**Sorrel.** Long, dry, never gets down from his pony. *Lines:* "That's a dead man's light." · "I'm not asking for it. I'm asking where it's going." · "The Thane will want to see that. I'm telling you before he does." · "How many Hearts are you carrying? No, don't tell me. I can count."

**If they…**
- **Tell the truth about the lamp:** an escort, all the way to the camp, as guests. Brakka takes her hat off to the light.
- **Lie about the Court:** they're let go, with a rider a mile behind them all night.
- **Roll for it:** Influence, Persuasion or Animal Handling, DC 15. A good word to a pony counts as a reason.
- **Ask about Kessa:** he doesn't sleep. He laughs a lot. He'd give you the coat off his back, and then race you for it.
- **Ask about Yenna:** "Red skull, by the horse lines. She's the one who tells him when he's wrong."
- **Fight:** they ride off. The whole camp knows by morning, and the first thing the Thane says to them next session is about it.

**Bring it back.** The smallest pony walks up, sniffs the lamp, and sneezes a spark.

**How it ends.** From the ridge they can see the camp: white tents in rings, a hundred fires. Two things stand out. At the edge there's a red horse-skull on a pole, and in the middle, a long white hall that hums.

> **SAY:** "One's a daughter who doesn't know you're coming. The other's a Thane who'd give you almost anything for what's in your hand. Who will you carry the lamp to?"

- **If they refused the lamp:** ask instead, "What will you tell Yenna?"

### Running the fights (Session 16A)

#### The storm-stone vault

*One, two or three Storm-stone Golems (AC 15, 93 HP each). The fight gets bigger only when somebody gets greedy.*

- **Setup:** a round room about 50 feet across, shelves round the walls, one door 15 feet wide. The first golem's beside the door. The other two are standing in alcoves with their heads down.
- **What happens:** the awake one goes for whoever touched a stone, then for whoever's wearing the most metal. It slams twice (+7 to hit; 13 Bludgeoning plus 4 Lightning), or sends a line of lightning (30 feet long, Dexterity DC 14, 22 damage or half). Roll for that again each turn: it comes back on a 5 or 6.
- **The sparks:** once a round, if it's hit in melee with metal, everyone within 10 feet makes a Dexterity save, DC 14, or takes 4 Lightning. Wooden weapons, arrows and spells don't set it off. Tell them after the first spark.
- **Lightning heals it.** Say so the first time.
- **Who to worry about:** anyone in plate, standing in front. Two golems hitting one character can do 65 damage a round. Remind them that the door's right there.
- **The clever way out:** once a golem's Bloodied (46 HP or fewer), a Utilize action and Strength (Athletics) DC 15 pulls its stone out. It stops where it stands. That stone's theirs, and it doesn't wake another.
- **When it ends:** they're all switched off, or everybody's outside the door. They don't follow.
- **Scale on the fly:** two golems for a lighter fight. For a harder one, each round a stone on the shelves goes off near somebody (Dexterity DC 13, 2d8 Lightning or half).
- **Non-lethal:** they're machines doing a job. A golem that drops a character picks it up, carries it to the door, and puts it outside.

### If you get stuck (Session 16A)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **A stone cracks,** and Garrow swears at it by name.
2. **The lamp leans west** in somebody's hand, like a dog that's heard something.
3. **The bell drips.** One drop, a long way down.

**If nobody wants the lamp:** Garrow says, "Then just tell her. You can carry a *sentence*, can't you?" That's enough to give them a welcome next time, even if it's a cooler one.

**Names for miners** (cross one off when you use it): Bram Ott, Little Hask, Tunny, Wil Carrick and his brother Wil, Old Penn, Merry Dace, the boy Toby who carried water.
