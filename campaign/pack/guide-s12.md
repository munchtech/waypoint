## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. Tonight is one room and one voice. The Glass Sexton is the boss of this act, and he doesn't want a fight. He wants somebody to listen. Most of the session is talking, and the danger is a slow one: after a while, nobody feels like saying anything. Short on sessions? [Run Sessions 12 and 13 as one evening](play:s12-one-evening).*

### Before you start (Session 12)

**Level:** four characters at level 5.

**Check the world state first.** Four facts change tonight:
- **Is Maren awake?** If so, they have one free warning before the glass cracks.
- **Do they know Maudrin holds the Key?** It's his question, and their answer shapes the next two sessions.
- **Edda Thorne's diary:** do they have it? It's the best thing they could bring in here.
- **Isolde:** close, but she matters in Session 15. Keep her offstage.

**What this session is about.** Imber Vesk was the High Sexton of the Ninefold Church. He tuned the bells on the Quiet Night with the Tuning Key, the one the Chancellor holds now, against Edda Thorne's warning, and the world went silent. He has been sealed in his own winter for three hundred and eighty years, preaching that silence is a kindness. He half believes it. He has one question: who rules the Court now?

**Your goals tonight:**
- Make him gentle. The sermon should be *persuasive*, and kind, and wrong.
- Show the line clearly. When they argue, the glass cracks and he asks, "Do you wish me to go on?" That sentence is the warning light for the whole act.
- Patience wins. An hour of listening makes him Friendly with no roll.
- Get Hobb and his sons out of the pews.
- The confession is next session. If they're eager, you can start it tonight.
- End on: "Will you listen to him?"

**Prepare:**
- **Tokens or minis:** the four characters; the Glass Sexton; Hobb and his two sons; one Stilled in the pews; Maren or Yara at the door.
- **Maps:** a long chapel: pews of ice, an aisle, a glass pulpit at the far end. Forty feet wide, eighty long.
- **Props:**
  - *A glass and a spoon.* Tap it once, very softly, each time somebody argues.
  - *A clock.* Mark hours in the chapel: each hour is a saving throw.

**Rules you'll use tonight, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **The sermon (out of a fight):** each hour in the chapel, each listener makes a Wisdom saving throw, DC 15. On a failure they don't feel like speaking. They still can, and they act freely, but they have disadvantage on Charisma checks against the Sexton until they leave the chapel or take a Short Rest. It never takes control of a character.
- **The sermon (in a fight):** it becomes an attack. Wisdom DC 15, or Charmed with Speed 0 until the end of the character's next turn. Calm Emotions can suppress it.
- **Influence:** he starts Indifferent. An hour of patience makes him Friendly, no roll. Arguing makes him Hostile; one sincere apology (Influence DC 15) brings him back.
- **Lying to him:** Deception DC 15 against a man who buried most of the people you'd name.
- **Extreme Cold:** still running, inside and out. Hobb and his sons have no cloaks.
- **Silence, the spell:** works. Nobody inside it can hear the sermon, speak, or cast a spell with words. He notices.

**Places at a glance:**
- **The arch:** trees leaning together into a doorway. No door.
- **The chapel:** pews of ice, a floor you can see the stream through, candles with flames that don't move.
- **The pulpit:** a column of clear glass, and a man in it, in ceremonial robes. On his head is a crown with a cold blue clapper set in the brow. That's the Frost Heart.

**Rumours (d6), from Maren, Yara or the pews:**
1. "He was a good singer." (True.)
2. "He was the one who did it. The Quiet Night." (True. He'll say so himself, next session.)
3. "He can't leave the pulpit." (True, in the glass.)
4. "Nobody who sits down gets up." (Half true. They can. They stop wanting to.)
5. "If you say a dead friend's name out loud, he loses his place." (True. It breaks the sermon's hold for a minute.)
6. "He's a Vesk." (True. The same family as the Chancellor.)

### Opening recap (Session 12)

> **SAY:** "Last time, one of you looked into the glass and saw somebody smiling back, holding a key. *(Don't say more.)* Maren Talla took her first breath in four hundred and ten years. *(Or: Wyn is still sitting with her, waiting.)* You crossed the sleepers' wood. *(Say how: quietly, or not.)* Now you're on the bank of a frozen stream, and the voice is close enough to understand."

- **Then hand it over:** "Before you go in: what do you want from him?" One sentence each. Their answers tell you which scene they'll push for.

### Scene 1: The sermon in the trees

**Where and why.** The stream bank, first light. The voice they've heard for two nights.

> **SAY:** "A voice rises from the glass and echoes through the frozen trees. It is calm and patient, like a man who has spent four centuries giving the same sermon. *'Quiet is the gift of the Silence. Memory is the burden of the living. Give your burdens up. Be still. Be at rest. There is no shame in forgetting.'*"

**What's really going on.** He isn't casting a spell. The glass carries his voice, and Hieme's silence carries the glass. It works on everyone.

**If they…**
- **Ask what it feels like:** "Like being tucked in."
- **Stop their ears:** wax, cloth, a hand. It helps until somebody needs to hear something. Give them a free hour before the first save.
- **[Cast Silence:](play:s12-silence-the-sermon)** it works, and nobody inside can talk or cast with words. When they walk in with it up, he stops mid-sentence. Read:

> **SAY:** "The sound goes out of the chapel like a candle snuffed. The Sexton stops mid-sentence and tilts his head. 'Ah,' he says, with no sound at all. 'You are not listening.'"

- He's Hostile until they drop it and explain. One apology makes him Indifferent.

**Bring it back.** A bird that isn't glass, a real one, lands on a branch, opens its beak to sing, and doesn't.

**How it ends.** They go in, or they talk for a while first about who goes in.

### Scene 2: The Chapel of Winter

**Where and why.** The other side of the arch.

> **SAY:** "It's a cathedral, or the memory of one. The pews are ice. The floor is clear, and the stream runs under it without moving. Hundreds of candles are lit, and not one flame flickers. At the far end, in a pulpit made of a single piece of glass, stands a man in robes. He has a face. His eyes turn to look at you as you come in."

**What's really going on.** He doesn't attack unless the glass around him is broken or somebody argues with him. The back pews are full of Stilled, kneeling. The front pews have three living people in them who haven't spoken in days.

**Say this to your players now, in these words:** "You can talk to him, sit down, or leave. You're not in a fight. If that changes, I'll tell you first."

**If they…**
- **Sit down and listen:** go to Scene 3.
- **Look at the crown:** the Frost Heart is set in the brow, a bronze clapper furred with frost. It's frozen into the glass around his head.
- **[Try to take it:](play:s12-grab-the-tongue)** Mage Hand can't; the Heart is fused into the winter-moment. Strength (Athletics) DC 20 prises at it, cracks the glass, and earns his question. Read:

> **SAY:** "The invisible hand closes on the Heart and the crown rings, once, a high thin note. Every pane in the chapel answers."

- **[Attack him:](play:s12-attack-on-sight)** he asks, "Do you wish me to go on?" If they go on, run the Glass Sexton fight ([the short version is below](#running-the-fights-session-12); the full one is in Session 13's guide). Leaving ends it; he doesn't follow.

**Bring it back.** Without meaning to, one of them has sat down. Ask that player: "When did you do that?"

**How it ends.** He greets them.

### Scene 3: The Sexton's question

**Where and why.** The front pew.

> **SAY:** "'Oh, look. Visitors. Children of the Court, I imagine. You carry the stink of memory-ink on your hands. Sit, please. I have waited so long to say something to someone who might understand. Who rules the Court now? I have not heard its name spoken in four centuries.'"

**What's really going on.** [He wants to be asked questions](play:s12-sit-and-listen), and he has one of his own. Whoever they name matters to him more than anything else they say.

**The Sexton.** Slow, echoing, long sentences that turn corners, never raises his voice until it's too late. *Lines:* "Ah, the congregation arrives at last, late, and few, and with snow upon their shoulders." · "I had a beautiful voice. Everyone said so. That was the difficulty." · "You will forgive me. It is a long sermon. It has no end that I have found." · "Sit. You must be so tired of carrying things." · If someone tells a joke: a long pause, then, delighted, "Do you know, I had forgotten those."

**If they…**
- **[Listen for an hour:](play:s12-sit-and-listen)** he's Friendly, no roll. Everyone makes the [hourly save](play:s12-resist-sermon) (Wisdom DC 15). He tells them about the church as it was: nine choirs, a bell for every season, his daughter Cassia asleep in the vestry because the rehearsals ran late.
- **Answer "Maudrin Vesk":** he's silent a long time. Then: "Vesk. Then it stayed in the family." He won't explain until he has confessed (next session, or tonight if they've earned it).
- **[Lie:](play:s12-lie-about-the-court)** Deception DC 15. On a success he believes it and is a little sad. On a failure: "That is not how a Vesk would answer." He's still Indifferent, but the patient hour won't work until someone answers honestly.
- **Won't answer:** he answers himself. "Still a Vesk, I suppose. It always was."
- **[Argue:](play:s12-argue)** tap the glass. Read the box. Then, every time: **"Do you wish me to go on?"** They can apologise, ask a question instead, or leave. If Maren is awake, the first crack doesn't count: she says "Don't" from the doorway, and they have one free chance to stop.

> **SAY:** "The word goes up into the vaults like a stone thrown at a window. The glass of the pulpit sings, very high, and a crack runs down it like a tear."

- **[Agree with him:](play:s12-agree)** let it be real. He's delighted, and asks what they'd set down if they could. Nothing is taken. He'll trust that character first. If the others object, let the argument be between the players. Read:

> **SAY:** "'There is no shame in forgetting,' the Sexton says gently, and for a moment it is the kindest thing anyone has said to you in Corvane."

- **Mention Edda Thorne:** he goes completely still. Every candle in the chapel leans towards him. "Who told you that name?"
- **Ask why he thinks silence is kind:** "I had thirty years to watch them, after. The ones who remembered, wept. The ones who forgot, slept." He says it like someone who has tried to believe it.
- **Ask him for the Frost Heart:** "When I have said what I have to say. It is the only thing I have left to give, and I should like to be thanked for it."

**Bring it back.** He stops in the middle of a sentence. "Forgive me," he says. "Cassia used to laugh at that part."

**How it ends.** He's Friendly, or they've had his warning and stepped back.

### Scene 4: The people in the pews

**Where and why.** The third pew. Any time.

> **SAY:** "Three men sit in the third pew, shoulder to shoulder, in carters' coats with frost on the collars. The oldest has his hat on his knees. He's smiling very slightly. His eyes follow you, and that's all."

**What's really going on.** [Hobb](play:s12-lead-out) and his sons stopped on their way home to sell lamp oil, sat down to be polite, and haven't spoken in four days. They're cold and hungry and don't mind.

**If they…**
- **Take his hand:** he stands when they pull. His sons stand when he does. The walk to Yara's camp is two hours of Extreme Cold, and they have no cloaks. Their voices come back within an hour. Read:

> **SAY:** "The carter's hand is cold and very heavy, like a child's when it is asleep. He stands when you pull. His sons stand when he does."

- **Talk to him:** he'd like to answer. He opens his mouth and can't think why.
- **Say a dead friend's name aloud in the chapel:** it breaks the sermon's hold for a minute. Hobb blinks. "Merrin," he says. "My wife's name was Merrin. I hadn't thought of it all week." Then it closes over him again.
- **[Split up](play:s12-split-lure-hobb)** (some lead them out, some stay to keep the Sexton talking): cut between the two. The walkers make cold saves for the three men. The listeners make their Wisdom saves. Yara or Maren meets the walkers halfway.
- **Leave them:** Yara fetches them out the next morning and charges the party for the cloaks.

**Hobb, once he can talk.** A slow, polite man. *Lines:* "How long have I been sat there? I told Dann ten minutes." · "It's a lovely speech. You'd want to hear the end of it. There isn't one." · "Is it all right to go home? Or should we wait for the winter to finish?"

**Bring it back.** Hobb turns round in the doorway, puts his hat back on, and takes it off again to the pulpit. The Sexton bows.

**How it ends.** Three people are out, or three people are still in the pews and the party knows it.

### Scene 5: Will you listen?

**Where and why.** Evening, in the chapel or just outside it.

> **SAY:** "'I have a thing to say,' says the man in the glass. For once he doesn't sound as if he's reading it. 'I have said it to the frost every night, and the frost does not answer. I would like to say it, one time, to people. It is not short. You will not like me, afterwards.' He looks at each of you. 'Will you listen?'"

- **If they say yes now and you have an hour,** go straight on to Session 13's first scene.
- **If it's late,** stop on the question.

### Running the fights (Session 12)

#### If they attack him tonight

*The Glass Sexton, alone, plus one Stilled who wakes in round 2. The full step-by-step is in Session 13's guide.*
- **First, every time:** "Do you wish me to go on?" Wait for the answer.
- **He's in the glass:** 95 hit points, AC 15. He never leaves the pulpit. His sermon is Wisdom DC 15 or Charmed, Speed 0, until the end of the character's next turn.
- **When it ends:** if nobody attacks him for a round, he stops and asks, "Have we finished?" Leaving the chapel ends it. He never follows.
- **Counterspell** does nothing. His powers aren't spells.

#### The congregation

*Only if someone breaks glass in the back pews: two Stilled, then one more.*
- **Setup:** they wake kneeling, in pain, and lash out at whoever is nearest.
- **When it ends:** Influence DC 15, as an action, calms one. The Sexton will stop preaching and call them by name, if asked: "Sit, Ama. Sit. There." They sit.

### Pacing (Session 12)

| Time | What's happening |

| 0:10 to 0:30 | Scene 1: the sermon in the trees |
| 0:30 to 0:50 | Scene 2: the chapel |
| 0:50 to 2:20 | Scene 3: the Sexton's question |
| 2:20 to 3:00 | Scene 4: the people in the pews |
| 3:00 to 3:30 | Scene 5: will you listen? |

- **Where to break:** straight after the first crack in the glass, or after he says "Then it stayed in the family."
- **Running long:** skip Scene 1's options, and let Yara lead Hobb out.
- **Running short:** start the confession.
- **If you need to stop early:** stop on "Vesk. Then it stayed in the family."

### If you get stuck (Session 12)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **He asks one of them, by what they're wearing, what they carry.** "You, with the bow. What was the first thing you killed?"
2. **Mark an hour.** Everyone rolls.
3. **A candle goes out.** In four hundred years, none has. Everyone, including him, looks at it.

**Names in the pews** (cross one off when you use it): Ama Reed, Brother Lorn, Jessamy Coldwell, the twins Ott and Bram Pruett, a choirmaster called Vane.

### End of session 12

**The closing question:** "Will you listen to him?" Then: "Let's each say one thing we'll remember."

**Record in Waypoint:**
- **Plays that happened:** listening, arguing, what they told him about the Court, and who led Hobb out.
- **World-state facts to check:** is the Sexton Friendly, Indifferent or Hostile; does he know Maudrin holds the Key; is anyone under the sermon's pull (disadvantage on Charisma checks until a Short Rest).
- **Note** which character he trusts most. He'll speak to them first next time.

**Rewards:**
- **The Sexton's trust,** if they listened: the confession starts with no roll.
- **Hobb's family:** three more pairs of hands in Session 15, and news of the thaw carried south.
- **Heroic Inspiration:** for a true answer to "what would you set down?", and for the first apology.
