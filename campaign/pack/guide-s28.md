## Running this session (new DM guide)

*Read this once the day before. One evening, two or three: your choice. There's nothing to beat any more. You're going to visit people. Pick the ones your table loves, give each a scene, and ask a question at the end.*

### Before you start (Session 28)

**Level:** four characters at level 11. Nobody'll check.

**First, which ending was it?** It changes what you run.
- **The Concord or the Second Dawn:** all five scenes, as they're written.
- **A Remake:** Scenes 1 to 4 in a world that's a little different, then [the rebuilt one](play:s28-remake-rebuild) in Scene 5. Whatever each player named is there, whole, in every scene.
- **An Unmake:** skip to Scene 5. [The last night](play:s28-last-night) is all there is. Nothing happens years later.
- **The Empty Choir:** Scene 2, in whispers, then [the Court rules on](play:s28-court-rules) in Scene 5.
- **They waited, or the Key's with the Court and they're still fighting:** you're not here yet.

**Then check the world state for each person, before you say a word about them:** Ila, Eld, Quill, Ansel, Rook, Haldric, Maudrin, Kessa, the lamp, Corin, Ilam and Jonah, Wren and Tessa, the jars, Pellmoor, the Stormwall, the Choir and the verse.

**How to split it:**
- **One evening:** Scene 2, Scene 3, Scene 5.
- **Two:** Scenes 1 and 2; then 3 and 5.
- **Three (28, 29 and 30):** Scenes 1 and 2; then 3 and 4; then 5, by itself, with food.

**What this session is about.** Everyone they ever helped is still alive somewhere, having a Tuesday.

**Your goals:**
- Two people each session, and a closing question.
- Say what's true. Nothing unearned gets mended, and nothing earned gets forgotten.
- Give every player the last word about their own character.
- End the last one on the question: **"What will you ring next?"** Then stop.

**Prepare:**
- **The roster notes** from session zero: what each character had lost.
- **The Memory ledger.** Every Toll, every step.
- **What they said would survive.**
- **A list of eight names,** the people your table likes best. Cross them off as you visit.
- **A candle, or a lamp.** You're going to blow it out.
- **If somebody became the bell's voice:** something for that player to do. They play everybody else. Hand them Ila, or Bet, or Kessa. And now and then, they hum.

**Rules you'll use, in plain words (D&D 2024):**
- **Nobody rolls to see whether.** If you want dice, roll to see **how well.**
- **[Jars](play:s28-return-jars):** owners who open their own get their memories back, whole. Nobody can open one for somebody else.
- **Tolls don't come back.** Nor do the stairs. Not from jars, not by Wish, not in any ending.
- **[A smashed jar](play:s28-jars-destroyed)** is gone; only a Remake could have mended it.
- **The Great Toll:** nobody remembers the name. Not one person. Don't let somebody half-remember it to be nice.
- **Heroic Inspiration:** anybody who's got one, spend it now. They may add one true thing to the world.

**Rumours (d6)** (people will be telling these for years):
1. "There were nine of them." (There weren't.)

### Opening recap (Session 28)

> **SAY:** "It's quiet. You'd forgotten how much noise being frightened makes. The morning after, you slept till noon. The morning after that, somebody asked what you wanted for breakfast, and you all looked at each other, because nobody'd asked you that in a year. *(What did you say would survive? One line each.)* And then, because there was nothing left to run from, you went to see some people."

- **Then hand it over:** "What do you have for breakfast?" It's a real question. Let it take five minutes.

*Start of Session 29:* "Back a bit, to the first autumn. Nobody's in charge of anything yet." *Start of Session 30:* "No recap. You know what happened."

### Scene 1: The grey vault

**Where and why.** Session 28, a few weeks after. *(Not in an Unmake or the Empty Choir.)* Under Hallowmere. There's a queue out of the door and up the hill.

> **SAY:** "Bet Ostrow opens a small labelled jar in the grey vault, and smells bread, and finally knows why she has cried at that smell for seventy years."

*If the jars were destroyed, read instead:* "A grey vault of broken glass." Someone's swept a path through it. Bet's come anyway. She's brought rolls.

**What's really going on.** [The Tithe's being undone, one jar at a time](play:s28-return-jars), by people who are very bad at queueing. There's no magic. There's Lysse, at a desk, with a list.

**Lysse Carrow.** Ink to the elbow. *Interrupts herself.* **Wants** it all written down in the right order this time.

*Lines:*
- "Name? No, the one you had before. Oh, I'm sorry. Take your time. I'm so sorry. *Next.*"
- "I found mine filed under nine other people. I've been nine other people. It's very tiring."
- "What do I call you, in the book? You get to choose. Nobody else has had to ask."

**Give each player one jar to hand over.** You say who's in front of them; they say what they say. Pick from:
- **Jonah Bray,** in the queue by himself with [a wooden bird.](play:s28-ilam-jonah) Ilam's outside, because he didn't want to hope in front of him. If Mara was found, she's holding the jar, and it's for both of them. Jonah looks at her face. "Oh," he says. "*That's* who that is."
- **A Hushman** who doesn't know whether he's allowed. It's his wife's name.
- **Tessa Caddock,** if she's free, with her mother a step behind. Wren's sewn the sleeve back on, and isn't sure about the rest of it. Tessa takes her hand or doesn't. **Ask the table what they think she does.**
- **Tobias Quill,** if he was Tithed by his own office. Pale, polite, no idea who they are. "Forgive me. Have we met? I've a form, if so." His jar's right there. **They can give it to him.** Or not. Nobody'd know.
- **An empty shelf,** with a label in the Coster's hand: *shipped.* That's for another day.

**If they…**
- **Look for their own Tolls:** there's no jar. There never was one. Lysse is the only person who says it properly: "I can't find it. I looked twice. I'm going to leave a page."
- **Ask about [those who paid the stairs](play:s28-stairs-payers):** Ilam says nothing about a morning he cannot remember. Each ally who went down says one thing that's gone soft at the edges. Let the players answer.
- **Meet Yara Thenn:** selling tools for opening jars. She's got a glass-flower on the counter, not for sale. If somebody's kind, she opens it, and the whole room hears Teo laugh, once. Then it's a flower.
- **Want to open one that isn't theirs:** it won't.

**Bring it back.** The bread. Every bit of it, since Session 4.

**How it ends.** Bet's got the bell now, a small one. She's nine years old, and it's a baking morning. She rings it, and everybody in the queue looks up.

### Scene 2: The oldest dock

**Where and why.** Session 28. Three or four years on. Say so.

> **SAY:** "The Mere is so still the ships look painted on it, as always. On the oldest dock, a young woman in a ferry coat that finally fits her is counting the masts."

**Stop. Let one of them speak first.** She doesn't turn round. She says, "Are you here for the Heart, or for the bell?"

**What's really going on.** [It's Ila.](play:s28-ledger) She's the ferry-folk's Ringer, or near enough, and she's keeping the ledger. *If she was Tithed:* she's here, and she's counting, and doesn't know why she likes it; if they brought her jar, she's got it in her pocket. It's hers to open. *If she travelled with them:* she's been home a week, and she's bossy about it.

**Ila Harrow.** Seventeen or so. *Blunt, flat. Counts when she's nervous; she's not nervous.* **Wants** to show them something.

*Lines:*
- "You got old. I'm allowed to say that. I've been keeping track."
- "I've got a page for everybody. There's one for people who aren't anybody any more, as well. I don't know who it's for. I just thought there ought to be one."
- "Don't cry on it, it's ink."

**Mother Sallow,** dry as ever, on an upturned boat, with her pipe unlit. If Ossa rang, she reads a name out of the ledger, aloud, over the water. "Davin Sallow. Net-maker. He was tall." *She can see his face.* Then, if the verse was never sung, she sings the last line of it, with Ila, and far out something stops being sad.

**If they…**
- **[Brought Nessa,](play:s28-ila-nessa)** or told her, or Calla rang and word got about: "A woman at the ferry-dock stops, mid-step." Say nothing. Let Ila count something. She gets to four.
- **Ask for Brother Eld:** *still here:* he's on the chapel steps with the missing page, and today he reads it out, to everybody, with his own name at the bottom. *Forgiven:* he runs the school. *Fled or gone:* there's a letter, and it's only the list. *Dead:* Sallow's put him in the book. "He's a name like anyone."
- **Ask for Ansel:** he's bought a house on the coast. It's damp, and he's thrilled. He's copied the whole ledger from memory, "for insurance."
- **Ask for Quill:** he helps at the Landing House, badly, if he was a friend.
- **Go to the Memory Shore:** Session 1's stone, with one of their names on it. The date's still six years off. Sallow's scratched it out. "It remembers what hasn't happened yet," she says. "It can be *told.*"
- **Look for the Tidewarden:** he's bringing the last of them in. He ships his oar. It's stopped dripping.
- **Came here in the Empty Choir:** it's the same dock, at night, with the door shut. She's keeping the ledger under the floor. She says, "I'm not stopping." Neither's anyone.

**Bring it back.** Her first question. And then what they answered, in Session 1, word for word if you've got it.

**How it ends.** She closes the book and holds it out, in case somebody'd like to carry it for a bit. Ask: **"Who keeps the ledger now?"**

### Scene 3: The empty chair

**Where and why.** Session 29. The first autumn. Hallowmere, the top terrace. Nobody's sitting in it.

> **SAY:** "Hallowmere's terraces are full of people looking up, waiting to be told who speaks for them now."

**What's really going on.** [Somebody's got to.](play:s28-who-rules) Three people have a claim, one of them wants it, and everyone's looking at the party, because it's easier. **They don't have to choose. They do have to say something.**

**Who's here:**
- **[Captain Rook](play:s28-haldric-rook),** if she's alive. If she won the duel, half the city'd follow her. *Clipped. "We" for "I," till tonight.* "We'd want… no. *I'd* want it looked after. I don't want to be looked at." If she lost, she's on the edge of the crowd in somebody else's coat.
- **[Tristan Vesk](play:s28-tristan-chair),** if Maudrin's gone. "Tristan Vesk sits in the chair a little too carefully." He stands straight back up again. *Soft. Writes it down.* "I'm not her. I know you'd rather I was worse. I'd do the drains."
- **Thane Kessa,** if he's alive, with rain on him. *Big. "Rider."* "End the Tithe-tax on my grass and you can put a goat in it."
- **The Blanks,** if they were freed, right at the front. Nobody's asked them. If a player does, it goes very quiet, and one says, "Could it be a *bench?*"

**Maudrin.**
- **[Captured](play:s28-maudrin-trial):** "She sits in the Dawn Order's hall in plain grey." No gloves. The party speaks last. Trial, exile, or her own office's Tithe: say all three out loud and let the table choose. She'll answer anything. She doesn't ask for anything.
- **[Exiled](play:s28-maudrin-exile):** "A ship at the Pellmouth." She carries her own bag. She stops next to whoever answered her on the Tor, and says their name.
- **Dead:** Haldric's got the letters. He reads one out. It's about watching a small boy learn to fence, from across a hall.
- **The bell's voice:** nobody can remember who sat there. There's a dove-grey glove on the arm of the chair. It seems a shame to move it.

**If they…**
- **Back somebody:** it's them. Ask what the party wants in return. They get it.
- **Want it themselves:** somebody puts a cushion on it. Good luck.
- **Want nobody:** then it's the bench. It's a mess, and it works on alternate weekdays.
- **Ask for Haldric:** *turned:* at the back, plain cloak, next to Rook. *Disgraced:* outside, holding horses, and being quite good at it. He knows, or he's just found out. He asks one of them, "What's one supposed to *call* her?"
- **Ask for Isolde:** auctioning the Court's secrets from a balcony, "darling," or in a very comfortable cell.
- **Ask for Kessa's [last storm](play:s28-kessa-storm):** if the table wants it. "He rides out into the rain." His clans go with him as far as the ridge. Give it a minute's quiet. Roll the thunder yourself, on the table.
- **Ask for Yenna:** [the lamp](play:s28-yenna-lamp) hangs on a pole outside her tent, and she laughs, and it's *his* laugh. Everybody who met the old man down the Old Works knows it.

**Bring it back.** A promise. Somewhere in your notes there's one they made, and forgot. Somebody on the terrace hasn't. It's being kept, right now, without them.

**How it ends.** Ask: **"Who will rule the Ninefold Shore?"**

### Scene 4: A sail, and another

**Where and why.** Session 29, or the start of 30. *Only if Thrane rang, and it isn't an Unmake.* The Pellmouth, some years on.

> **SAY:** "From the Pellmouth you can see it: where the wall of storm stood for four hundred years there is only weather now, grey and ordinary, and beyond it a sail, and another, and another."

**What's really going on.** [The Stormwall's thinning.](play:s28-new-world) [The fleets are coming](play:s28-landing-fleets): three in a month. The Charter can't hold. Corvane's just a place, now, on a map, next to other places. Some of them are lovely.

**Brannagh Wick,** counting. *"Lad."* "Thirty-one. Thirty-two. I'm going to need a bigger string."

**Ask each player:** "What does your character make of it?" A Stormborn going home. Somebody who's never seen a wave. A Lander who's decided to stay.

**If they…**
- **Go and look:** it's just the sea.
- **Meet Master Venn:** he's got nothing left to sell; people have stopped. He's taken up clocks. His own's ticking. "Friend! Listen!"
- **Go to [Corin's workshop](play:s28-corin-epilogue),** on the way: "The workshop is quiet." He's asleep in a chair. Peal's on his feet. Or there's nobody, and all the bells on the wall ring a bit when you come in, and nobody knows who hung them.
- **Meet Imber:** he's preaching to six people and a dog in a wood that's dripping. He gets to the end. "That's all," he says, astonished. "That's the end of it."
- **Find the Librarian:** it's open on a step, in the sun. **It reads out their names.** Each of them, whole. (It leaves a space, the length of a name, if somebody became the voice.) Then it closes, and it's a book.
- **Didn't ring Thrane:** the wall's still there. Kessa, or whoever rides after him, will see to it. Skip the scene.

**Bring it back.** The boat they came in on.

**How it ends.** A ship's hailing the Pellmouth, in a language nobody here's ever heard. Somebody should answer.

### Scene 5: The last round

**Where and why.** Session 30. Find your ending:

**The Concord, or the Second Dawn.** They're all in one room, or at one fire. Ask them where. Then [the Epilogue round](#epilogue-round-session-30), just above this guide: it's four steps, and it does the work.

**A Remake.**

> **SAY:** "A city of grey stone grows gold at the edges."

[One scene each](play:s28-remake-rebuild), built round what they named. Start every one with the thing, right there, in somebody's hand. Then the Epilogue round.

**An Unmake.**

> **SAY:** "The light is soft on the water, and everyone you loved is standing in it."

[One goodbye each.](play:s28-last-night) Who to? Play the other person, very simply. Don't be clever. No years later. **The last line belongs to the table:** ask them to write it together, and have the quietest person read it. Skip the Epilogue round; read the Tolls aloud, then stop.

**The Empty Choir.**

> **SAY:** "Dawn never fully comes to Hallowmere."

[Then say what's still changed,](play:s28-court-rules) all of it, slowly. Somebody's keeping a ledger under a floor. Ask: "Where are you hiding?" Then the Epilogue round, quietly.

**If somebody became the voice:** in the Epilogue round their player goes last. Everyone else says what they're doing the first time they hear a bell, a year on. Then that player hums. It's their turn.

**Read the Tolls aloud,** in order, in their words. It's in the ledger. Pause after each. Don't hurry because it's long.

**What's really going on.** You've been sitting round a table with these people for a year. That's the thing that survived.

**If they…**
- **Can't think what to say:** "What's in your character's pockets?" That'll do it.
- **Make a joke:** wonderful. Leave it in.
- **Want to talk as themselves:** yes. That's what the end of this one's for.

**Bring it back.** The very first dice roll of the campaign. Who made it? What for?

**How it ends.** Light the lamp. Hum the lullaby once through, and leave the last note for them. Ask: **"What will you ring next?"** Let everyone answer. Blow it out. Then stop.

### Running the fights (Session 28)

**There aren't any.** Nobody's left who wants one.

#### If somebody starts one anyway

*Use a Knight (AC 18, 52 HP) or a Hushman (AC 12, 32 HP), and only until somebody sits down.*

- **Setup:** a drunk, a duel for old times' sake, a bar.
- **What happens:** one round. Everybody's level 11. It's over, and someone's buying.
- **When it ends:** when the first person laughs.
- **Non-lethal:** all of it.

### Pacing (Session 28)

*Each of the three evenings goes the same way.*

| Time | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:15 | A recap, and breakfast |
| 0:15 to 1:05 | The first person, and whoever else they want to see |
| 1:05 to 1:20 | A break, and cake |
| 1:20 to 2:10 | The second person |
| 2:10 to 2:30 | Loose ends: one line each, as fast as you like |
| 2:30 to 2:45 | The closing question |

- **Where to break:** between the two people, while everybody's still smiling.
- **Running long:** "And what happened to…?" Answer in a sentence.
- **Running short:** ask who they haven't seen. There's always somebody.
- **If you need to stop early:** ask the closing question anyway.

### If you get stuck (Session 28)

**Three quick moves:**
1. **Somebody comes in with a plate.** Who is it?
2. **A bell rings, somewhere, for no reason.** Everybody looks up, and it's all right.
3. **Ask a player, "Who'd be annoyed if we didn't visit them?"**

**If they ask for someone dead:** there's a stone, or a song, or a child with the same nose.

**If it gets too sad:** Venn's clock. Garrow's laugh. The goat.

**If two of them remember it differently:** they're both right. Write down both.

**If they want to keep playing:** there's a ship hailing the Pellmouth. There are jars across the sea. Say, "Not tonight," and grin.

**Names, for anyone new** (cross one off when you use it): Dillon, Fenwick, Hewett, Lettice, Pascoe, Ursel.

### End of session 28

**The closing questions:** Session 28, "Who keeps the ledger now?" Session 29, "Who will rule the Ninefold Shore?" Session 30, "What will you ring next?" Then, the very last time: "Let's each say one thing we'll remember."

**Record in Waypoint:**
- **Plays that happened:** every visit.
- **World-state facts to check:** the Tithe jars, Chancellor Maudrin Vesk, Thane Oru Kessa, the Stormwall, Pellmoor Landing, the Drowned Choir.
- **Who's in the chair,** or on the bench.
- **Each character's last scene,** in their player's own words. Then mark the last session played.

**Rewards:**
- **A page each,** in Ila's book.
- **Breakfast.**
- **The next one.** Whoever wants to run it, hand them this screen.
