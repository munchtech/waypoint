The places

Your characters know this much and no more.

Pellmoor Landing

Where the story begins. Corvane's only harbour for foreign ships: a busy port of quays, warehouses and foreign inns on the Mute Mere, with the old reed-roofed ferry village still standing at its edge.

Sounds like: a dozen languages arguing on the quays, and a hum under the water that some people pretend not to hear.

The Mute Mere

A vast, perfectly still lake with a drowned town beneath it. Its water remembers the dead. Spend a night on its shore and you may wake with a memory that was never yours.

Sounds like: nothing at all. Not even waves.

Hallowmere

The grey capital, on the Ninefold Shore. Terraces, archives and shuttered windows. Home of the Quiet Court and the Dawn Order.

Sounds like: footsteps, whispered business, and church bells that ring false.

Kell's Crossing

A fork in the North Road, where the old Pellmoor track comes in. The farmland around it has gone to ash.

Sounds like: wind over the fields, and a harvest-song nobody is allowed to sing.

Glassreach

A forest in the northwest where winter froze into glass. The people inside are still waiting for spring. Bring a warm cloak.

Sounds like: glass branches chiming against each other.

Brassfall

Eastern mountains of bronze, full of old lightning that no longer strikes. Miners pick their stones very carefully.

Sounds like: picks on bronze, and a crackle before a storm that never comes.

Vael Tor

A lone high hill in the middle of the land, with a hall full of sleepers. Dreams go there to walk.

Sounds like: breathing. Too many people breathing, slowly, all at once.

The Ironhorn Steppe

Northern grasslands held by the horse-clans of the Ironhorn Compact. Riders greet strangers with a wager.

Sounds like: hooves, laughter, and someone asking what you'll bet.

The Stormwall and the Seam

A ring of storm, thirty to sixty miles deep, circles the whole land at sea. For four hundred years nothing crossed it. Thirty years ago a calm lane opened at the mouth of the river Pell: the Seam. It drifts with the seasons, so every ship takes a pilot.

Sounds like: thunder that never stops, a long way off.
