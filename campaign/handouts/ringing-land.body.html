Where Corvane is

Corvane is a continent called Hesk, part of the Forgotten Realms, far out in the Trackless Sea past the edge of every Sword Coast chart.

A permanent band of storm circles it at sea, thirty to sixty miles deep. Sailors call it the Stormwall. For four hundred years no ship crossed it, and nobody on either side knows what made it. Sword Coast sailors only had stories of "the Ringing Land" beyond the storm.

There is one way through: the Seam, a lane of calm water a mile or two wide. It opens at the mouth of the river Pell, which runs inland to the Mute Mere and Pellmoor Landing.

The voyage: Sword Coast → about six weeks at sea → the Seam (about a day, with a pilot) → the river Pell (two days) → Pellmoor Landing.

Ships sail from spring to early autumn.

Pellmoor Landing

The Mute Mere is so still that the ships look painted on it. A drowned town lies just under the water, its rooftops like the backs of sleeping animals, so every ship needs a local pilot to reach the quays.

Rules of the Landing

  • Foreigners pay a 5 gp landing fee and register at the Landing House.
  • Without a writ of passage, a foreigner may go no further than the Mere road.
  • Clocks must be silent. The Landing House removes every chime.
  • The Court's grey-gloved officers, the Hushmen, walk the Old Shore every week.
  • There are no gulls on the Mere, and ferry-folk children don't play near the water.

Memory is money

In Corvane a memory can be taken out of you, sealed in a small glass vial, and spent. Gold arrived with the ships thirty years ago, and in Pellmoor most stalls take either.

How it works

  1. Drawing. A licensed Reckoner draws the memory out with a tuning fork. It takes about a minute and only ever happens with your consent.
  2. Sealing. The sealed vial is called a keep. It glows faintly and hums when you hold it. Nobody knows what's inside unless it's opened.
  3. Spending. Keeps are passed from hand to hand, still sealed, like banknotes.
  4. Opening. If you open your own keep, the memory comes back to you. If someone else opens it, they live the memory as a guest, without becoming you. It fades each time; after three strangers have opened it, it is spent.

Good to know

  • Some things can't be sold: skills, spells, languages, anything you can do. "You can sell the day you learned to swim, but never the swimming."
  • The Tithe. The Quiet Court takes its tax in memories and says it protects people in return. A Tithed memory goes into a jar in the Court's vaults. People who give away too much stop recognising their own families; they're called Blanks.
  • Gold and the Court. Corvanese must pay their Court taxes in memory; foreigners pay in gold. Paying someone's tax for them in gold is called gilding. It's illegal, and it happens every day.
  • The capital's gate. Hallowmere asks every visitor for a small "courtesy memory" at the gate, and won't take gold instead.

At our table

  • You can pay with a memory anywhere coin is accepted in Corvane, at the posted rate. A Reckoner who buys a memory for gold keeps a tenth as a fee.
  • You always choose the memory. Write one line about it on your sheet, and tell the DM.
  • Motes and keeps change nothing on your character sheet.
  • If you sell a heartkeep, rewrite one thing it held up for your character: a belief, a bond, a fear.
  • Giving a memory freely to someone who needs it may earn Heroic Inspiration.
  • Ringing a bell costs a Toll, which is different: you always hear the price first, and a Toll can never be bought back.

Three ways to arrive

Pick the one that suits your character. All three start in Pellmoor.

  • Species. Most Corvanese are human, with halfling ferry families and old dwarven mining clans. Elves are rare. Dragonborn, tieflings, orcs, goliaths, gnomes and aasimar came with the ships, and people stare (more curious than hostile).
  • Faith. Corvane's own church, the Ninefold Church, worships the Silence and calls the Faerûnian gods "the loud gods." Their priests work freely in Pellmoor. In the capital, the Court forbids foreign dawn-worship. Divine magic works normally.
  • Backgrounds. Any 2024 background works. Sailor, Merchant, Guide, Scribe, Sage, Acolyte, Wayfarer and Guard fit a port town naturally.

Some reasons to be in Pellmoor

  • Your ship sailed without you, and the next one is a month away.
  • You came to buy memories, or to sell yours.
  • You heard of a city where the sun never rises, and your god wants it to.
  • Someone you love sailed into the Stormwall and never came out.
  • You sold a memory years ago and you want it back.
  • You're ferry-folk, and a pilot you knew drowned six weeks ago.
  • You hear a hum at night that nobody else can hear.
