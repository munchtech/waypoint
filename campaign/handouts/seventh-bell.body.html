The story

Corvane is a land far out in the Trackless Sea, hidden for centuries behind a wall of storm. Seven great bells once kept its world in shape: dawn, memory, winter, harvest, storm, dreams and sleep.

Four hundred and twelve years ago, every bell fell silent in a single night, the Quiet Night. The dawn went grey. Winter froze into glass. The dead stopped resting. People learned to live without bells, and they paid for their forgetting in memories.

Thirty years ago a way through the storm opened, and ships from the Sword Coast began to arrive at Pellmoor Landing, a port on a still, drowned lake called the Mute Mere.

Now something bronze is humming under that lake, and a fourteen-year-old girl can hear it. You are the people who find out why.

Each bell kept one part of life in its proper form. The days of the week are still named for them, though nobody alive has heard one ring.

Hearts and the Toll

A bell rings only with its Heart: the heavy bronze clapper that swings inside it. Some bells still hang without theirs. The Hearts are scattered across Corvane: some hidden, some guarded, some in the hands of people who want very different things from them. You'll be looking for them.

Ringing a bell restores something that was lost. It also costs the person who rings it something of their own: the Toll.

How a ringing works

  1. You find a bell's Heart and bring it to its bell.
  2. The DM tells you the price before you ring.
  3. You choose. Pay it and the bell rings, or walk away and nothing is taken.
TollWhat it means
A memoryYou forget one memory you name. You still know it happened.
A yearYou age one year, for good.
A senseSight, hearing, taste, smell or touch, gone for a day.
A nameOne person forgets your name.
A fearOne fear is gone for a day. You may walk where you never could.
A giftSomething you value, given freely to someone in need.

Tolls add up, and you'll feel them later. A Toll can never be bought back. Some bells you'll want to ring; some you'll want to leave silent.

Magic

  • Your spells work as written. In Corvane every spell hums faintly as it's cast; locals call magic Tonework. It adds no new costs. Divine magic from the Faerûnian gods works here too.
  • Silence-stones stop magic. The Quiet Court uses grey silence-stones. Spells fail within about 10 feet of one. You always see the edge first: the light dims and your ears feel pressure.
  • Stones can be broken. A stone isn't a spell, so dispelling won't help. Smash it (AC 12, 10 hit points), carry it off, or step out of its range.
  • The gloves don't stop you. The Court's Hushmen wear silence-stone gloves that only muffle their own blows. You can cast right next to one.
  • Be discreet inland. In Pellmoor, cast as loudly as you like. Inland, Hushmen call loud magic “bell-work”, and open spellcasting in the capital draws attention like a drawn sword.
