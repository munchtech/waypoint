# What the 2024 books do, and what Waypoint takes from them

A study made on 2026-11-20 of the 2024-rules adventures and books on 5e.tools, written so it can guide the Seventh Bell campaign and Waypoint. It describes patterns and techniques in our own words; it quotes nothing.

## What was read

- **21 adventures** published from 2025 to November 2026, from level 1 one-shots to level 20:
  - Dragon Delves (ten one-shots, levels 1 to 12)
  - Heroes of the Borderlands, plus the two Borderlands Quests
  - Welcome to the Hellfire Club
  - The Lost Library of Lethchauntos
  - Fated Flight of the Recluse
  - Shadows of Sithicus
  - Red Wizards' Gambit
  - Arcana Unleashed: Deadfall
  - the two World of Warcraft adventures
- **Books:**
  - the 2024 Dungeon Master's Guide and the 2024 Player's Handbook
  - the 2025 Monster Manual
  - Ravenloft: The Horrors Within
  - the Forgotten Realms books (Adventures in Faerûn and Heroes of Faerûn)
  - Eberron: Forge of the Artificer
- **Size:** across the adventures, 734 read-aloud boxes, 507 stat blocks, 186 sidebars and 80 tables.

## How the adventures are laid out

Every adventure uses the same set of parts in the same order. The section names below are the ones that recur, with how often each one appears across the 21 adventures:

| Part | Appears | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Treasure | 175 | A short block at the end of each room or scene that has loot, with the item and its value. |
| Development / Concluding the Encounter | 68 | What changes after the scene, and where the story goes next. |
| Conclusion / Rewards / Character Advancement | 49 | How the adventure ends, what the party gets, and when they level up. |
| Adventure Background / Key Plot Points | 53 | The truth behind the story for the DM, then the three or four beats the players must hit. |
| Preparation / Getting Started / Beginning the Adventure | 81 | What to read and print first, how the party is hooked, and the opening scene. |
| Doors, Ceilings, Lighting, Secret Door | 89 | Short location "features" listed before the room-by-room text. |
| Player Assistance | 14 | Small jobs for players at the table (mapper, note-taker, initiative tracker). |
| One-Shot / In an Ongoing Campaign | 20 | How to run it alone and how to fit it into a longer campaign. |

The repeating pattern for each place is:

1. **Features** come first, as a short list: light, ceilings, doors, footing, sound and exits.
2. A **read-aloud box** follows, two to five sentences, ending on something the players can act on.
3. The **DM text** comes next: what's really there, then who is there and what they want.
4. **Treasure** and **Development** close each place.

## How the adventures tell their stories

- **The hook is personal and urgent.** A patron, a missing person or a threat arrives in the first scene, with a reason to act today.
- **Key plot points.** The DM gets the three or four beats that must happen. Everything else can be skipped or moved.
- **Truth first, for the DM.** Every scene states plainly what's really going on before any player-facing text, so the DM can improvise.
- **Mysteries that can't stall.** Essential clues are free. Rolls decide how fast the clue comes, how much more the players learn, or what it costs them.
- **Signposted danger.** The threat shows before it strikes: footsteps, a lantern, a noise. A fight usually has a way out.
- **Endings that hand off.** Every adventure ends by naming what comes next: a new hook, a recurring villain or a debt.
- **One-shots with three hooks.** The Dragon Delves each give three ways in (a patron, an object, treasure) and the same three-part shape: arrive, explore, face the dragon.

## Motifs that recur

- **Lost or stolen memory, and the past that won't stay buried.** This is strongest in the Ravenloft book, Shadows of Sithicus and The Lost Library.
- **A place with two faces:** a busy surface, and a ruin or a drowned or secret layer beneath it.
- **A bargain with a cost** that is named before it is paid.
- **Bells, songs and sound** as magic. Silence is a threat.
- **Children and the vulnerable** are protected on-screen. Harm to them happens only off-screen, and they can be rescued.

## NPC types and tropes

- **The anxious patron** who knows less than they say.
- **The local who knows the land,** with a price: a pilot, a guide or a hunter.
- **The bureaucrat villain:** polite, with a form for everything, who can be bribed.
- **The kind villain:** a good person doing harm because their faith or their orders told them to.
- **The ghost who wants to be remembered by name.**
- **The smiling merchant who wants the macguffin.**
- **The child who sees the truth first.**

The books' NPC format is a short table up front with Name, Role (a species and job, plus one twist), Stat block and Where to find them. Each NPC entry then gives:

- their starting attitude (Friendly, Indifferent or Hostile)
- what they want and what they know
- a secret
- one line of description they can be recognised by

The 2024 Monster Manual gives each common person a stat block (Commoner, Noble, Spy, Priest, Druid, Scout, Tough and so on), so a named NPC is usually "base plus a twist".

## Rules the books lean on for social and exploration play

- **Influence (PHB 2024).** The DM decides whether the creature is willing (no roll), unwilling (no roll, it refuses) or hesitant. Only hesitant needs a check. The default DC is 15 or the creature's Intelligence score, whichever is higher. A Friendly creature gives Advantage on the check and a Hostile one gives Disadvantage. After a failure, wait 24 hours before urging the same thing again.
- **Study, Search, Help and Hide** are actions with named checks. Hide is DC 15.
- **The DMG's preparation advice** splits material into what will definitely happen, what might happen and what probably won't, and spends prep time in that order. It also gives stock moves for a stalled session: an ally offers advice, the villain acts, or the DM reminds the players of a lead.
- **Something for everyone.** Each session should give each player style (acting, exploring, fighting, instigating, problem-solving, socialising, storytelling) a moment.
- **Horror (Ravenloft, and the DMG's Fear and Mental Stress rules)** fits the Tolls and memory loss:
  - build dread before showing the threat
  - give the players fewer answers, but always one next step
  - make a cost lasting

## How the Seventh Bell compares

The Act I guides already do most of this, and some of it better than the books:

- read-aloud boxes
- "What's really going on"
- NPC voice, mannerism, wants and lines
- clues that never sit behind a failed roll
- telegraphed fights with non-lethal exits
- pacing tables
- "If you get stuck" moves
- rewards at the end of each session

The gaps against the published format were:

- **No key-people table.** NPCs were described inside each scene, so there was no one place to see who matters tonight, what they start as and what they hide.
- **Starting attitudes and Influence DCs** were spread through the text, and one was off the 2024 rule: Quill in Session 3 had DC 16. The rule gives 15, because his Intelligence is 10.
- **No location features block.** Light, footing, sound, exits and cover were in the prose, not where a DM looks mid-fight.
- **No rumour table.** Canon §7 has rumours, but the guides had none to roll on.
- **No table jobs for players** in session zero.

The gaps in Waypoint were:

- Stat blocks didn't say what Influence DC to use.
- Foresight's brief listed plays but didn't say what to prepare first when time is short.

## What changed (revision 7.7 and Waypoint 1.43.0)

**The campaign:**

- **Session 1 to 3 guides:** a new **Key people tonight** section before the first scene, in the books' order:
  - a table with who they are, stat block, where they are, starting attitude, Influence DC and a secret
  - **Places at a glance**, with light, footing, sound, exits and cover for each scene's location
  - a **d6 rumour table** drawn from canon
- **Session zero** gains **Table jobs**: optional jobs a player can take, such as mapper, chronicler, Toll keeper, initiative keeper and the rules look-up.
- **Quill's Influence DC** in Session 3 now follows the 2024 rule (DC 15).

**Waypoint:**

- **Stat blocks** now show the 2024 Influence DC, worked out from the creature's Intelligence, with the attitude rule beside it.
- **Foresight's brief** gains **Prep in an hour**. It lists, in the DMG's order, the plays that will probably happen (prepare these fully), those that might (skim them), and the wildcards (know where to find them).
